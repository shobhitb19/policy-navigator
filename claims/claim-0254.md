# claim-0254

Auckland's city centre produces about US$121,000 of GDP per worker — very strong in national terms but in the bottom third of comparable international city centres, which the report attributes to insufficient clustering of high-performing sectors.

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland & TBoC (2026)](../sources/source-0048.md) — [Focus area 3: gaps and headwinds](../sources/source-0048.md#city-centre-gaps)

> "Scale not yet fully harnessed. City centre productivity is very strong in national terms but internationally sits in the bottom third of peers, suggesting it needs to achieve stronger clustering of high performing sectors to achieve wider uplift in the city and nationwide." (p. 34)
>
> Productivity, GDP per worker: Auckland US$121k, against a peer field including Zurich, Dublin, Vancouver and Toronto. (p. 33, spectrum chart)

**Qualifiers:**
- Benchmarked against a 12-city peer set using local sources; the report notes peer figures "should be seen as indicative" given cross-border comparability problems. Exchange-rate and PPP treatment is not specified.
- This is the single most important finding in the 2026 report for spatial policy: Auckland's densest, most job-rich and most specialised square kilometres — the place where agglomeration effects should be strongest — still under-perform internationally. The productivity gap is not merely a suburban or dispersal problem; it reaches into the core.
- Read with claim-0248 (peak CBD job density just below peer average) the pattern is consistent: Auckland's centre is not dense enough, nor specialised enough, to generate peer-level productivity.
- The 2030 "signal to watch" is that city centre productivity per worker exits the bottom third of international peers.
