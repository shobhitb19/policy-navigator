# claim-0176

Government programmes that improve the productivity of non-tradeable (locally-serving) businesses are likely to produce more widely distributed regional benefits than sector-specific or place-specific interventions targeted at particular industries.

**Source:** [Coleman, Maré & Zheng (2019)](../sources/source-0029.md) — [Section 5: Conclusion and policy implications](../sources/source-0029.md#section-5-conclusion)

> "Government programmes aimed at enhancing the way non-tradeable businesses improve their productivity are likely to produce the widest regional benefits." (Coleman, Maré & Zheng, 2019, Section 5)

**Qualifiers:**
- This follows from the finding that non-tradeable/widely-distributed industries are present in most cities and towns, unlike regionally specialised tradeable industries (manufacturing or primary-sector) that are concentrated in particular places.

- [claim-0175](claim-0175.md) — Structural shift to services has favoured large cities and is unlikely to be reversed by regional interventions
