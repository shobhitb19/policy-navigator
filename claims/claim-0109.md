# claim-0109

Auckland average urban travel speeds increased ~50% between the 1930s and early 1990s as cars were adopted and roads were improved (sealed urban roads rose from ~20% in 1930 to ~90% in 1970), but then declined by ~one-third by the 2010s due to rising congestion — eroding a substantial share of the accessibility gains that had facilitated mid-century housing supply.

**Source:** [Te Waihanga (2022)](../sources/source-0023.md) — [Explaining these patterns](../sources/source-0023.md#explaining-these-patterns)

> "By the late 2010s, increasing traffic congestion had eroded around one-third of previous gains in travel speeds despite significant motorway expansion." (Nunns & Dodge, 2022, p.4)
>
> "Vallyon (2013) finds that travel speeds on nine arterial roads and motorways declined by an average of around 30% between 1986 and 2012 despite significant road widening." (p.21)

**Qualifiers:**
- Travel speed estimates are reconstructed from historical sources (mode shares, road quality data, Vallyon 2013 route data); there is measurement error, particularly in pre-1950 estimates.
- The analysis focuses on Auckland; patterns in other NZ cities are not quantified but are expected to be directionally similar.
- Transport modelling for the 2021–2031 ATAP period suggests congestion delays will increase by a further 10% despite $31 billion of proposed investment.
- The accessibility loss compounds with the downzoning effect: both constrained housing supply from the same period (1970s onward).
