# claim-0185

Households and firms systematically prefer different New Zealand locations: households are drawn to sunny, dry locations near water (high Quality of Life), while firms are drawn to larger cities (high Quality of Business), producing the negative QL-QB correlation documented in claim-0178.

**Source:** [Preston, Maré, Grimes & Donovan (2018)](../sources/source-0030.md) — [Section 6: Discussion — divergent household and firm location preferences](../sources/source-0030.md#section-6-discussion)

> "Households and firms prefer different amenities and hence prefer different locations ... households appear to prefer sunny, dry locations near water, while firms appear to prefer to locate in larger cities." (Preston, Maré, Grimes & Donovan, 2018, Section 6)

**Qualifiers:**
- The authors note QB may be less reliably measured than QL, since the model uses residential rents as a proxy for commercial rents, which could attenuate or bias the estimated QB relationships.
- This divergence implies that policies optimising purely for household amenity (QL) or purely for firm amenity (QB) will tend to pull in opposite directions for most New Zealand locations.
