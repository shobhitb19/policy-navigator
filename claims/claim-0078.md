# claim-0078

New Zealand's housing stock is valued at approximately $1.6 trillion — approximately four times GDP — compared with approximately two times GDP in the 1990s, reflecting a doubling of housing wealth relative to economic output over three decades.

**Source:** [MHuD (2024)](../sources/source-0018.md) — [Section E: Capital allocation and wealth effects](../sources/source-0018.md#section-e-capital-allocation)

> "New Zealand's housing stock is valued at approximately $1.6 trillion, approximately four times GDP. This compares with a ratio of approximately two times GDP in the 1990s." (MHuD, 2024, para. 39)

**Qualifiers:**
- The $1.6 trillion figure is a stock-level estimate; the GDP multiple is the key indicator of relative resource concentration in housing.
- Rising housing wealth relative to GDP reflects both rising prices (land scarcity) and rising quantity of housing; the land component dominates.
- The mechanism linking this to lower productivity is capital allocation: household savings directed into land rather than productive capital.
- This is consistent with claim-0070: high land prices don't reduce GDP in the national accounts mechanically, but they signal and accompany resource misallocation.
