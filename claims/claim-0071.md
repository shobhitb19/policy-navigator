# claim-0071

Auckland firms have labour productivity 17.9% higher than firms in other New Zealand urban areas. Industry composition explains approximately a quarter of this gap; within industries, the Auckland premium is 13.5%.

**Source:** [Maré (2016)](../sources/source-0017.md) — [Section 4.1: Labour productivity and MFP premiums](../sources/source-0017.md#section-4-1-productivity-premium)

> "The raw difference in labour productivity between Auckland firms and those in other urban areas is 17.9%. Differences in industry composition explain about a quarter of this premium." (Maré, 2016, p. 15)

> "Within industries, labour productivity for firms employing in urban areas outside Auckland is, on average, 13.5% below that of Auckland firms." (Maré, 2016, p. 15)

**Qualifiers:**
- Labour productivity is output per worker-hour; it bundles agglomeration, composition, firm quality, and worker quality effects.
- The 13.5% figure is the composition-adjusted (within-industry) gap — not yet purged of firm or worker quality sorting.
- The preferred MFP estimate (claim-0072) further strips out firm and worker quality to isolate the agglomeration component.
