# claim-0035

Leapfrog development — new urban land parcels that are spatially disconnected from existing built-up areas — accounted for more than 40% of built-up area growth in several major African cities (Accra, Kigali, Lagos, Nairobi, Maputo) over 2000–2010, creating fragmented cities with ~40% fewer potential interactions than Asian cities of similar size.

**Source:** [Collier, Jones & Spijkerman (2018)](../sources/source-0007.md) — [Section 2/3: Leapfrog development](../sources/source-0007.md#section-3-leapfrog)

> "In several major African cities, like Accra, Kigali, Lagos, Nairobi, and Maputo, leapfrog patches account for more than 40% of the increase in built-up area that occurred over the period 2000 to 2010." (Collier et al., 2018, p. 14; citing Lall et al., 2017)

> "After controlling for total urban population and GDP per capita, urban residents in Africa have about 40% fewer (potential) interactions than urban residents in Asia and Latin America." (Collier et al., 2018, p. 13; citing Henderson and Nigmatulina, 2016)

**Qualifiers:**
- The leapfrog statistic is from Lall et al. (2017) based on satellite imagery; Collier et al. cite it rather than derive it directly.
- The 40% fewer interactions figure is from Henderson and Nigmatulina (2016) and uses the DRP connectivity measure, not a direct count of actual interactions.
- Both statistics are averages for a sample of major cities; there is city-level variation within Africa.
- Leapfrog development is partly driven by weak land markets and lack of effective planning enforcement — causes, not just a description of form.
