# claim-0159

An expansionary immigration shock reduces real wage levels by around 0.18% on impact, remaining significantly negative for at least four months, although the long-term effect is likely to turn positive.

**Source:** [Javed, Power & Richardson (2026)](../sources/source-0028.md) — [Section 4.1: Baseline IRFs — unemployment, real GDP, participation rate, real wages](../sources/source-0028.md#section-4-1-unemployment-gdp-participation-wages)

> "...a negative impact response of real wage levels of around 0.18%... real wages remain significantly negative for at least the first four months. However, in the long-term, the effect on real wages is likely to be positive, as the major mass of probability intervals lies in positive territory." (Javed, Power & Richardson, 2026, Section 4.1, p. 13)

**Qualifiers:**
- By construction of the model's sign-restriction identification scheme, an expansionary immigration shock is defined to produce a negative co-movement between real GDP and real wages, so the short-run result is partly built into how the shock is identified.
- The long-term positive effect is described in terms of where the probability mass of the credible interval lies, not as a separately confirmed statistically significant result.
