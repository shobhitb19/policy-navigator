# claim-0051

A 10% increase in effective employment density is associated with a 0.69% increase in firm productivity in New Zealand, using a within-local-industry specification that controls for the sorting of productive firms into dense areas.

**Source:** [Maré & Graham (2009)](../sources/source-0011.md) — [Section 5: Results](../sources/source-0011.md#section-5-pooled-results)

> "A 10 percent increase in effective employment density is associated with productivity that is 0.69 percent higher for firms in that area... This is our preferred estimate as it controls for industry composition and the potential for highly productive industries to sort into dense areas." (Maré & Graham, 2009, Motu WP 09-06, p. 16)

**Qualifiers:**
- Uses the NZ Prototype Longitudinal Business Database (1999–2006); results apply to the measured sector only.
- Effective density is distance-weighted employment within a region, using a decay parameter of α=1.
- "Within local industry" means controlling for both the industry composition of the region and the region's fixed characteristics — the estimate is conservative relative to pooled OLS.
