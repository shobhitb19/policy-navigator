# claim-0155

New Zealand's immigrant share of the total population rose from 15% in 1990 to 27% in 2019, a higher share than most OECD comparator countries, exceeded only by Australia.

**Source:** [Javed, Power & Richardson (2026)](../sources/source-0028.md) — [Section 1: Introduction — NZ immigrant share of population](../sources/source-0028.md#section-1-introduction-immigrant-share)

> "This question is an important consideration for policymakers in New Zealand given the increasing share of immigrants as a percentage of the total population, rising from 15% in 1990 to 27% in 2019. This proportion exceeds that of most OECD countries, including Canada (21%), the United States (14%), Sweden (16%), Germany (14%), Norway (14%), the United Kingdom (13%), and France (12%). Among comparable economies, only Australia (29%) has a higher immigrant share." (Javed, Power & Richardson, 2026, Section 1, p. 1)

**Qualifiers:**
- Figures are stock measures (immigrant share of population), not flow measures of annual net migration.
- The comparator list is illustrative, not an exhaustive ranking of all OECD members.
