# claim-0172

A negative employment shock to a city's primary sector is amplified rather than offset by the rest of the local economy: a forecast loss of 100 primary-sector jobs is associated with an actual total loss of 108 jobs (63 in the primary sector itself, plus a further 45 in other sectors), with no compensating expansion elsewhere.

**Source:** [Coleman, Maré & Zheng (2019)](../sources/source-0029.md) — [Section 3.2: Bartik shock results — primary sector](../sources/source-0029.md#section-3-2-primary-sector-shock)

> Own-shock response for the primary sector is β = 0.63 (95% CI 0.4–0.8): "if a city is forecast to lose 100 jobs due to a primary sector shock, our estimates suggest it loses 108 jobs of which 63 are in the primary sector and a further 45 in other sectors" — unlike widely-distributed manufacturing, there is no compensating expansion elsewhere in the local economy. (Coleman, Maré & Zheng, 2019, Section 3.2)

**Qualifiers:**
- This implies primary-sector-dependent towns face a multiplier effect that worsens rather than cushions negative shocks, unlike the widely-distributed manufacturing case in claim-0171.
