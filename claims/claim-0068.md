# claim-0068

Auckland firms have labour productivity 17.9% higher than firms in other New Zealand urban areas; this falls to 13.5% once industry composition is adjusted for — meaning composition explains roughly 4.4 percentage points of the raw premium, but a substantial within-industry urban effect remains.

**Source:** [Maré (2016)](../sources/source-0017.md) — [Section 4.1: Labour productivity and MFP premiums](../sources/source-0017.md#section-4-1-productivity-premium)

> "The raw difference in labour productivity between Auckland firms and those in other urban areas is 17.9%. Differences in industry composition explain about a quarter of this premium." (Maré, 2016, p. 15)

> "Within industries, labour productivity for firms employing in urban areas outside Auckland is, on average, 13.5% below that of Auckland firms." (Maré, 2016, p. 15)

**Qualifiers:**
- The 17.9% and 13.5% figures are from Maré (2016, Motu WP 16-21), which addresses spatial price variation, labour sorting, imperfect competition and firm heterogeneity.
- Composition accounts for ~4.4pp (17.9 − 13.5); the MFP premium after further adjustment is 7.9% (unadjusted for labour quality) or 2.2% (quality-adjusted) — see claim-0072.
- Consistent with claim-0065 (Auckland aggregate GDP per capita premium ~13%).
