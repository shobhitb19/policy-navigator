# claim-0165

A robustness check splitting immigrants by age finds that a "younger" (15-29) immigrant shock produces a statistically significant fall in the unemployment rate and in tradable price levels, while house prices are likely to increase.

**Source:** [Javed, Power & Richardson (2026)](../sources/source-0028.md) — [Section 5: Robustness check — younger (15-29) vs older (30-44) immigrant age groups](../sources/source-0028.md#section-5-robustness-age-groups)

> "In response to a 'younger' immigrant shock, we observe a statistically significant fall (using the 68% credible intervals) in the unemployment rate and the level of tradable prices, while house prices are likely to increase given a major mass of probability intervals lie in the positive region." (Javed, Power & Richardson, 2026, Section 5.1, pp. 19-21)

**Qualifiers:**
- The house price result is described by where probability mass lies, not as a separately confirmed statistically significant result at the 68% credible interval.
- This robustness check splits the single baseline immigration shock by migrant age band; it does not re-estimate the full five-shock identification scheme separately for each age group.
