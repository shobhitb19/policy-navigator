# claim-0240

New Zealand's national productivity frontier is even more spatially concentrated than economic activity generally: the three major cities (Greater Auckland, Greater Christchurch, Wellington) hold 48% of all firms and 63% of employment, but 52% of national-frontier firms and 73% of employment in frontier firms — with the services-sector frontier disproportionately clustered in the three major cities.

**Source:** [Zheng (2016)](../sources/source-0047.md) — [Spatial distribution of the national frontier](../sources/source-0047.md#frontier-spatial-distribution)

> "The three major cities of Greater Auckland, Greater Christchurch and Wellington account for 48% of the total firm population and 63% of total employment years in the country. Consistent with agglomeration benefits, the spatial concentration of firms at the national frontier is even more marked with 52% of frontier firms and 73% of employment in firms at the national frontier clustered in these three cities." (Zheng, 2016, pp. 4–5, Figures 2–3)

**Qualifiers:**
- LBD data, 2000–2012; frontier defined per industry as firms above the weighted 90th percentile of firm-level MFP; regions are labour market regions from Census travel-to-work data.
- Sectoral pattern: the primary-sector frontier is predominantly rural; the goods-producing frontier is urban and semi-urban; the services frontier is disproportionately in the three major cities.
- This is the precise, primary-source version of the Treasury's "most national frontier firms are located in Auckland" (claim-0233): the exact statistic is three-city, with Auckland the largest component and the Auckland-specific concentration shown in the Treasury document's Figure 24.
- The 73% employment share means frontier firms in the major cities are also larger than frontier firms elsewhere.
