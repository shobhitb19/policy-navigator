# claim-0130

Over the last 20 years New Zealand spent on average around 5.8% of GDP per year on infrastructure, placing it in the top 10% of OECD countries for infrastructure spend, while ranking in the bottom 10% of the OECD for the quality and efficiency of those investments.

**Source:** [NZ Government (2026)](../sources/source-0026.md) — [Overview of the Government Response](../sources/source-0026.md#overview-of-government-response)

> "Contrary to common perceptions, New Zealand spends a lot on infrastructure. Over the last 20 years, on average, New Zealand spent around 5.8% of GDP on infrastructure per year. That puts us in the top 10% of OECD countries for infrastructure spend. Yet, we rank in the bottom 10% for the quality and efficiency of our investments. In other words, we spend a lot, but we don't get good value for our spend." (Overview of the Government Response, p.6)

**Qualifiers:**
- The source attributes this gap to causes "spread across the investment lifecycle – from a lack of asset management and long-term plans to inefficient funding and financing, to poor project selection, to the absence of benefits realisation reviews," rather than to any single cause.
- The document separately states New Zealand ranks "fourth to last for asset management in the OECD," a related but distinct ranking referenced elsewhere in the same Overview section.
- Figures are sourced to Te Waihanga (the New Zealand Infrastructure Commission) research, not independently derived by the Government.
