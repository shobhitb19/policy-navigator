# claim-0119

The New Zealand Infrastructure Commission's Forward Guidance projects infrastructure spending rising from just over $20 billion per year currently to more than $40 billion per year by the 2050s, with around 60% of that spending needing to go towards renewing and replacing existing assets rather than new builds.

**Source:** [Te Waihanga (2026)](../sources/source-0025.md) — [Maintenance and renewals as the priority for the decade ahead](../sources/source-0025.md#maintenance-and-renewals-funding-gap)

> "Forward Guidance: We expect spending on infrastructure to go from just over $20 billion per year to more than $40 billion per year by the 2050s. Around 60% of this should go towards renewing and replacing what we already have. For sectors and areas with little demand growth, the figure could be even higher." (Te Waihanga, 2026, Section 4.2, "Prioritise adequate maintenance and renewals")

**Qualifiers:**
- This updates an earlier Forward Guidance estimate (Sense Partners 2022, as cited in NZ Infrastructure Commission 2025) of spending rising to "slightly more than $30 billion" by the 2050s — the 2026 Plan's figure of "more than $40 billion" is the Commission's current published projection and should not be conflated with the earlier $30 billion figure.
- The 60% renewals share is described as a minimum for sectors with demand growth; for sectors/areas with little demand growth, the renewals share "could be even higher."
- Figures are not stated in real/nominal terms explicitly in this passage (contrast with the $5,000-per-person figure elsewhere in the Plan, which is explicitly in 2025 NZD).
