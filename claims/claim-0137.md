# claim-0137

New Zealand's public-sector "infrastructure gap" — the value of infrastructure the country should have built but has not — was estimated at $104 billion, with a further "future infrastructure gap" of $106 billion over the next 30 years between planned and required Government spending.

**Source:** [NZ Productivity Commission (2022)](../sources/source-0027.md) — [Chapter 3, Findings 6-8: Infrastructure has not kept pace with population growth](../sources/source-0027.md#chapter-3-absorptive-capacity-infrastructure-gap)

> "However, infrastructure owned or funded by the public sector has not kept up. The Treasury's 2022 Investment Statement cited the Infrastructure Commission's recent estimate of New Zealand's infrastructure challenges. This includes an 'infrastructure gap' – the value of what New Zealand should have built but has not – of $104 billion. The Government also has a 'future infrastructure gap' of $106 billion over the next 30 years, which is the difference between what the Government is currently planning to spend and what it would need to spend to keep up with demand (The Treasury, 2022)." (NZPC, 2022, Part 3, p.37-39)

**Qualifiers:**
- Figures originate from the NZ Infrastructure Commission via Treasury's 2022 Investment Statement, not from NZPC's own primary research.
- Privately funded infrastructure (telecommunications, energy, airports) has generally kept up with demand; the gap is concentrated in publicly owned or funded infrastructure, with housing infrastructure (build-ready land, water connections) as a notable exception even within the private sphere.
- The Commission frames this gap as a disconnect between immigration policy and infrastructure investment policy, not as evidence that immigration itself caused the shortfall — stresses on absorptive capacity predate the pre-Covid migration surge.
