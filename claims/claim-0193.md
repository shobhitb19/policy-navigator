# claim-0193

Preliminary modelling finds strong positive agglomeration economies that differ by channel and skill level: in production, agglomeration benefits are strongest for high-skilled workers, while in consumption, agglomeration benefits accrue to both high- and low-skilled households.

**Source:** [Donovan (2019)](../sources/source-0031.md) — [Preliminary findings from the dynamic model](../sources/source-0031.md#slide-preliminary-findings)

> There are "strong positive agglomeration economies: In production, especially for high-skilled workers; In consumption, for both high and low skilled." (Donovan, 2019, slide deck — preliminary/unpublished findings)

**Qualifiers:**
- Explicitly labelled as a preliminary finding from ongoing, unpublished research extending the Rosen-Roback model to allow households to differ by skill level (education).
- This suggests that low-skilled workers and households may gain less from urban density through the production channel (wages) but still benefit through the consumption channel (variety and quality of local amenities), which has distributional implications for who benefits from agglomeration policy.
