# claim-0108

Auckland's 1970 District Scheme halved housing capacity from ~475,000 (under the 1961 scheme) to ~250,000 people against a 1970 population of 152,200, reducing the average floor area ratio limit from ~1.28 to ~0.80 — a 'great downzoning' not reversed until the 2016 Auckland Unitary Plan.

**Source:** [Te Waihanga (2022)](../sources/source-0023.md) — [Explaining these patterns](../sources/source-0023.md#explaining-these-patterns)

> "Central Auckland's capacity for new housing was cut in half in the early 1970s – a change that was partially reversed in the 2016 Auckland Unitary Plan." (Nunns & Dodge, 2022, p.4)
>
> "After downzonings in the 1970s and 1980s, it only had capacity to increase its population by 70%." (p.19)

**Qualifiers:**
- Population capacity estimates assume full redevelopment of all sites to maximum permitted density; realised capacity is lower.
- The 1981 District Scheme partially restored capacity (FAR ~0.96) but also tightened minimum site areas, keeping effective constraints high.
- Post-RMA plans (1991 onwards) retained the substance of the downzonings while adding plan complexity.
- International parallels: New York City reduced zoning capacity 79% in 1961; Los Angeles reduced capacity 60% between 1960 and 1980.
