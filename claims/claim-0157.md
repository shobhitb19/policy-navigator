# claim-0157

An expansionary immigration shock raises real GDP by around 0.4% at six months, with the effect remaining statistically significant for around 18 months.

**Source:** [Javed, Power & Richardson (2026)](../sources/source-0028.md) — [Section 4.1: Baseline IRFs — unemployment, real GDP, participation rate, real wages](../sources/source-0028.md#section-4-1-unemployment-gdp-participation-wages)

> "Quantitatively, we see an expansion in real GDP of around 0.4% six months after the expansionary immigration shock, while it remains significant for around 18 months." (Javed, Power & Richardson, 2026, Section 4.1, p. 13)

**Qualifiers:**
- Significance is assessed at the 68% credible interval.
- This is a level effect on real GDP, not a permanent change in the growth rate.
