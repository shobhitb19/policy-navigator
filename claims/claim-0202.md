# claim-0202

The geographic localisation of patent citations shows only modest decay with the age of the cited patent, suggesting that knowledge spillovers remain geographically bounded for considerably longer than simple diffusion models predict.

**Sources:** [Jaffe, Trajtenberg & Henderson (1993)](../sources/source-0033.md) — [Section V: Time decay](../sources/source-0033.md#section-5-time-decay)

> "Although localization does decay over time, this decay is slow; citations are still significantly more likely to come from the same geographic area as the cited patent even after many years." (Jaffe, Trajtenberg & Henderson, 1993, pp. 595–596)

**Qualifiers:**
- The decay is "modest" — some attenuation is present at 10–15 year horizons, but it is incomplete.
- Li (2014, claim-0205) confirms and extends this: using 1975–2002 data, Li finds no evidence that the cross-country localisation pattern has declined over time ("dying over time" rejected), and only limited support for "dying with age."
- The persistence of geographic concentration may reflect that tacit knowledge continues to be valuable — as public knowledge ages, newer layers of tacit knowledge build on top, maintaining proximity advantages.
- The result is consistent with the Audretsch & Feldman (2004, claim-0203) theoretical account of why proximity remains valuable even for older technologies.

**Related:**

- [claim-0205](claim-0205.md) — Li (2014): no evidence of dying-over-time or dying-with-age at international level
