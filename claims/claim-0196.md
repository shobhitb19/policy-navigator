# claim-0196

Doubling urban population density is associated with approximately 21% more patent activity, a citation-weighted average that reflects both genuine learning spillovers and the sorting of innovative workers and firms into dense locations, without decomposing the two channels.

**Source:** [Ahlfeldt and Pietrostefani (2019)](../sources/source-0032.md) — [Table 6: Recommended elasticity estimates by category](../sources/source-0032.md#table-6-recommended-elasticities)

> "The recommended elasticity estimate for patent intensity is 0.21... in line with the original OECD analysis." (Ahlfeldt and Pietrostefani, 2019, Table 6 and accompanying discussion)

**Evidence base:** [evidence-0003](../evidence/evidence-0003.md)

**Qualifiers:**
- Patent intensity is not one of the five categories the authors regard as causally interpretable; this is an association, not a causal estimate.
- The elasticity does not separate learning-spillover effects from the sorting of already-innovative workers and firms into dense cities.
- Supersedes the patent-rate figure previously bundled into claim-0043 (retired), which cited this result second-hand via Lall et al. (2021).

**Related:** Replaces (in part): [claim-0043](claim-0043.md) (retired). See also: [claim-0057](claim-0057.md).
