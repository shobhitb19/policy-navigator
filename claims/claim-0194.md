# claim-0194

The "Amenable City" is proposed as a successor policy framing to the industrial city: a city that delivers a bundle of amenities valued by both households and firms, explicitly acknowledges pricing failures and heterogeneous preferences, and accepts megatrends like technology and climate change while pursuing dynamically efficient policy rather than a fixed end-state.

**Source:** [Donovan (2019)](../sources/source-0031.md) — [The 'Amenable City' policy framing](../sources/source-0031.md#slide-amenable-city)

> "The old industrial city is dead" — the policy response should "enable agglomeration; manage congestion" and "understand what it means to be an 'amenable' city." The Amenable City "delivers a bundle of amenities that are valued by both households and firms," "acknowledges the challenges created by imperfect (or non-existent) pricing and variation in preferences," and "accepts 'mega-trends', e.g. technology and climate change, and instead focuses on policies that are dynamically efficient. That is, let's do better over time." (Donovan, 2019, slide deck)

**Qualifiers:**
- This is a normative policy framing proposed by the author, not an empirical finding; it synthesises the preceding conceptual (claims 0186-0188) and preliminary empirical (claims 0189-0193) material from this source.
- "Dynamically efficient" is presented as a process goal (continuous improvement) rather than a single optimal policy configuration to be reached once.
