# claim-0083

Capital investment and technology adoption are complementary, not separable: when a firm invests in new plant, equipment, or software, it simultaneously raises its capital-to-labour ratio and embeds newer technology vintages — treating them as separate policy silos misses this joint mechanism.

**Source:** [Winter et al. (2025)](../sources/source-0019.md) — [Section 3: Capital intensity and innovation in NZ](../sources/source-0019.md#section-3-capital-innovation-nz)

> "Capital investment is not only a supply-side input; it is also a vehicle for technology diffusion. When a firm invests in new plant, equipment, or software, it simultaneously raises its capital-to-labour ratio and embeds newer technology vintages. Treating capital investment and innovation policy as separate silos misses this complementarity." (Winter et al., 2025, AN 25/12, Section 3)

**Qualifiers:**
- This is a conceptual/analytical claim rather than a direct empirical estimate; the strength of the complementarity in NZ is not separately quantified.
- The implication for NZ policy is that investment incentives and innovation policy should be designed jointly.
