# claim-0073

New Zealand's urban agglomeration elasticity is 0.079 (unadjusted for labour quality) or 0.022 (quality-adjusted), implying a 10% increase in effective city size raises firm MFP by 0.79% or 0.22% respectively.

**Source:** [Maré (2016)](../sources/source-0017.md) — [Section 4.2: Agglomeration elasticities](../sources/source-0017.md#section-4-2-agglomeration-elasticities)

> "The estimated agglomeration elasticity is 0.079 in the preferred specification without labour quality adjustment, and 0.022 in the quality-adjusted specification." (Maré, 2016, p. 16–17)

**Qualifiers:**
- The quality-adjusted 0.022 is the most conservative and most defensible estimate for pure agglomeration.
- The global meta-analytic central estimate is ~0.04–0.05 (Donovan et al., 2021). Maré's quality-adjusted figure (0.022) is below this range; the unadjusted (0.079) is above it.
- MHuD (2024) reports Auckland's agglomeration elasticity at ~0.04 (claim-0076), citing Donovan et al. (2022) — a different study yielding a result between Maré's two bounds.
