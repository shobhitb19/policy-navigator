# claim-0087

Auckland's city centre generates NZ$33 billion in GDP — approximately 21% of Auckland's total output — and has been growing approximately 2 percentage points per year faster than the rest of New Zealand.

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland et al. (2025)](../sources/source-0020.md) — [In focus: Auckland's productivity gap](../sources/source-0020.md#in-focus-productivity)

> "Auckland's city centre economy is a relative bright spot. The central city generates NZ$33 billion in GDP = approximately 21% of Auckland's total output (up from approximately 16% in 2000), growing approximately 2 percentage points per year faster than the rest of New Zealand." (Committee for Auckland et al., 2025, State of the City 2025, In Focus: Productivity)

**Qualifiers:**
- City centre boundary definition not specified; likely corresponds to Auckland Council's defined city centre zone.
- The shift from ~16% (2000) to ~21% of Auckland output reflects both growth and likely boundary effects.
- This outperformance is consistent with agglomeration theory: density enables matching, knowledge spillovers, and specialisation.
