# claim-0161

An expansionary immigration shock is followed by significant increases in the levels of house prices and household credit, corresponding with the hump-shaped responses of immigration and economic activity; the immigration shock explains around 20% of house price variation at the three-year horizon.

**Source:** [Javed, Power & Richardson (2026)](../sources/source-0028.md) — [Section 4.1: Baseline IRFs — house prices, household credit, labour productivity](../sources/source-0028.md#section-4-1-house-prices-credit-productivity)

> "The most important results are significant increases in the levels of house prices and household credit, which unsurprisingly correspond with the hump-shaped responses of immigration and economic activity." (Javed, Power & Richardson, 2026, Section 4.1, p. 15)

**Qualifiers:**
- The paper describes this as among its "most important" results, indicating the authors treat it as the headline finding of the baseline model.
- Consistent with the NZ Productivity Commission's framing of housing infrastructure as a key component of "absorptive capacity" that limits how quickly the economy can accommodate population growth without strain.
- The unemployment, GDP, and credit/house-price responses are reported as a coherent demand-side bundle in the paper, rather than independent effects.
