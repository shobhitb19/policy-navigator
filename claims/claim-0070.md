# claim-0070

Housing and land-use constraints reduce productivity through misallocation: when housing and urban land are too scarce or too expensive in the places where productivity would otherwise be highest, workers and firms cannot sort efficiently into those locations, weakening labour allocation, agglomeration economies, human capital accumulation, and capital allocation.

**Source:** [MHuD (2024)](../sources/source-0018.md) — [Executive Summary](../sources/source-0018.md#executive-summary); [Section A: Labour Allocation](../sources/source-0018.md#section-a-labour-allocation)

> "Housing conditions affect productivity through labour allocation, agglomeration economies, human capital and capital allocation... If housing and urban land are too scarce or too expensive in the places where productivity would otherwise be highest, workers and firms cannot sort efficiently into those locations." (MHuD, 2024)

**Qualifiers:**
- The misallocation channel is the aide-memoire's key framing: housing supply constraints prevent efficient sorting of workers and firms across space, suppressing productivity through each of the four mechanisms.
- A corollary accounting point: high land prices do not mechanically "absorb" GDP — land price changes are wealth transfers, not subtractions from output. The productivity cost operates through the misallocation channel described here, not through prices themselves appearing in the national accounts.
- This does not contradict claim-0066 (restrictive land use reduced NZ GDP 0.9–1.8%); it clarifies the channel.
