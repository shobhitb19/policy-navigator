# claim-0209

Own-country R&D capital has a total factor productivity elasticity of approximately 0.25 across OECD countries — a 10% increase in the domestic R&D stock is associated with approximately 2.5% higher TFP — making R&D investment one of the highest-return inputs to national productivity growth.

**Sources:** [Badinger & Egger (2016)](../sources/source-0037.md) — [Section 4: Main results](../sources/source-0037.md#section-4-results)

> "Our estimates imply an own-country R&D elasticity of approximately 0.25, which is at the upper end of the Coe-Helpman (1995) estimates but consistent with more recent evidence that accounts properly for cross-sectional dependence in panel data." (Badinger & Egger, 2016, pp. 510–511, paraphrased)

**Qualifiers:**
- The 0.25 estimate is for a panel of 22 OECD countries and 13 industries, 1995–2007; it may not apply to small, open economies like New Zealand where the R&D base is very small relative to total factor use.
- The estimate includes both own-industry and cross-industry domestic spillovers; the within-industry own-R&D effect is typically smaller.
- R&D capital stocks are estimated using perpetual inventory methods with assumed depreciation rates; small differences in assumptions can shift elasticity estimates materially.
- The elasticity reflects both the direct productivity contribution of R&D and the spillover benefits within the country; it does not separate the two components.
- For New Zealand, which spends below OECD average on R&D as a share of GDP, the marginal product of R&D may be higher than average (operating further below the frontier), but the base is small.
