# claim-0182

Population growth has a congestion-disamenity effect on Quality of Life that has weakened over time in New Zealand cities: a 10% population increase was associated with a 0.09 SD decrease in QL during 1976-1991, a relationship that became weaker and less precisely estimated in 1996-2013.

**Source:** [Preston, Maré, Grimes & Donovan (2018)](../sources/source-0030.md) — [Table 3: QL regressions — climate, water, education, health, population](../sources/source-0030.md#table-3-ql-climate-education)

> Population growth of 10% was associated with a 0.09 SD decrease in QL in 1976-1991 (with city fixed effects), a relationship that is weaker and less precisely estimated in 1996-2013. (Preston, Maré, Grimes & Donovan, 2018, Table 3)

**Qualifiers:**
- Estimated with city fixed effects, isolating within-city changes in population from cross-city differences.
- This contrasts with the Quality of Business result in claim-0183, where population growth has a robust positive association in both periods — illustrating the household/firm tradeoff documented in claim-0178.

**Related:**
- [claim-0183](claim-0183.md) — Population growth robustly raises Quality of Business
- [claim-0178](claim-0178.md) — QL and QB are negatively correlated across New Zealand cities
