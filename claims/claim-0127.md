# claim-0127

Modelling by Infrastructure Victoria (Australia) found that the infrastructure required to service each home in a more dispersed city cost about AU$59,000 (around NZ$68,000) more than in a more compact city, with more consolidated cities found to have stronger economies and more affordable infrastructure.

**Source:** [Te Waihanga (2026)](../sources/source-0025.md) — [Upzoning around key transport corridors](../sources/source-0025.md#upzoning-transport-corridors)

> "In Australia, research from Infrastructure Victoria found more consolidated, compact cities had stronger economies and more affordable infrastructure. Their modelling suggested that the infrastructure required to service each home in a more dispersed city cost AD$59,000 (around NZD$68,000) more than in a compact one." (Te Waihanga, 2026, Section 6.1, "Commit to upzoning around key transport corridors")

**Qualifiers:**
- This is evidence from Australia (Infrastructure Victoria), cited by Te Waihanga as supportive context for New Zealand upzoning policy, not original New Zealand-specific modelling.
- The NZD conversion (~$68,000) is the Plan's own approximate currency conversion of the original AUD figure ($59,000), not an independently modelled New Zealand cost.
- "Compact" vs "dispersed" city forms are not further defined in this passage; the figure should be read as a stylised per-home servicing-cost differential rather than a precise marginal cost estimate.
