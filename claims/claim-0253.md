# claim-0253

Auckland's city centre hosts close to 160,000 jobs — among the most concentrated of its peer centres — giving it by far the thickest labour market in New Zealand, alongside well over 50,000 tertiary students (53,900).

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland & TBoC (2026)](../sources/source-0048.md) — [Focus area 3: city centre assets](../sources/source-0048.md#city-centre-assets)

> "Scale of jobs that drives agglomeration and commuting demand. Hosting close to 160,000 jobs, among the most concentrated among peer centres, it provides by far the thickest labour market in New Zealand, with the deepest choice of skills and employers. It also provides reliable anchors for commuting and transport infrastructure." (p. 32)
>
> "Home to well over 50,000 tertiary students, Auckland's city centre has one of the largest and most resilient student populations of any comparable city centre." (p. 32)

**Qualifiers:**
- The 2030 "signal to watch" targets 175,000 city centre jobs, from a stated 159,000 today (Infometrics).
- Student figure of 53,900 from the spectrum chart (p. 33), where Auckland trails Zurich and Vancouver but the row is presented as one of Auckland's stronger positions.
- Job counts are for the city centre as defined by Infometrics/Stats NZ boundaries; peer figures use local definitions (e.g. City of Melbourne CLUE 2.49 km² core) and are indicative only.
- Labour-market thickness is the mechanism through which the centre's job concentration is meant to raise productivity — but see claim-0255: a smaller share of Aucklanders can reach the centre within a convenient commute than in most comparable cities, so the effective thickness of that market is smaller than the raw job count implies.
