# claim-0170

Slowly growing and small New Zealand cities exhibit substantially higher "excess churn" (job creation and destruction beyond what net employment change requires) than fast-growing and large cities; a regression across 65 industries and 30 urban areas finds coefficients of −0.27 on log city growth and −0.017 on log city size (R² = 0.80).

**Source:** [Coleman, Maré & Zheng (2019)](../sources/source-0029.md) — [Section 2.3 / Table 2-6: Excess churn regression results](../sources/source-0029.md#section-2-3-table-2-6-excess-churn)

> "Not only do slowly growing cities have higher excess churn than other cities, but small cities have higher excess churn than large cities." Regression of 65-industry excess churn on ln(city growth) and ln(city size) yields a coefficient of −0.27 on ln(city growth) and −0.017 on ln(city size), R² = 0.80, N = 30. (Coleman, Maré & Zheng, 2019, Table 2-6)

**Qualifiers:**
- Excess churn is a measure of labour-market turnover and reallocation friction, not a direct productivity measure; high churn in declining/small cities may reflect costly reallocation of displaced workers rather than dynamism.
