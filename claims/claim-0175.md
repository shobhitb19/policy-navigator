# claim-0175

The long-run decline of manufacturing and the growth of services has structurally favoured large cities through stronger agglomeration benefits, a shift the authors characterise as unlikely to be reversed or offset by regional policy interventions.

**Source:** [Coleman, Maré & Zheng (2019)](../sources/source-0029.md) — [Section 5: Conclusion and policy implications](../sources/source-0029.md#section-5-conclusion)

> The decline of manufacturing and the growth of services has disproportionately favoured large cities through stronger agglomeration benefits, a shift the authors describe as "unlikely to be overcome by regional interventions." (Coleman, Maré & Zheng, 2019, Section 5)

**Qualifiers:**
- This is a policy conclusion drawn by the authors from the preceding empirical results (claims 0167-0174), not an additional independent empirical finding.
- Recovery speed from negative shocks still depends on industry type — rural-processing manufacturing is harder to recover from than other manufacturing (see claim-0176).
