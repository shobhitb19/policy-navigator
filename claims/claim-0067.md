# claim-0067

Auckland congestion costs approximately $0.7 billion per year (~0.5% of Auckland's GDP); dispersed urban growth compounds this by lengthening commutes, raising infrastructure costs, and forgoing the productivity benefits of compact urban form.

**Source:** [Auckland Council Chief Economist (2025)](../sources/source-0015.md) — [Warning lights](../sources/source-0015.md#warning-lights)

> "The annual economic cost of congestion is estimated at $0.7 billion per year, equivalent to almost 0.5% of Auckland's GDP... Restrictions on higher-density development in central areas push growth to the urban fringe. A more dispersed pattern of growth raises infrastructure costs, lengthens commutes, and underuses existing networks of roads, pipes, and public transport." (Blick & Stewart, 2025, p. 2, citing EY 2025)

**Qualifiers:**
- The $0.7bn congestion cost is from EY (2025) and represents direct time and vehicle operating costs; it excludes productivity losses from unreliable travel times and restricted business interactions.
- Earlier estimates put NZ road congestion costs at ~NZ$1.3 billion/year nationally (see claim-0017); the Auckland figure alone at $0.7bn implies Auckland is the primary locus.
- The dispersed growth effect is qualitative in the quarterly report; quantitative estimates of infrastructure cost premiums are available in OECD and meta-analytic literature (see claim-0199).
