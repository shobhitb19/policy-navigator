# claim-0059

New Zealand's GDP per capita fell from approximately 110% of the OECD average in 1970 to ~79% by 2010 — a decline driven entirely by lagging labour productivity, not by lower labour utilisation.

**Source:** [Conway & Meehan (2013)](../sources/source-0013.md) — [Section 5: International comparison](../sources/source-0013.md#section-5-oecd-comparison)

> "New Zealand's GDP per capita fell from 110% of the OECD average in 1970 to 79% by 2010. The gap was driven entirely by labour productivity decline, not labour utilisation." (Conway & Meehan, 2013, NZ Productivity Commission Research Paper, p. 28–30)

**Qualifiers:**
- Labour utilisation (hours worked per capita) has actually been relatively high in NZ compared to OECD peers — the problem is output per hour, not hours worked.
- The 1970 comparison captures NZ near its historical productivity peak; the relative decline is partly a convergence story for other countries.
- Updated data through 2022 confirms the trend continued (see claim-0062 from NZPC 2023).
