# claim-0076

Auckland's agglomeration elasticity is approximately 0.04 — identical to other major New Zealand cities — and Auckland is estimated to be 5–8% more productive than Hamilton, Tauranga, Wellington, and Christchurch due to size alone.

**Source:** [MHuD (2024)](../sources/source-0018.md) — [Section B: Agglomeration economies](../sources/source-0018.md#section-b-agglomeration), citing Donovan et al. (2022)

> "Auckland's agglomeration elasticity is approximately 0.04." (MHuD, 2024, Table 1)

> "Auckland is estimated to be 5–8% more productive than Hamilton, Tauranga, Wellington or Christchurch due to its size alone." (MHuD, 2024, para. 30)

> "Donovan et al. (2022) estimate the average NZ agglomeration elasticity as approximately 0.05, suggesting that doubling city population increases output per worker by about 3.5%." (MHuD, 2024, para. 28)

**Qualifiers:**
- The 0.04 elasticity (Auckland-specific) sits between Maré's (2016) quality-adjusted 0.022 and unadjusted 0.079 estimates (claim-0073).
- The national average elasticity (0.05) is consistent with the Donovan et al. (2021) meta-analytic range (claim-0001).
- MHuD (2024) notes that agglomeration benefits have reduced since the 1980s–1990s per Donovan et al. (2024).
- The "same elasticity across NZ cities" finding implies Auckland's productivity advantage is driven by scale, not a qualitatively different urban economy.
