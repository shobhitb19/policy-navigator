# claim-0029

Large cities (population >2 million) generate positive productivity spillovers to surrounding regions up to 200–300km distant; halving travel time between a large city and its hinterland region is associated with 0.2–0.4 percentage points higher annual GDP per capita growth in the hinterland.

**Source:** [OECD (2015)](../sources/source-0006.md) — [Chapter 3: Metro GDP contribution and regional spillovers](../sources/source-0006.md#chapter-3-countries)

> "The positive agglomeration effects of large metropolitan areas extend to regions within 200-300 km... halving the travel time between a metropolitan area and surrounding regions increases annual GDP per capita growth by 0.2-0.4 percentage points." (OECD, 2015, Chapter 3, p. 102)

**Qualifiers:**
- The 200–300km reach is specific to large cities (>2m population); spillover distance for smaller metros is not quantified here.
- The travel-time elasticity (halving time → +0.2–0.4pp growth) is derived from a cross-regional regression; causality is plausible but not proven by the design.
- The finding implies that transport investment linking secondary regions to large metros can generate regional growth dividends — but the analysis does not identify which transport modes or corridors are most effective.
- Benefits accrue to hinterland regions, but the distribution within those regions (whether growth is captured by residents or by in-commuters) is not specified.

**Related:** Supports: [claim-0021](claim-0021.md) (metro areas generate disproportionate output). See also: [levers/transport-investment](../levers/transport-investment.md).
