# claim-0030

OECD metropolitan areas (population ≥500,000) contributed approximately 50% of total OECD GDP growth over 2000–2010, despite covering only approximately 4% of OECD land area.

**Source:** [OECD (2015)](../sources/source-0006.md) — [Chapter 3: Metro GDP contribution and regional spillovers](../sources/source-0006.md#chapter-3-countries)

> "Metropolitan areas contributed about half of overall GDP growth in OECD countries in 2000-10." (OECD, 2015, Chapter 3, p. 97)

**Qualifiers:**
- The 50% figure is an approximation across OECD countries; the share varies by country depending on the size of the primate city and the national urbanisation level.
- This is a growth contribution figure, not a share of GDP levels; metro areas' share of level GDP is approximately 55% ([claim-0021](claim-0021.md)), but their share of incremental growth over 2000–2010 converges on ~50%.
- The 2000–2010 period includes the 2008–09 financial crisis, which differentially affected industries concentrated in large metros (finance, real estate); the growth share may differ in other decades.
- Contribution to aggregate growth depends on both productivity levels and population growth rates in metro areas — both mechanisms are at play.

**Related:** Grounds: [claim-0021](claim-0021.md) (metro areas generate ~55% of OECD GDP). See also: [claim-0029](claim-0029.md) (large cities generate regional spillovers), [claim-0022](claim-0022.md) (agglomeration productivity elasticity).
