# claim-0021

OECD metropolitan areas (population ≥500,000) cover approximately 4% of OECD land area, house roughly 50% of the OECD population, and generate approximately 55% of OECD GDP.

**Source:** [OECD (2015)](../sources/source-0006.md) — [Introduction](../sources/source-0006.md#introduction)

> "Metropolitan areas cover less than 5% of land in OECD countries, but are home to around half the population and generate the majority of economic output." (OECD, 2015, Introduction, p. 15)

**Qualifiers:**
- Figures are approximate; the exact share varies by year and by how metropolitan areas are delimited.
- Based on OECD Functional Urban Areas (FUAs) for metropolitan areas with population ≥500,000.
- Population and GDP shares are broadly consistent across OECD countries despite variation in national urbanisation levels.

**Related:** Supports: [claim-0030](claim-0030.md) — metro areas' GDP growth contribution is consistent with their disproportionate output share. See also: [claim-0022](claim-0022.md) (agglomeration elasticity), [claim-0016](claim-0016.md) (Auckland primacy as a national-level instance of this pattern).
