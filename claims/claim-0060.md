# claim-0060

New Zealand's labour productivity growth decelerated from 2.6% p.a. in the 1990s to 1.3% in the 2000s to 0.5% in 2008–2011; the slowdown was driven by declining MFP growth, not by reduced capital deepening.

**Source:** [Conway & Meehan (2013)](../sources/source-0013.md) — [Section 3: Aggregate performance](../sources/source-0013.md#section-3-aggregate-performance)

> "Labour productivity growth slowed markedly from 1990s (2.6% p.a.) to 2000s (1.3% p.a.) to 2008–2011 (0.5% p.a.), driven by MFP deceleration not capital deepening." (Conway & Meehan, 2013, p. 14–16)

**Qualifiers:**
- MFP growth was 1.9% p.a. in the 1990s (post-reform productivity surge) and fell to ~0.2% in the 2000s.
- Capital deepening was relatively stable; the collapse in MFP is consistent with exhaustion of reform-era one-off gains and a slowdown in technology diffusion.
- The global productivity slowdown post-GFC makes the NZ trend harder to separate from international factors.
