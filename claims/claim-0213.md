# claim-0213

New Zealand's firm productivity distribution is comparatively narrow and stable — the ratio of 90th- to 10th-percentile labour productivity sat at around 5 over 2003–16 — but this likely reflects the low level and slow growth of the domestic frontier rather than unusually effective technology diffusion.

**Source:** [NZPC (2021)](../sources/source-0039.md) — [Chapter 3: Width of the productivity distribution](../sources/source-0039.md#chapter-3-productivity-distribution)

> "New Zealand generally had the lowest gaps between the performance of frontier and non-frontier firms. The productivity distribution in New Zealand thus appears relatively narrow: the ratio of 90th-percentile to 10th-percentile productivity sits at around 5. … Keeping up with frontier firms whose productivity is low and growing only slowly is not such a great achievement." (NZPC, 2021, pp. 43–44, Figure 3.7, F3.3)

**Qualifiers:**
- Over 2003–16, New Zealand firms at the 10th percentile grew labour productivity at 0.6%/yr and at the 90th percentile 0.4%/yr; in the European SAEs the equivalent figures were −1.1%/yr and +1.7%/yr — frontier firms elsewhere pulled away while New Zealand's did not.
- The Commission explicitly cautions against reading the narrow distribution as evidence of effective diffusion (F3.3): it may instead mean laggard firms find a slow frontier easy to follow.
- Other studies on different data estimate New Zealand's 90:10 ratio higher — 6.3 for manufacturing, 8.1 for services on 2011 data (Papa et al., 2018) — within the range of other small advanced economies, so the "narrow distribution" finding is not fully robust across datasets.
