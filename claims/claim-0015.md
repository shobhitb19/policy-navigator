# claim-0015

Workers based in Auckland are 11.1% more productive than workers outside Auckland, per 2020 data; this differential reflects both genuine agglomeration effects and compositional sorting of higher-skilled workers into the city.

**Source:** [Gluckman et al. (2022)](../sources/source-0005.md) — [chapter 1](../sources/source-0005.md#chapter-1)

> "Workers based in Auckland are 11.1 per cent more productive than those outside Auckland." (Gluckman et al., 2022, Chapter 1, citing Infometrics, 2021)

**Qualifiers:**
- The 11.1% figure is a gross productivity differential and does not control for worker skills, sector mix, or firm size. Some portion reflects sorting rather than place-based agglomeration.
- The underlying data source is Infometrics (2021) Auckland economic profile; Koi Tū (2022) reports the figure but is not the originating study.
- The report separately notes that Auckland's agglomeration effects appear weak relative to comparably sized primate cities internationally, suggesting the gross differential understates unrealised potential.
- Auckland population was approximately 1.72 million in 2020, and the city accounts for 37.9% of New Zealand's GDP with 35.5% of the national workforce.
