# claim-0151

New Zealand's settlement policy settings ranked in the top 10 countries on the international Migrant Integration Policy Index (MIPEX) in 2019, well above the OECD average, but the Commission recommended increased settlement-support resourcing — including business mentorship, civics courses, personalised settlement plans, and realistic living-cost information — to improve migrant retention and social inclusion.

**Source:** [NZ Productivity Commission (2022)](../sources/source-0027.md) — [Chapter 6, Recommendation 22: Settlement support and migrant retention](../sources/source-0027.md#chapter-6-settlement-support)

> "New Zealand has been comparatively successfully in selecting and supporting migrants to settle. New Zealand's settlement policy settings rank in the top 10 countries examined in the international Migrant Integration Policy Index (MIPEX) and well above the OECD average... Recommendation 22: The Government should increase resources for settlement support to improve the retention of migrants and promote economic and social inclusion and acceptance of migrants in communities. Settlement initiatives could include business mentorship, civics courses, personalised settlement plans, and information on the labour market and living in New Zealand." (NZPC, 2022, Part 6, p.81-84, Figure 6.1)

**Qualifiers:**
- The Commission cautions against complacency despite the strong MIPEX ranking, citing increased post-pandemic global competition for both high-skilled and low-skilled migrants.
- Submitters identified specific settlement barriers: English language proficiency, partner/spouse employment difficulties, lack of professional networks, and information gaps about the true cost of living in New Zealand.
- International evidence cited in the report finds spousal employment strongly predicts migrant retention (e.g., in the Netherlands, the probability of a high-skilled migrant staying five years longer is 18 percentage points higher if the spouse is employed; in Norway, retention is almost twice as high for male migrants when the spouse is employed), implying settlement policy that addresses whole-family outcomes, not just the principal applicant, is more likely to improve retention.
