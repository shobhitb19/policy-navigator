# claim-0188

Congestion functions as a negative externality that disproportionately afflicts publicly-provided goods — such as transport, education, and recreation — where price signals are inefficient or non-existent, unlike privately-provided goods where prices can ration access.

**Source:** [Donovan (2019)](../sources/source-0031.md) — [Congestion as a negative externality in publicly-provided goods](../sources/source-0031.md#slide-congestion-externality)

> Congestion is described as a "negative externality; often afflicts publicly-provided goods, where price signals are inefficient or non-existent," with transport, education, and recreation given as examples. (Donovan, 2019, slide deck)

**Qualifiers:**
- This is a conceptual framing rather than a quantitative New Zealand estimate; it provides the rationale for congestion pricing and demand-management levers elsewhere in this knowledge base (e.g. transport congestion pricing).
