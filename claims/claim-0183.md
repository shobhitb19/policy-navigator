# claim-0183

Population growth robustly raises a New Zealand city's Quality of Business: a 10% increase in population is associated with a roughly 0.1 SD increase in QB, significant at the 1% level in both the 1976-1991 and 1996-2013 periods.

**Source:** [Preston, Maré, Grimes & Donovan (2018)](../sources/source-0030.md) — [Table 4: QB regressions — population, education, health](../sources/source-0030.md#table-4-qb-population-education)

> With city fixed effects: "experiencing a 10% increase in population is associated with an increase of QB by approximately 0.1 SD in both periods, with statistical significance at the 1% level" (coefficients 1.374*** and 1.067***). (Preston, Maré, Grimes & Donovan, 2018, Table 4)

**Qualifiers:**
- This relationship is consistent with conventional agglomeration economics (larger markets raise firm productivity and profitability).
- The raw (unconditional) correlation between QB and log population remains strong (approximately 0.76-0.83, weighted) across all census waves from 1976 to 2013, weakening only once large cities are excluded from the sample (Table 5).
