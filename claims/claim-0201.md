# claim-0201

National borders act as strong barriers to knowledge spillovers: patent citations are substantially more likely to remain within the country of the cited patent than the international distribution of patenting activity would predict.

**Sources:** [Jaffe, Trajtenberg & Henderson (1993)](../sources/source-0033.md) — [Section IV: Main results](../sources/source-0033.md#section-4-results); [Li (2014)](../sources/source-0035.md) — [Section 4: International results](../sources/source-0035.md#section-4-international-results)

> "Not only are domestic citations disproportionately likely to cite other domestic patents, but the citations are more likely to come from the same state, and especially from the same SMSA as the cited patent." (Jaffe, Trajtenberg & Henderson, 1993, p. 591)

**Qualifiers:**
- Both the MSA-level and national border effects are identified via the same matched-control methodology; the border effect is typically larger in magnitude than the within-country regional effect.
- Li (2014) extends and confirms the border finding using a larger dataset (39 countries, 1975–2002) and finds no evidence that the border effect has diminished over time (see claim-0205).
- The border effect could reflect not only knowledge flows per se but also institutional differences (patent law, enforcement), language barriers, and citation practices — the paper cannot fully decompose these.

**Related:**
- [claim-0200](claim-0200.md) — within-US metropolitan localisation of knowledge spillovers
- [claim-0205](claim-0205.md) — Li (2014): border effect not declining over 1975–2002
- [claim-0210](claim-0210.md) — Badinger & Egger: cross-country productivity spillovers diminish with distance
