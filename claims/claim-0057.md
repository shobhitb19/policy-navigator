# claim-0057

A 10 percentage point increase in the share of university graduates in a city is associated with a 3% productivity premium — an externality from knowledge spillovers rather than a direct effect of individual human capital.

**Source:** [Ahrend et al. (2015)](../sources/source-0012.md) — [Section 4: Human capital spillovers](../sources/source-0012.md#section-4-human-capital)

> "A 10 percentage point increase in the share of university graduates in a city is associated with a 3% increase in productivity, an externality effect rather than direct human capital impact." (Ahrend et al., 2015, SERC DP178, p. 19–20)

**Qualifiers:**
- The two-step estimation approach separates individual-level human capital returns (captured in the first stage) from city-level externalities (measured in the second stage); the 3% is the residual city-level effect.
- The externality interpretation is consistent with knowledge spillover theory but cannot fully exclude unobserved city amenities attracting high-skill workers.
