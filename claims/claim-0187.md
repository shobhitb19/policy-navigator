# claim-0187

Agglomeration economies arise from three distinct sources — Matching (a thicker local labour market improves worker-firm match quality), Sharing (co-located firms split infrastructure and input costs), and Learning (proximity facilitates knowledge spillovers) — and these economies vary heterogeneously by sector and by worker skill level.

**Source:** [Donovan (2019)](../sources/source-0031.md) — [Three sources of agglomeration economies: Matching, Sharing, Learning](../sources/source-0031.md#slide-agglomeration-sources)

> Agglomeration economies arise from three sources: Matching (a thicker labour force improves worker-firm match quality), Sharing (co-located firms share infrastructure and reduce costs), and Learning (proximity facilitates knowledge spillovers). "Agglomeration economies are relatively heterogeneous, varying by sectors and skills." (Donovan, 2019, slide deck)

**Qualifiers:**
- This is a conceptual taxonomy rather than a New Zealand-specific quantitative estimate; it is the standard Matching/Sharing/Learning decomposition used widely in the urban economics literature.
