# claim-0250

Nearly half of all greenfield foreign direct investment across the OECD goes to barely 40 subnational city-regions.

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland & TBoC (2026)](../sources/source-0048.md) — [Focus area 1: gaps and headwinds](../sources/source-0048.md#gateway-gaps)

> "…there are several areas where Auckland's gateway city calibre needs reinforcing if it is to compete in an extremely fierce environment for investment, where currently nearly half of greenfield foreign direct investment across the whole OECD go to barely 40 subnational city-regions." (The Business of Cities & Committee for Auckland, 2026, p. 22)

**Qualifiers:**
- Greenfield FDI only — new facilities and expansions, excluding mergers, acquisitions and portfolio flows, which are more dispersed.
- The report gives no underlying citation for this figure and does not name the 40 city-regions, so it should be treated as an order-of-magnitude framing rather than a precise statistic.
- This is a general statement about the international investment landscape, not a claim about Auckland, but it establishes the competitive context: investment attraction is a winner-take-most contest between a small number of metropolitan regions, which raises the stakes on Auckland's own FDI underperformance (claim-0089).
