# claim-0260

Beyond Auckland's core, areas that added gentle density gained businesses, young people and a mix of uses much faster than areas that did not — a pattern also observed in comparable international cities.

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland & TBoC (2026)](../sources/source-0048.md) — [Focus area 5: assets and advantages](../sources/source-0048.md#homes-assets)

> "Gentle density has paid off. Beyond Auckland's core, areas that added gentle density have gained businesses, young people and a mix of uses much faster than those that did not. This trend, which is also reflected in other international cities, shows Auckland can accommodate growth while creating more economically active neighbourhoods." (The Business of Cities & Committee for Auckland, 2026, p. 44)

**Qualifiers:**
- TBoC research based on the JRC Global Human Settlement Layer and WorldPop. This is an observed association between neighbourhoods that intensified and neighbourhoods that did not — not a controlled comparison, and no magnitudes are reported.
- Selection is a live concern: neighbourhoods that added density may have done so because they were already gaining businesses and young residents. The report's observation that the pattern repeats internationally is supporting evidence but not identification.
- The claim matters because it addresses the main political objection to intensification directly — that density degrades neighbourhood life. On this evidence, in Auckland, the neighbourhoods that took density got more local economic activity, not less.
- "Gentle density" is not defined numerically; in the New Zealand context it typically means terraces, duplexes and low-rise apartments replacing detached housing on existing lots.
