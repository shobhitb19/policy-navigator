# claim-0256

Only 15.8% of Auckland's jobs are located in its city centre, the centre holds 4.2 jobs per resident, and 39% of trips to it are made by public or active transport — the three measures on which Auckland ranks worst against comparable city centres.

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland & TBoC (2026)](../sources/source-0048.md) — [Focus area 3: spectrum of performance](../sources/source-0048.md#city-centre-assets)

> "Driver of whole city jobs — share of the wider city's jobs located in the centre: Auckland 15.8%" (peer field: Dublin, Glasgow, Vancouver, Munich)
>
> "Job to resident ratio — jobs per resident in the centre: Auckland 4.2 to 1" (peer field: Zurich, Prague, Dublin, Vancouver, Toronto, Melbourne)
>
> "Non car commute share — trips to the centre by public and active transport, %: Auckland 39%" (peer field: Zurich, Vancouver, Melbourne, Toronto)
>
> (The Business of Cities & Committee for Auckland, 2026, p. 33. Rows are ordered from Auckland's strongest position to its weakest.)

**Qualifiers:**
- Auckland figures from Stats NZ, Infometrics and TravelTime; peer figures are city or city-region estimates treated as indicative.
- The three readings describe one condition from three angles: Auckland's centre holds a small share of the metropolitan job market, is heavily net-importing of workers relative to its resident population, and is reached by car more than comparable centres are. Jobs are dispersed across the region, but the centre that does exist is not well served by non-car access.
- A low centre share of metropolitan jobs is not inherently bad — polycentric cities can perform well — but it is a problem where, as in Auckland, the dispersed employment is neither clustered into secondary centres served by transit nor matched to where housing is being built (claims 0258–0259).
- These are levels, not trends. The report does not give a time series for any of the three.
