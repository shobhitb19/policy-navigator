# claim-0072

Auckland firms are 7.9% more productive on total factor productivity (TFP/MFP) than comparable firms in other New Zealand urban areas in the preferred specification; this falls to 2.2% once labour quality differences are controlled for.

**Source:** [Maré (2016)](../sources/source-0017.md) — [Section 4.1: Labour productivity and MFP premiums](../sources/source-0017.md#section-4-1-productivity-premium)

> "Auckland firms are 7.9% more productive than comparable firms in other urban areas" (preferred MFP specification, without labour quality adjustment). (Maré, 2016, p. 15)

> "Auckland's MFP productivity is estimated to be 2.2% higher than that of other urban firms" (quality-adjusted labour specification). (Maré, 2016, p. 15)

**Qualifiers:**
- The 7.9% figure controls for industry composition and firm heterogeneity but not worker quality sorting.
- The 2.2% figure is the most conservative estimate — it strips out both firm and worker quality sorting and best isolates pure agglomeration from scale.
- The gap between 7.9% and 2.2% (≈5.7pp) is attributable to worker quality sorting: higher-skilled workers disproportionately locate in Auckland.
- Wellington MFP is 3.8% above Auckland (unadjusted) — see claim-0074.
