# claim-0126

The Plan recommends upzoning around key transport corridors as one of ten priorities for the decade, arguing that enabling more housing in places with existing or planned infrastructure capacity (e.g. around Auckland's City Rail Link stations) increases the number of people who benefit from and pay for that infrastructure.

**Source:** [Te Waihanga (2026)](../sources/source-0025.md) — [Upzoning around key transport corridors](../sources/source-0025.md#upzoning-transport-corridors)

> "Forward Guidance: Meeting the needs of a growing population will be the second largest driver of infrastructure investment over the next 30 years, after renewing existing assets. Enabling more housing in places with existing or planned infrastructure capacity means more people get to benefit from and pay for the services that infrastructure provides... Zoning rules have substantively constrained building new homes and therefore the number of people living near inner-suburban stations such as Kingsland and Mount Eden." (Te Waihanga, 2026, Section 6.1, "Commit to upzoning around key transport corridors")

**Qualifiers:**
- Cites a specific example: some councils such as Tauranga "lose money on growth, spending more to service it than they recover through rates and development charges" — a boundary condition on when growth pays for itself.
- The Plan notes Auckland Council is "currently progressing a plan change" to allow more homes near stations like Kingsland and Mount Eden, indicating the recommendation reflects a reform already underway rather than a wholly novel proposal.
- Key actions specify upzoning should target areas with spare capacity in water and transport networks, or where capacity can be added cost-effectively — not blanket upzoning.
