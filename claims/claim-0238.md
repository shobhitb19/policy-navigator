# claim-0238

Auckland's patenting intensity (PCT applications per million inhabitants, 2001–2015) is low relative to high-patenting city-regions such as Helsinki, Oregon and Copenhagen, sitting at about the midpoint of the set of internationally comparable city-regions.

**Source:** [MartinJenkins (2022)](../sources/source-0046.md) — [Knowledge and skills: patent intensity](../sources/source-0046.md#knowledge-innovation)

> "Comparing Auckland to internationally comparable city-regions … shows that PCT patent applications are low, relative to high patenting regions like Helsinki, Oregon, and Copenhagen (Figure 89). However, Auckland inventors are seeking patents at about the midpoint of the set of internationally comparable city-regions." (Chen, Knuckey & Jones, 2022, p. 95, OECD regional data, fractional count by inventor and priority year)

**Qualifiers:**
- Based on OECD TL2 regional data for 2001–2015; the authors note more recent WIPO data suggest Auckland businesses file a high share of New Zealand's PCT applications, and that Waikato's apparent lead over Auckland in the older data may since have reversed.
- Patent counts are an imperfect innovation measure, biased toward manufacturing and biotech and away from software and services — the sectors where Auckland's advanced industries concentrate.
- Innovation-output corroboration of the weak-knowledge-engine theme: given density's strong association with patenting internationally (claim-0196: doubling density → +21% patents), Auckland's mid-table patent intensity despite primate-city status is consistent with under-realised agglomeration.
