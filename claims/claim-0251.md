# claim-0251

Auckland holds 48% of New Zealand's stock of internationally visible, investable companies — rising to 52% among firms with 20 or more employees, 54% among those with at least US$1m raised, and 55% among those launched since 2020 — yet peer cities retain more of their growth firms, with New Zealand companies moving offshore earlier.

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland & TBoC (2026)](../sources/source-0048.md) — [Focus area 1: gateway assets and gaps](../sources/source-0048.md#gateway-assets)

> "Auckland holds 48% of the national stock, with the share rising to 52% among firms with 20 or more employees, 54% among those that have attracted at least US$1m in funding, and 55% among those launched since 2020." (p. 19)
>
> "Auckland is a gateway for companies to capitalise but often can't retain them. It leads New Zealand in the concentration of firms with growth potential, yet peer cities retain more businesses as they add staff and raise external capital. New Zealand firms appear to move offshore earlier, weakening Auckland's role as a home for high-quality growth companies." (p. 22)

**Qualifiers:**
- Source: Dealroom.co 2025. The universe is firms visible in Dealroom's database, which skews toward technology and venture-backed companies and under-counts established private firms.
- The rising gradient — 48% of all firms, 55% of the newest — is the substantive finding: Auckland's share of the national growth-firm stock is increasing with firm size, funding stage and recency of founding. Concentration is intensifying, not eroding.
- The retention gap is stated qualitatively; no quantified offshore-relocation rate is given.
- The 2030 "signal to watch" is that the Auckland share of New Zealand's investable firms stays above 55%.
