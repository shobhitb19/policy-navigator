# claim-0133

The New Zealand Government's response to Recommendation 5 (multi-year budgeting) was "Supported in-principle" rather than "Supported," on the grounds that the existing annual appropriations system already incorporates multi-year features, including multi-year appropriations available for infrastructure spending over periods of up to five years.

**Source:** [NZ Government (2026)](../sources/source-0026.md) — [Recommendation 5: Multi-year budgeting](../sources/source-0026.md#recommendation-5-multi-year-budgeting)

> "Government Response: SUPPORTED IN PRINCIPLE. The existing capital budgeting system already has aspects of multi-year budgeting. While appropriations are approved annually, funding is usually agreed for multiple years, and there are multi-year appropriations available to support infrastructure spending over a period of up to five years. Delegated decision-making by agency chief executives also allows for multi-year funding, through existing use of agency balance sheets and depreciation." (Recommendation 5, p.19)

**Qualifiers:**
- This is an example of "Supported in-principle" as defined in the document's response taxonomy: the Government agrees with the recommendation's intended outcome (more durable, multi-year funding certainty) but argues the mechanism already substantially exists, rather than adopting the Plan's specific proposed reform.
- The response cites three multi-year infrastructure plans as complementary evidence of extending planning horizons: the Health Infrastructure Plan (released April 2025, identifying a pipeline of more than $20 billion of investment over 10 years), the Long-Term Investment Plan for School Property (10-year pipeline), and the Defence Capability Plan (15-year horizon).
- The Green Party Response separately criticises a related but distinct mechanism — the Government Policy Statement (GPS) on Land Transport 2024-2027 — for allocating "significantly more funding for new capital expenditure for state highways than any other type of activity, including renewals and maintenance," which it argues sits "at odds with recommendations 1, 2, 7 and 13."
