# claim-0054

In New Zealand, firms in denser areas use 9–15% less capital and labour per unit of output but consume 12% more intermediate inputs — agglomeration raises productive efficiency but also raises input costs, partially offsetting the output gains.

**Source:** [Maré & Graham (2009)](../sources/source-0011.md) — [Section 5: Factor augmentation effects](../sources/source-0011.md#section-5-factor-augmentation)

> "Agglomeration is associated with reduced demand for capital and labour inputs (9–15% lower) but increased demand for intermediate consumption (12% higher), depending on factor demand elasticities averaging −1.2 to −1.3." (Maré & Graham, 2009, p. 21–22)

**Qualifiers:**
- These are factor augmentation effects derived from a factor demand model alongside the production function; they reflect both efficiency gains and price effects.
- The net productivity gain (claim-0051) is the output of both effects combined.
