# claim-0214

New Zealand businesses are typically capital-shallow, which has held down labour productivity; contributing factors include the high off-the-shelf cost of capital goods, past periods of high long-term real interest rates, fast population growth, and ready access to low-cost immigrant labour, which has reduced firms' incentives to invest in labour-saving, productivity-enhancing equipment.

**Source:** [NZPC (2021)](../sources/source-0039.md) — [Chapter 2: Capital shallowness](../sources/source-0039.md#chapter-2-capital-shallowness)

> "New Zealand businesses are typically capital-shallow (ie, workers have limited equipment and other capital goods to work with) and this has held down labour productivity. Historically, this has been partly explained by the high off-the-shelf cost of capital goods, past periods of high long-term real interest rates, and fast population growth. Low returns to investment, low wages and ready access to low-cost immigrant labour are also contributing factors." (NZPC, 2021, F2.3, p. 25, Figure 2.2)

**Qualifiers:**
- The Commission describes the reasons for capital shallowness as "complex" and offers these as likely contributing factors, not a decomposition with estimated shares.
- The population-growth channel operates via the ready availability of labour at modest or low wages (including through immigration settings allowing high levels of low-skill migration), which reduces incentives to invest in automation (developed further in Chapter 9 of the source).
- Capital intensity comparison is from Groningen Growth and Development Centre data (capital stock per hour worked, constant US$2011 PPP, 2002 and 2017).
