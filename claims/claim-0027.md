# claim-0027

Building regulations in central London and Manhattan raise floor-space prices to 2–8 times above construction costs.

**Source:** [OECD (2015)](../sources/source-0006.md) — [Chapter 2: Building regulations and floor-space costs](../sources/source-0006.md#chapter-2-land-use)

> "Building regulations have increased the price of floor space to between 2 and 8 times the construction cost in central London and in Manhattan." (OECD, 2015, Chapter 2, p. 65; citing Glaeser et al., 2005)

**Qualifiers:**
- Original finding from Glaeser, Gyourko, and Saks (2005) for Manhattan and Cheshire and Hilber (2008) for central London; OECD (2015) cites these as illustrative cases.
- The 2–8x range spans a wide band; the upper end reflects the most supply-constrained submarkets (e.g. Manhattan prime areas).
- Construction cost benchmarks used in the underlying studies may not fully account for all legitimate development costs (site preparation, financing, profit margins).
- These are two exceptional global cities; the regulatory premium in other cities is likely lower.
- The wedge between price and cost is interpreted as a regulatory tax on supply, not as land scarcity alone.

**Related:** See also: [claim-0025](claim-0025.md) (governance and sprawl), [levers/zoning-and-far](../levers/zoning-and-far.md).
