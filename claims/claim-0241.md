# claim-0241

New Zealand firms converge faster toward their local productivity frontier than toward the national frontier — all 15 industries show positive catch-up to the local frontier but only nine to the national frontier — indicating that knowledge diffusion within New Zealand is geographically localised.

**Source:** [Zheng (2016)](../sources/source-0047.md) — [Abstract](../sources/source-0047.md#abstract); [Convergence results](../sources/source-0047.md#convergence-results)

> "New Zealand firms have faster productivity convergence towards the local frontier compared to the national frontier, suggesting that knowledge diffusion is geographically localised to some extent." (Zheng, 2016, p. iii)

**Qualifiers:**
- LBD data 2000–2012; convergence measured as the speed at which lagging firms close the MFP gap to the relevant frontier.
- Industries with weak convergence to either frontier account for 54% of private-sector employment and 69% of gross output — the diffusion problem is concentrated in large service industries.
- This is the within-country counterpart of the international diffusion findings: NZPC (2021) found NZ non-frontier firms receive no diffusion from the international frontier (claim-0212); Zheng shows that even within New Zealand, diffusion attenuates with distance from the frontier. Distance decay operates at every scale.
- Completes a three-scale evidence chain with Jaffe et al. (1993)/Li (2014) at city scale and Badinger & Egger (2016) at country scale.
