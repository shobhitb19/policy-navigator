# claim-0181

Education's relationship with Quality of Life strengthened markedly between the two halves of New Zealand's post-1976 history: there was no relationship between a city's workforce education share and QL in 1976-1991, but by 1996-2013 a one standard deviation increase in education was associated with a 0.27 SD increase in QL (highly significant).

**Source:** [Preston, Maré, Grimes & Donovan (2018)](../sources/source-0030.md) — [Table 3: QL regressions — climate, water, education, health, population](../sources/source-0030.md#table-3-ql-climate-education)

> With city fixed effects: there is "no relationship between education and QL in the first period [1976-1991] but a positive and highly significant relationship in the second period [1996-2013]: a one SD increase in education is associated with a 0.27 SD increase in QL"; health is weakly significant (0.162*) only in the second period. (Preston, Maré, Grimes & Donovan, 2018, Table 3)

**Qualifiers:**
- The health workforce share shows a much weaker (0.162, significant only at the 10% level) relationship with QL in the same second period, and no relationship in the first.
- The authors do not establish the direction of causality — it is consistent with educated workers being drawn to higher-QL cities and with education-sector employment itself raising local QL (e.g. through associated cultural/social amenities).
