# claim-0156

In a sign-restricted SVAR of the New Zealand economy (1992q1-2019q4), an expansionary immigration shock is followed by a statistically significant fall in the unemployment rate that persists for around 18 months, with a 0.25% shock associated with a fall of over 0.20 percentage points at the 2-3 year horizon.

**Source:** [Javed, Power & Richardson (2026)](../sources/source-0028.md) — [Section 4.1: Baseline IRFs — unemployment, real GDP, participation rate, real wages](../sources/source-0028.md#section-4-1-unemployment-gdp-participation-wages)

> "We find a significant fall in the unemployment rate that lasts around 18 months after the immigration shock. To be more precise, a 0.25% expansionary immigration shock is associated with a significant fall in the unemployment rate of over 0.20 percentage points in the medium-term (i.e., 2-3 years)." (Javed, Power & Richardson, 2026, Section 4.1, p. 11)

**Qualifiers:**
- Significance is assessed at the 68% credible interval, the convention used throughout the paper's Bayesian SVAR estimates, which is a looser bar than a conventional 95% interval.
- The model identifies the immigration shock as one of five fully identified structural shocks (immigration, domestic labour supply, business cycle, technology, residual), so the result is net of these other labour-market disturbances.
- This is the opposite-signed but directionally consistent result with the broader literature this paper follows methodologically (Furlanetto and Robstad, 2019).
