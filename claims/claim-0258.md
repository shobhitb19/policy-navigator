# claim-0258

The distance between where Aucklanders live and where jobs are being created is rising: businesses are forming fastest in the inner suburbs while most new housing is going elsewhere, producing a spatial mismatch the report identifies as a key drag on medium-term productivity and liveability.

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland & TBoC (2026)](../sources/source-0048.md) — [Focus area 5: gaps and headwinds](../sources/source-0048.md#homes-gaps)

> "Jobs and people are drifting apart. The distance between where Aucklanders live and where jobs are being created is rising, creating a key drag on medium-term productivity and liveability. Businesses are being created fastest in the inner suburbs, yet most new housing is going elsewhere, creating a potential spatial mismatch that raises travel times and recruitment challenges." (The Business of Cities & Committee for Auckland, 2026, p. 46)

**Qualifiers:**
- TBoC research based on the JRC Global Human Settlement Layer, Foursquare and TEMPO. No magnitude is given for the increase in distance, and the finding is presented as a direction of travel rather than a measured elasticity.
- The mechanism runs in both directions: longer job-access distances thin the effective labour market available to each firm (a matching loss) and raise commuting costs for households (an accessibility loss). The report names both — "raises travel times and recruitment challenges."
- This is the central spatial-policy finding of the 2026 edition. It says that Auckland's housing supply success (claim-0261) and its business formation success are happening in *different places*, so the two do not compound.
- "Inner suburbs" is not defined precisely; the report elsewhere notes that relative place quality across the inner suburbs has lifted over the past five years.
