# claim-0162

Despite New Zealand's high-skilled immigration composition, labour productivity shows a muted, statistically insignificant response to an expansionary immigration shock, consistent with the New Zealand Productivity Commission's conclusion that immigration is neither the cause of, nor the solution to, NZ's productivity challenges.

**Source:** [Javed, Power & Richardson (2026)](../sources/source-0028.md) — [Section 4.1: Baseline IRFs — house prices, household credit, labour productivity](../sources/source-0028.md#section-4-1-house-prices-credit-productivity)

> "Since New Zealand is characterised by high-skilled immigration, one would expect immigration to have a positive impact on labour productivity and consequently a positive impact on long-term economic growth. However, the IRF... reports otherwise: a muted response of labour productivity to an expansionary immigration shock. This result is in line with what the New Zealand Productivity Commission concluded in its 2022 report: 'immigration is likely to be neither the solution to, nor the cause of, the productivity challenges facing New Zealand' (New Zealand Productivity Commission, 2022)." (Javed, Power & Richardson, 2026, Section 4.1, p. 16)

**Qualifiers:**
- The authors note this result runs counter to the a priori expectation generated by NZ's high-skilled immigration composition, making it a notable null result rather than an unsurprising one.
- The paper itself explicitly draws the connection to the NZPC (2022) finding, so this is a case of independent quantitative corroboration of a qualitative/descriptive conclusion already in this corpus (claim-0154), rather than a literature citation being mistaken for a primary finding.
