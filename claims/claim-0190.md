# claim-0190

Preliminary modelling finds that artificially raising rents lowers wages, so policies that restrict urban development — such as density controls — squeeze workers on two fronts simultaneously: higher housing costs and lower wages.

**Source:** [Donovan (2019)](../sources/source-0031.md) — [Preliminary findings from the dynamic model](../sources/source-0031.md#slide-preliminary-findings)

> "Artificially raising rents leads to lower wages. Policies that restrict urban development, e.g. density controls, squeeze workers on two fronts: Higher housing costs and lower wages." (Donovan, 2019, slide deck — preliminary/unpublished findings)

**Qualifiers:**
- Explicitly labelled as a preliminary finding from ongoing, unpublished research; the underlying mechanism is not detailed in the slide deck beyond this summary statement.
- This implies that the welfare cost of land-use restrictions on workers is larger than a housing-cost-only analysis would suggest, since it omits the additional wage channel.
