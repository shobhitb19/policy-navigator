# claim-0234

Treasury's 2017 assessment was that Auckland is more productive than the rest of New Zealand but underperforms relative to its size and comparable primate cities internationally — and that its productivity premium had been shrinking over time.

**Source:** [The Treasury (2017)](../sources/source-0045.md) — [On a page](../sources/source-0045.md#on-a-page)

**Reported finding** (paraphrased — the source document is not publicly available): the document assesses that Auckland underperforms relative both to its size and to comparable primate cities internationally, and that while Auckland is more productive than the rest of New Zealand, its productivity premium had been shrinking over time (The Treasury, 2017, p. 4).

**Qualifiers:**
- Draft internal working document, "Not Government Policy"; the assessment is Treasury's synthesis of research including Sapere work commissioned for the Auckland Story (Glass and Fry, 2016) and Zheng (2016).
- The "shrinking premium" observation (as of 2017) is a statement about the level of Auckland's productivity premium; it is consistent with MHuD's later observation that agglomeration benefits have reduced since the 1980s–90s (claim-0076 source), and coexists with Donovan et al. (2022)'s finding that Auckland's agglomeration *elasticities* strengthened after the 1980s reforms — level and elasticity are different objects.
- Corroborates, eight years earlier, the same underperformance diagnosis later made by Blick & Stewart (2025) and the Committee for Auckland (2025).
