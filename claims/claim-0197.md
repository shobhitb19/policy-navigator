# claim-0197

Doubling urban population density is associated with approximately 7% lower energy consumption, a citation-weighted average across the collected literature that the authors treat as associative rather than causal.

**Source:** [Ahlfeldt and Pietrostefani (2019)](../sources/source-0032.md) — [Table 6: Recommended elasticity estimates by category](../sources/source-0032.md#table-6-recommended-elasticities)

> "The recommended elasticity estimate for energy use reduction is 0.07... citation-weighted mean." (Ahlfeldt and Pietrostefani, 2019, Table 6 and accompanying discussion)

**Evidence base:** [evidence-0003](../evidence/evidence-0003.md)

**Qualifiers:**
- Energy use is not one of the five categories the authors regard as causally interpretable; this is an association, not a causal estimate.
- This is general energy consumption (e.g. building heating/cooling efficiency from shared walls and smaller dwellings), not transport-specific fuel use or CO₂ emissions — it should not be conflated with transport-emissions findings such as claim-0028 or claim-0036.
- Supersedes the energy-consumption figure previously bundled into claim-0043 (retired), which cited this result second-hand via Lall et al. (2021).

**Related:** Replaces (in part): [claim-0043](claim-0043.md) (retired).
