# claim-0064

New Zealand's capital stock per hour worked is roughly half that of Australia, Sweden, and the US, and the gap has been widening — Denmark deployed 81% more capital per hour than NZ in 1970, rising to 164% more by 2019; South Korea went from 15% of NZ's level to 50% above it over the same period.

**Source:** [NZ Productivity Commission (2023)](../sources/source-0014.md) — [Section 3: Capital stock](../sources/source-0014.md#section-3-capital)

> "New Zealand's capital stock per hour worked has remained at around half that of countries like Australia, Sweden, and the US. However, it has been dropping further behind other countries. Denmark deployed 81% more capital for every hour worked than New Zealand in 1970, but this had risen to 164% (that is, 2.5 times that of New Zealand) in 2019. South Korea's capital per worker was a mere 15% of New Zealand's in 1970 and, by 2019, this had risen to almost 50% higher than New Zealand's." (NZPC, 2023, p. 38)

**Qualifiers:**
- Capital is measured as real capital stock per hour worked, not per worker; differences in hours worked affect the comparison.
- Low capital intensity could reflect: high cost of capital, low savings rates, capital allocation to non-productive assets (housing), or industry composition.
- Consistent with Conway & Meehan (2013) finding that 39% of the NZ–Australia productivity gap is attributable to capital intensity (claim-0061).
