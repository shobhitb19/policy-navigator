# claim-0145

The Productivity Commission recommended that, as part of the Accredited Employer Work Visa design, the Government should enable migrants to move between any accredited employer with minimal government-imposed transaction costs, while collecting information on migrants' movements to monitor for system gaming and poor employer behaviour.

**Source:** [NZ Productivity Commission (2022)](../sources/source-0027.md) — [Chapter 4, Findings 16-17 and Recommendation 6: Tying migrants to a single employer enables employer monopsony power](../sources/source-0027.md#chapter-4-employer-tying-monopsony)

> "Recommendation 6: As part of the design of the Accredited Employer Work Visa (and/or future employer-sponsored visas), the Government should enable migrants to move between any accredited employer with minimal government-imposed transaction costs for the migrant or employer. Information on migrants' movements within the employment system should be collected to monitor for risks of gaming the system and for risks of poor employer behaviour." (NZPC, 2022, Part 4, Recommendation 6, p.57)

**Qualifiers:**
- This is a normative policy recommendation, not an empirical finding; it follows directly from the Commission's finding that employer-tying enables monopsony power (claim-0144).
- The Commission notes the recommendation requires complementary measures — employer accreditation checks and adequate resourcing of the Labour Inspectorate — to manage the moral-hazard and monitoring risks created by greater job mobility.
- Presented as an opportunity created by the (at the time) imminent shift to the Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV) system, which the Commission viewed as already requiring employer checks.
