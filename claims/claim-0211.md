# claim-0211

The labour productivity of New Zealand's frontier firms is less than half that of the best frontier firms in comparator small advanced economies, with the New Zealand frontier declining from 48% to 45% of the small-advanced-economy frontier over 2003–16.

**Source:** [NZPC (2021)](../sources/source-0039.md) — [Chapter 3: Benchmarking the New Zealand frontier](../sources/source-0039.md#chapter-3-benchmarking-frontiers)

> "Not only did the top firms in New Zealand have the largest productivity gap relative to the small-advanced-economy frontier, but that this gap increased over time. The New Zealand frontier declined from 48% to 45% of the small-advanced-economy frontier over 2003–16. … The large size of this lag – over 50% – is a central finding of the inquiry." (NZPC, 2021, p. 40, Figure 3.5, F3.1)

**Qualifiers:**
- The comparator small advanced economies are Belgium, Denmark, Finland, the Netherlands and Sweden; the SAE frontier is defined per industry as the average of the three highest national frontiers, using CompNet microdata (Zheng et al., 2021).
- "Frontier firms" here are the top 10% of firms by labour productivity within each broad industry; the gap exists across all nine industries studied, with variation.
- Over the same period the New Zealand frontier's labour productivity grew at only 0.6% a year — similar to the Netherlands, faster than Denmark and Finland, slower than Sweden and Belgium — so the frontier was not catching up despite its much lower starting level (F3.2).
- Cross-country level comparisons of labour productivity depend on currency conversion (euro 2005 prices, PPP-adjusted); MFP levels could not be compared.
