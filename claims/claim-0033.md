# claim-0033

Urban wage premiums in large emerging economies are far larger than in either advanced economies or Africa: approximately 45% in China, 122% in India, and 176% in Brazil; by contrast, estimated urban wage premiums in Africa (after full worker controls) are only 6–11%.

**Source:** [Collier, Jones & Spijkerman (2018)](../sources/source-0007.md) — [Introduction](../sources/source-0007.md#introduction)

> "While urban workers in the United States, earn about 30% more than their rural counterparts (Glaeser and Maré, 2001), the urban-wage premium is 45% in China, 122% in India, and 176% in Brazil (Chauvin et al, 2016). In Sub-Saharan Africa, the estimated urban wage premium is somewhat smaller. New research from Jones et al (2017) estimates an urban wage premium of 6% in Nigeria, 11% in Tanzania, and 9% in Uganda when a full set of worker controls are included." (Collier et al., 2018, Introduction, p. 5)

**Qualifiers:**
- The Brazil, China, and India figures are from Chauvin et al. (2017); different studies use different control sets and spatial definitions, so cross-country comparisons should be treated as indicative.
- The Africa figures (Jones et al., 2017) include full worker controls (education, experience); raw urban-rural wage gaps in Africa are larger and would be closer in magnitude to other regions.
- Urban wage premiums may partly reflect sorting (more skilled workers moving to cities) rather than pure agglomeration returns — and the extent of sorting correction differs across studies.
- The US figure (30%) predates remote work trends; current estimates may differ.

**Related:** Provides cross-country context for: [claim-0031](claim-0031.md) (agglomeration output elasticity in developing cities), [claim-0001](claim-0001.md) (global meta-analysis median 4.6%).
