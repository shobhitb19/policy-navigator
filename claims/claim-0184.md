# claim-0184

A New Zealand city's workforce shares in education and health are negatively associated with its Quality of Business: in 1996-2013, a one standard deviation increase in education workforce share is associated with a 0.43 SD decrease in QB, and health with a 0.37 SD decrease, both highly significant.

**Source:** [Preston, Maré, Grimes & Donovan (2018)](../sources/source-0030.md) — [Table 4: QB regressions — population, education, health](../sources/source-0030.md#table-4-qb-population-education)

> In 1996-2013, education and health workforce shares are negatively associated with QB (−0.427*** and −0.371*** respectively), possibly reflecting government siting of education and health facilities in otherwise less business-friendly locations. (Preston, Maré, Grimes & Donovan, 2018, Table 4)

**Qualifiers:**
- The authors suggest this may reflect reverse causation — government-funded education and health facilities are often deliberately sited in lower-business-amenity locations to support disadvantaged communities, rather than education/health activity itself depressing QB.
- This contrasts with the positive relationship between education and Quality of Life documented in claim-0181, illustrating a further household/firm divergence beyond the population-driven one in claims 0182-0183.
