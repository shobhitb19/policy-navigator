# claim-0236

Auckland accounts for close to 40% of New Zealand's GDP and over a third of employment, holds 35% of all businesses and 61% of the top 200 companies, and is the departure point for 18% of the value of New Zealand's exports.

**Source:** [MartinJenkins (2022)](../sources/source-0046.md) — [Economy and industrial structure](../sources/source-0046.md#economy-footprint)

> "Auckland plays an important economic role for New Zealand, accounting for close to 40% of New Zealand's GDP and over a third of employment. It holds 35% of all businesses and 61% of the top 200 companies." (Chen, Knuckey & Jones, 2022, p. 5)

**Qualifiers:**
- Commissioned indicator compilation for the NZ Government Auckland Policy Office; publicly released. The insights are the authors' own and do not represent government policy.
- The 61% top-200-companies figure measures headquarters concentration — corroborating, from a different data source, the finding that New Zealand's most significant firms are disproportionately Auckland-based (see claim-0233 on frontier firms).
- The GDP share (~40%, as of c.2021 data) sits slightly above the 37.9–38% figures in Gluckman et al. (2022) and Blick & Stewart (2025); definitional and vintage differences explain the spread.

**Related:**
- [claim-0233](claim-0233.md) — most national frontier firms located in Auckland (Zheng 2016 via Treasury 2017)
- [claim-0016](claim-0016.md) — Auckland 37.9% of GDP, 35.5% of workforce (Gluckman et al. 2022)
- [claim-0226](claim-0226.md) — 38% of GDP but only 9% GDP-per-hour premium (Blick & Stewart 2025)
