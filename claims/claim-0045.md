# claim-0045

Urban built structures have a "putty clay" quality — once constructed, they can remain in place for 150+ years. Early infrastructure determines a city's spatial form for generations: Dar es Salaam's 1970s sites-and-services road plans still match its road network today, and those neighbourhoods command the highest land values in the city, exceeding even wealthy residential districts.

**Source:** [Lall et al. (2021)](../sources/source-0009.md) — [Chapter 4: Infrastructure path dependence](../sources/source-0009.md#chapter-4-path-dependence) and [Dar es Salaam evidence](../sources/source-0009.md#chapter-4-dar-es-salaam)

> "Urban structures share a 'putty clay' quality: once constructed, they are difficult to modify and can stay in place for more than 150 years. [...] Although drawn in the 1970s, the sites and services plans for Dar es Salaam closely match the shape of the city's road network today. This durability confirms that investment in infrastructure determines a city's future course, while leading to higher land values that are taxable and can finance future investments." (Lall et al., 2021, pp. 83, 85)

**Qualifiers:**
- The Dar es Salaam evidence is from Michaels et al. (forthcoming, *Journal of Political Economy*); the paper was not yet published at time of this report's release (2021).
- The policy implication — infrastructure before housing before industry — is a general principle with important local exceptions.
- Retrofitting infrastructure after settlement is consistently more expensive and faces political resistance.
