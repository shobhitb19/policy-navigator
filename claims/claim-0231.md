# claim-0231

Location outcomes emerge from the balance between agglomeration forces — lower transport costs, economies of scale and scope, thick labour markets with better matching, informational externalities and knowledge spillovers, and consumption benefits — and dispersion forces — higher living costs (particularly housing), higher factor costs, infrastructure pressure, pollution, and social problems.

**Source:** [Box (2000)](../sources/source-0044.md) — [Executive summary](../sources/source-0044.md#executive-summary)

> "At the heart of the framework is the idea that people and firms choose their location to maximise their welfare and profits respectively and that various forces which can affect welfare and profits push and pull people and firms towards a particular place." (Box, 2000, p. 3)

**Qualifiers:**
- Box distinguishes these endogenous forces from exogenous ones (natural geography, historical features including border effects, institutions) that agents take as given.
- The paper notes telecommunications were "predicted to remain a complement rather than a substitute for face-to-face contact" — a 2000 prediction subsequently borne out by the patent-citation evidence (claims 0200, 0205).
- The balance of forces differs across locations and industries; the efficiency case is to let the forces work rather than stopping an agglomeration early or pushing it past its natural scale.
