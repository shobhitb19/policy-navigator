# claim-0235

Treasury assessed in 2017 that the positive agglomeration benefits of Auckland's growth were being outweighed by the negative impacts of that growth, with environmental, housing and transport indicators all reflecting a city under increasing pressure.

**Source:** [The Treasury (2017)](../sources/source-0045.md) — [On a page](../sources/source-0045.md#on-a-page); [Growth pressures](../sources/source-0045.md#growth-pressures)

**Reported finding** (paraphrased — the source document is not publicly available): the document assesses that the benefits associated with city scale were being outweighed by the negative impacts of growth, with environmental, housing and transport indicators all showing a city under increasing pressure (The Treasury, 2017, p. 4).

**Qualifiers:**
- Supporting indicators cited by the document: Auckland added 174,000 people (a city the size of Hamilton) between the 2010 amalgamation and 2017; New Zealand had the largest increase in house prices relative to incomes in the OECD since 2010; congestion on the arterial network was increasing 2.5% per year; 45% of Auckland's population increase was due to migration.
- Draft internal working document, "Not Government Policy" — a contemporaneous official diagnosis, not a peer-reviewed finding.
- This is an early official statement of the position paper's core "growth absorbed into costs rather than converted into productivity" argument, predating Te Waihanga (2022), MHuD (2024) and Blick & Stewart (2025).
