# claim-0154

Immigration is not the cause of, nor is it likely to be the solution to, New Zealand's persistent productivity underperformance: the country's GDP growth has kept pace with OECD peers by adding more workers and longer hours rather than by raising output per hour worked.

**Source:** [NZ Productivity Commission (2022)](../sources/source-0027.md) — [Overview: Immigration is neither the cause of, nor solution to, New Zealand's productivity woes](../sources/source-0027.md#overview-immigration-productivity)

> "The Commission's overall conclusion is that immigration is not likely to be the solution to the productivity challenges facing 21st century Aotearoa, nor is it the cause or source of our productivity problems. This conclusion is consistent with what studies find overseas – mostly small positive effects of immigration on average levels of labour productivity... New Zealand's GDP growth has kept pace with other countries in recent years, but it has done so by working harder, rather than working smarter." (NZPC, 2022, Overview, p.4)

**Qualifiers:**
- The relationship requires balancing short-run and long-run impacts: migrants may increase productive capacity in the long run, but this can take time, and in the short run there may be costs associated with available physical and community infrastructure (see claim-0136, absorptive capacity).
- The Commission's finding is consistent with — not derived independently from — international evidence, which mostly finds small positive effects of immigration on average labour productivity.
- This conclusion was paraphrased secondhand in Lala, Spoonley & Gluckman (2026) (source-0021), which is how it first entered this corpus as a qualifier on claim-0094; this card now provides the primary citation.
