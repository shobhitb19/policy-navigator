# claim-0263

Auckland's innovation ecosystem is thin where depth matters: its alumni founder base is less than half the size of Lisbon's, Helsinki's or Vancouver's, the corporate share of its tech ecosystem is half Vancouver's or Dublin's, and scale-ups absorb 50% of venture capital deployed in the city against 29% globally.

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland & TBoC (2026)](../sources/source-0048.md) — [Focus area 2: innovation and talent engine](../sources/source-0048.md#innovation-talent)

> "While 19 alumni-founded startups have achieved rounds over US$100 million, the founder alumni base is less than half the size of Lisbon's, Helsinki's or Vancouver's, and Auckland's position weakens at each stage of business growth." (p. 28)
>
> "Need for deeper corporate base. The share of corporates in Auckland's tech ecosystem is only half that of Vancouver or Dublin, which makes it harder for reference customers, later-stage capital and first commercial deals to land." (p. 28)
>
> "Scale-ups command 50% of venture capital deployed in Auckland against 29% on average globally, a signal of investor confidence in Auckland's scale-ups and of more limited access to capital for earlier-stage firms." (p. 26)

**Qualifiers:**
- Dealroom.co Global Tech Ecosystem Index 2026. The VC concentration figure is genuinely double-edged and the report says so: it evidences confidence in the scale-ups that exist and a shortage of capital for the firms below them.
- Offsetting positive: university–industry co-authored publications nearly doubled from 4% to 7%, "one of the largest rises among similar cities" (CWTS Leiden Ranking 2025), and eight University of Auckland subject fields now rank in the global top 50. The 2030 target is a 10% co-publication share.
- Student-to-worker retention is flagged as under-developed despite Auckland rating 15th worldwide for student mix, with some talent-concentration measures falling over 40 places year on year.
- Data-centre stock sits in the bottom third of Asia-Pacific markets, which the report treats as a binding constraint on AI and cloud-intensive activity.
- Together these describe a missing middle: strong early formation, strong headline scale-ups, and too little between them — the same shape as the firm-retention gap in claim-0251.
