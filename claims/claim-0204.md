# claim-0204

Industries where knowledge spillovers are important — measured by R&D intensity, university research proximity, and the share of scientists and engineers — agglomerate their innovative activity more intensely than their production activity, beyond what the general concentration of production would predict.

**Sources:** [Audretsch & Feldman (2004)](../sources/source-0034.md) — [Section 3: Evidence on innovative activity clustering](../sources/source-0034.md#section-3-clustering-evidence)

> "Industries where knowledge spillovers play a greater role tend to cluster in space... the propensity of innovative activity to cluster is greater than the propensity of production to cluster, and that difference is positively associated with the importance of knowledge spillovers in the industry." (Audretsch & Feldman, 2004, p. 2724; summarising Audretsch & Feldman 1996)

**Qualifiers:**
- The original Audretsch & Feldman (1996) paper uses US data at the state level; the 2004 chapter reviews this and subsequent international replications.
- The finding is correlational — it identifies which industries cluster more without establishing a clean causal mechanism for why.
- "Propensity to cluster" is measured using a variant of the Ellison-Glaeser index; the comparison between production and innovation clustering is the key contribution.
- The mechanism is theorised as being knowledge spillovers (see claim-0203), but could partially reflect agglomeration of skilled workers who are complements to innovation rather than spillovers per se.
- Later work (Ahlfeldt & Pietrostefani 2019, claim-0196) estimates a city-density elasticity for patent intensity of +0.21, consistent with this finding at the city level.
