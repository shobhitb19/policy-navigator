# claim-0174

New Zealand cities and towns became more industrially diversified, not more specialised, between 1976 and 2013: 23 of 30 urban areas saw their Regional Specialisation Index decline, and the fastest-growing areas diversified faster (−0.10 points on average) than slower-growing areas (−0.04 points).

**Source:** [Coleman, Maré & Zheng (2019)](../sources/source-0029.md) — [Section 4: Regional Specialisation Index trends](../sources/source-0029.md#section-4-regional-specialisation)

> "Regional specialization declined over time, with 23 of the 30 urban areas becoming more diversified between 1976 and 2013." The fastest-growing areas (e.g. Nelson, Tauranga) became more diversified rather than more specialised, with their Regional Specialisation Index declining by 0.10 points on average compared with 0.04 points for slower-growing areas. (Coleman, Maré & Zheng, 2019, Section 4)

**Qualifiers:**
- The Regional Specialisation Index is a Gini-coefficient-based measure ranging from 0 (fully diversified, matching the national industry mix) to 1 (fully specialised).
- This trend runs counter to a simple "growth comes from specialisation/comparative advantage" narrative — growing NZ cities broadened their industry base rather than concentrating it.
