# claim-0149

Over 2015-19, New Zealand had one Labour Inspectorate inspector for every 40,000 workers — about a quarter of the International Labour Organisation's recommended benchmark of one inspector per 10,000 workers for industrial market economies — limiting enforcement against minimum employment standard breaches and migrant exploitation.

**Source:** [NZ Productivity Commission (2022)](../sources/source-0027.md) — [Chapter 5, Finding 25 and Recommendation 16: Labour Inspectorate under-resourced relative to ILO benchmark](../sources/source-0027.md#chapter-5-labour-inspectorate-enforcement)

> "Over 2015–19, New Zealand had one inspector for every 40 000 workers, or about a quarter of the number recommended by the ILO for effective enforcement of labour standards in industrial market economies... Finding 25: The Labour Inspectorate has insufficient resources to enforce minimum standards across the labour market, particularly in areas relating to migrant exploitation." (NZPC, 2022, Part 5, p.74-75)

**Qualifiers:**
- The ILO benchmark scales with development level: one inspector per 10,000 workers for industrial market economies, per 20,000 for transition economies, and per 40,000 for less-developed countries — New Zealand's actual ratio matches the threshold the ILO sets for less-developed countries, not the relevant high-income benchmark.
- MBIE's best estimate (2020) found 8% of temporary migrant workers (about 20,000 workers) had not received one or more minimum employment rights or had been asked to pay money to keep their job, against which the Labour Inspectorate completed only around 530 investigations per year across the entire workforce (2013-2019), 58% involving a migrant worker.
- The Commission recommends increasing Labour Inspectorate funding over three years to reach the one-per-10,000 benchmark, explicitly tied to the integrity of the (at the time) imminent Accredited Employer Work Visa scheme.
