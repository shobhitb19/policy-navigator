# claim-0062

New Zealand's GDP per hour worked has been below the OECD average for over 50 years; the relative decline began with the 1973 oil shock and continued to the mid-2000s, when NZ's position stabilised at well below OECD peers.

**Source:** [NZ Productivity Commission (2023)](../sources/source-0014.md) — [Section 3.1: Long-run productivity decline](../sources/source-0014.md#section-3-1-long-run)

> "Aotearoa New Zealand's workers have been producing less for every hour worked than those in other OECD countries for half a century. In 1970, New Zealand's GDP per hour worked was only marginally below the OECD average... In the first half of the 1970s, New Zealand's productivity dropped behind that of its peers, particularly after the Oil Shock of 1972. This marked the beginning of a long period of relative decline." (NZPC, 2023, p. 33)

**Qualifiers:**
- "OECD average" here refers to early OECD members (those joining before 1975); NZ is within this group.
- The stabilisation in the mid-2000s reflects a period in which many OECD countries also saw a productivity slowdown, not necessarily an improvement in NZ's underlying performance.
- Updated with data through 2022; consistent with Conway & Meehan (2013) for the earlier period.
