# claim-0028

Per capita ground-transport CO₂ emissions fall consistently as urban density rises; compact cities emit substantially less transport CO₂ per capita than sprawling cities of equivalent population.

**Source:** [OECD (2015)](../sources/source-0006.md) — [Chapter 3: Urban density and transport emissions](../sources/source-0006.md#chapter-3-environment)

> "Per-capita CO₂ emissions from ground transport fall as urban density rises... compact cities have substantially lower per-capita transport emissions than sprawling cities." (OECD, 2015, Chapter 3, p. 105; drawing on Kennedy et al., 2009)

**Qualifiers:**
- Cross-city comparison; does not control for income level, fuel mix, vehicle fleet composition, or modal availability — all of which co-vary with density.
- The density–emissions relationship is associational; compact form may be accompanied by other low-emission policies (transit investment, fuel taxes) that independently reduce emissions.
- The OECD draws on Kennedy et al. (2009), which covers a specific set of large cities; extrapolation to all city types should be cautious.
- Compact density can increase non-transport emissions (e.g. heating in high-rise buildings) that partially offset transport gains.

**Related:** Consistent with: [claim-0197](claim-0197.md) (density associated with lower general energy consumption — a related but distinct metric from transport CO₂). Supports: [claim-0025](claim-0025.md) (governance bodies associated with declining sprawl → lower emissions pathway). See also: [synthesis/07](../synthesis/07-resilience-environment-sustainability.md).
