# claim-0112

The Resource Management Act 1991 removed specific requirements for infrastructure planning from New Zealand planning legislation, decoupling urban development planning from infrastructure provision; prior legislation (Town and Country Planning Acts 1953, 1977) had required councils to plan coordinated infrastructure investment alongside development.

**Source:** [Te Waihanga (2022)](../sources/source-0023.md) — [Explaining these patterns](../sources/source-0023.md#explaining-these-patterns)

> "The Resource Management Act 1991, by contrast, removed specific requirements for infrastructure planning. Infrastructure was now seen as one of many effects to manage, rather than a foundation for urban development and community wellbeing that must be planned and coordinated in advance." (Nunns & Dodge, 2022, p.17)

Under the 1953 and 1977 Acts, regional plans were required to co-ordinate public utilities and transport; district schemes were required to plan sewerage, drainage, water, and transport networks. The RMA replaced these with a general effects-management framework with no specific infrastructure requirements.

**Qualifiers:**
- The paper characterises this as a cause of housing supply decline, but the causal direction runs through councils' reduced capacity to plan enabling infrastructure, not directly through prohibition on building.
- Plan complexity also increased substantially after the RMA (plan length in Auckland, Wellington, and Christchurch grew rapidly post-1991), partly because councils used the wider effects mandate to regulate more issues while retaining pre-RMA development restrictions.
- The paper notes that the 'great downzoning' of the 1970s and 1980s predates the RMA; the RMA worsened the situation but was not its origin.
- Subsequent reforms (National Policy Statement on Urban Development 2020, Medium Density Residential Standards 2021) partially restored the planning-infrastructure link.
