# claim-0164

The immigration shock explains less than 10% of the variation in labour productivity over the full forecast horizon, the smallest contribution of any variable in the model, consistent with the muted productivity response observed in the impulse response functions.

**Source:** [Javed, Power & Richardson (2026)](../sources/source-0028.md) — [Section 4.2: Forecast error variance decomposition](../sources/source-0028.md#section-4-2-fevd)

> "...the immigration shock explains less than 10% of the variation in labour productivity over the full horizon period, which is less than the proportion of variation explained for the other variables in the model." (Javed, Power & Richardson, 2026, Section 4.2, pp. 16-18)

**Qualifiers:**
- Reported alongside other variance shares for context: household credit (16%), OCR (15%), and the real exchange rate (13%) are all described by the authors as "negligible" relative to GDP, unemployment, and the participation rate.
