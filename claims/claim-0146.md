# claim-0146

Skill Shortage Lists used to grant employers easier access to migrant workers can become cumbersome, open to lobbying from employers and worker representatives, and are inherently backward-looking because they aim to solve labour shortages that have already emerged rather than anticipate future ones.

**Source:** [NZ Productivity Commission (2022)](../sources/source-0027.md) — [Chapter 4, Finding 15 and Recommendations 4-5: Skill Shortage Lists are backward-looking and prone to lobbying](../sources/source-0027.md#chapter-4-skill-shortage-lists)

> "Finding 15: Overly stringent settings related to temporary work visas will increase claimed labour shortages and the demand for exemptions. However, exemption lists can become cumbersome, open to lobbying, and are (by their nature) backward looking, in that they aim to solve yesterday's labour shortages." (NZPC, 2022, Part 4, p.54-55)

**Qualifiers:**
- The Commission acknowledges Skill Shortage Lists "emerged for a reason": they reduce wage costs for businesses and provide a politically pragmatic pathway for exemptions from general immigration settings.
- By enabling easier migrant access in listed occupations, SSLs can also reduce employer incentives to raise wages, invest in capital equipment, or train local workers (a related but distinct finding from the same chapter).
- The diagnosis of a "skill shortage" is described as inherently subjective, requiring substantial judgement even when data-driven indicators are used.
