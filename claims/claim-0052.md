# claim-0052

In New Zealand, 91% of the apparent density–productivity relationship reflects the sorting of productive firms into dense locations rather than true agglomeration: the pooled OLS elasticity of 0.171 falls to 0.048 with composition controls and to 0.015 with firm fixed effects.

**Source:** [Maré & Graham (2009)](../sources/source-0011.md) — [Section 5: Pooled results](../sources/source-0011.md#section-5-pooled-results)

> "The pooled elasticity of 0.171 is reduced to 0.048 when we control for industry and regional composition differences... The within-enterprise fixed effects specification yields an elasticity of 0.015, a 91% reduction from pooled estimates, suggesting substantial sorting of productive firms into denser areas." (Maré & Graham, 2009, p. 15–17)

**Qualifiers:**
- The 91% figure is the reduction from pooled OLS (0.171) to within-firm FE (0.015), and includes both selection (sorting) and attenuation from measurement error.
- The preferred estimate (0.069 with industry-specific production functions within local industry) lies between these bounds.
- Consistent with international evidence that raw OLS estimates of agglomeration are substantially upward biased (see claim-0014).
