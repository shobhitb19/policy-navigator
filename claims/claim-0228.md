# claim-0228

The productivity benefits of urban density are unevenly distributed across firms: in French data, establishments in above-median-density areas are on average 9.7% more productive than elsewhere, but the gain is only 4.8% for establishments at the bottom quartile of the productivity distribution and about 14.4% at the top quartile — the most productive firms are best able to reap the benefits of density.

**Source:** [Combes et al. (2012)](../sources/source-0042.md) — [Main findings](../sources/source-0042.md#section-1-main-findings)

> "More productive establishments are better able to reap the benefits of urban density, which dilates the log productivity distribution. As a result, while the average productivity gain is about 9.7 percent, establishments at the bottom quartile of the log productivity distribution are only 4.8 percent more productive in employment areas with above-median density than elsewhere whereas establishments at the top quartile are about 14.4 percent more productive in denser areas." (Combes et al., 2012, p. 4)

**Qualifiers:**
- The comparison is between employment areas with above-median density and all other areas, using establishment-level TFP in France.
- The "dilation" of the productivity distribution is the empirical signature of heterogeneous agglomeration benefits — not of selection (see claim-0227).
- Implication for frontier-firm policy: dense metropolitan environments disproportionately benefit the most productive firms, so the costs of weak urban systems fall hardest on exactly the firms with frontier potential.
