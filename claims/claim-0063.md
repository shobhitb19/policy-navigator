# claim-0063

New Zealand's output and income growth since the 1980s has come primarily from working more hours (labour utilisation), not from higher hourly productivity — New Zealanders work longer hours than most OECD peers while producing less output per hour.

**Source:** [NZ Productivity Commission (2023)](../sources/source-0014.md) — [Section 3.3: Labour utilisation vs. productivity](../sources/source-0014.md#section-3-3-half-century)

> "Compared with other early OECD members, the majority of Aotearoa New Zealand's output and income growth has come from increasing the amount of labour input used, rather than the productivity with which it is employed... New Zealanders have been consistently working more hours than other OECD economies since the 1980s." (NZPC, 2023, p. 36–37)

**Qualifiers:**
- "Working more hours" reflects both higher employment rates and stable (rather than declining) average hours per worker.
- This pattern is "not a recipe for sustained improvements in material living standards" per the Commission — it is extensive rather than intensive growth.
