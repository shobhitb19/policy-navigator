# claim-0075

Removing all housing supply constraints in New Zealand would raise output per worker by 1.1–1.6%, expand the labour force by 3.3–7.2%, and increase GDP by 5.0–8.4%, according to Nunns (2021).

**Source:** [MHuD (2024)](../sources/source-0018.md) — [Executive Summary](../sources/source-0018.md#executive-summary), citing Nunns (2021)

> "Nunns (2021) estimated that removing all housing supply constraints in New Zealand would result in output per worker, which is a proxy for labour productivity, being about 1.1% to 1.6% higher; the NZ labour force would become 3.3% to 7.2% larger; and total economic output (GDP) would increase between 5.0% to 8.4%." (MHuD, 2024, para. 5)

**Qualifiers:**
- Nunns (2021) is the published journal version (NZEP 55(1)) of Nunns (2019), EPC Working Paper 003, now held in the repo as source-0041. The working-paper version of this full-reform scenario reports GDP +4.5–7.7% and output per worker +0.9–1.4% (see claim-0224); the journal revision updated the figures to those quoted here. The Auckland-only scenario in the same study is claim-0066 (GDP +0.9–1.8%).
- The GDP gain (5.0–8.4%) is substantially larger than the Auckland-only estimate because it covers all NZ cities and all constraint types.
- The labour force expansion (3.3–7.2%) reflects both retention of workers who would otherwise emigrate and migration effects from improved housing affordability.
- "Removing all constraints" is a theoretical full-reform scenario; actual policy reforms would realise a fraction of this.
