# claim-0013

Manufacturing sector agglomeration elasticities are approximately 0.6% lower than economy-wide estimates and have declined throughout the entire six-decade period 1960–2020, with estimates by 2020 approximately 10% smaller than in 1960.

**Source:** [Donovan et al. (2021)](../sources/source-0004.md) — [section 4.1](../sources/source-0004.md#section-4-1), [section 5](../sources/source-0004.md#section-5)

> "The right panel of Figure 8 reveals that estimates for manufacturing fell for the entire six decades covered by our data, especially from around 2000 onwards. By 2020, estimates for manufacturing were approximately 10% smaller than they were in 1960. This is an economically meaningful effect, which—if accurate—may explain urban industrial flight." (Donovan et al., 2021, section 5, pp. 31–32)

**Evidence base:** [evidence-0001](../evidence/evidence-0001.md)

**Qualifiers:**
- The −0.6% manufacturing sector differential is from the benchmark regression model (Table 3, Model 5); the base is the economy-wide estimate.
- The time trend for manufacturing is a statistical observation; the paper proposes declining freight costs and stricter environmental regulations as possible causes but treats these as speculative.
- "Approximately 10% smaller" refers to the absolute elasticity magnitude, not a 10 percentage point reduction; given elasticities in the 3–5% range, the decline is of the order of 0.3–0.5 percentage points.
- The trend is robust across sensitivity tests.

**Related:** Qualifies: [claim-0001](claim-0001.md), [claim-0012](claim-0012.md)
