# claim-0242

Firms at New Zealand's national productivity frontier grew MFP at an average 0.77% a year over 2000–2012 — almost 1.6 times the rate of local-frontier firms and 2.2 times the rate of laggards — so the productivity gap between laggards and the national frontier widened over the period.

**Source:** [Zheng (2016)](../sources/source-0047.md) — [Frontier growth dynamics](../sources/source-0047.md#frontier-growth-dynamics)

> "The productivity of firms at the national frontier expands at an average annual rate of 0.77%. This is almost 1.6 times faster than the productivity growth of firms at the local productivity frontier and 2.2 times faster than the productivity growth of laggards. … As a result, the productivity gap between lagging and national frontier firms has increased over time." (Zheng, 2016, p. 7, Figures 5–6)

**Qualifiers:**
- LBD data, 2000–2012; MFP aggregated to industry level then industry-weighted.
- A domestic widening-gap finding that parallels the OECD's global frontier/laggard divergence — but note the tension with NZPC (2021, claim-0213), which found NZ's 90:10 productivity ratio narrow and stable over 2003–16 on CompNet data; different frontier definitions, laggard definitions and periods account for the difference.
- Even NZ's fastest-growing firms (0.77%/yr) grew slowly by international frontier standards — the SAE frontier grew ~2.7%/yr over a comparable period (NZPC 2021).
