# claim-0037

Mumbai's Floor Space Index restriction (1.33 since 1991, down from 4.5) produced average residential space of only 4 square metres per person and left 54% of its 16 million residents living in slums.

**Source:** [World Bank (2009)](../sources/source-0008.md) — [Box 7.3: Mumbai FSI restrictions and slum formation](../sources/source-0008.md#box-7-3-mumbai)

> "In Mumbai it was set at 4.5. The standard practice in cities with limited land is to raise the permitted FSI over time to accommodate urban growth... Instead, the Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai went the other way, lowering the permitted FSI to 1.33 in 1991... space consumption in Mumbai averages 4 square metres, much less than the 12 square metres in Shanghai... Estimates indicate that 54 percent of Mumbai's 16 million people now live in slums." (World Bank, 2009, Chapter 7, Box 7.3, p. 205–206; citing Bertaud, 2003)

**Qualifiers:**
- Slum share is an estimate; different definitions of "slum" and informal settlement yield different figures.
- The causal link from FSI restriction to slum formation is argued by the WDR rather than established by a controlled analysis; other factors (income levels, migration rates, land tenure, rent control) also contribute.
- The FSI figure of 1.33 applied to new construction; the existing building stock includes pre-1964 buildings with higher FSIs.
- Rent control regulations, which freeze ~30% of Mumbai's housing stock, also contribute to the supply squeeze independently of FSI.
- Population and space figures are as of the WDR 2009 compilation date.

**Related:** Supports: [claim-0027](claim-0027.md) (building regulations raise floor-space prices 2–8x in London/NYC; Mumbai is a more extreme case). See also: [claim-0038](claim-0038.md) (land market institutions are a prerequisite for inclusive urbanisation).
