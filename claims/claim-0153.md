# claim-0153

MBIE's best estimate, based on the 2018 Migrant Survey, found that 8% of temporary migrant workers in New Zealand — about 20,000 workers — had not received one or more of their minimum employment rights, or had been asked to pay money to their employer to get or keep their job.

**Source:** [NZ Productivity Commission (2022)](../sources/source-0027.md) — [Chapter 5, Finding 25 and Recommendation 16: Labour Inspectorate under-resourced relative to ILO benchmark](../sources/source-0027.md#chapter-5-labour-inspectorate-enforcement)

> "In 2020, MBIE provided their best estimate of the exploitation problem based on an extrapolation of the results from New Zealand's Migrant Survey in 2018. It found that 8% of temporary migrant workers (about 20 000 workers) had not received one or more of their minimum employment rights or had been asked to pay money to their employer to get or keep their job (Office of the Minister for Workplace Relations and Safety & Office of the Minister of Immigration, 2020)." (NZPC, 2022, Part 5, Box 5.2, p.74)

**Qualifiers:**
- This is an extrapolated estimate from the 2018 Migrant Survey, not a direct count of confirmed exploitation cases.
- Against this estimated scale of approximately 20,000 affected workers, the Labour Inspectorate completed only around 530 investigations per year across the entire workforce between 2013 and 2019, of which 58% involved a migrant worker and, in 2018-19, nearly half were classified as serious exploitation.
- The Government's shift toward the Accredited Employer Work Visa system and improvements to Labour Inspectorate monitoring capacity were estimated by the Minister to double the number of reported exploitation cases, implying the true scale of under-reporting was considered substantial even relative to the 8% estimate.
