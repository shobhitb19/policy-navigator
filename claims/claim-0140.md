# claim-0140

In 2019/20, New Zealand produced US$68 of output per hour worked compared with an OECD average of US$85, and New Zealand's labour productivity has remained well below the OECD average over 2000-2020, with the gap widening over that period.

**Source:** [NZ Productivity Commission (2022)](../sources/source-0027.md) — [Chapter 2, Finding 3: NZ works harder but produces less per hour than the OECD average](../sources/source-0027.md#chapter-2-productivity-gap)

> "New Zealand's labour productivity has been weaker than the average OECD, and has not improved over the period (Figure 2.13). New Zealand produced less output an hour ($68) compared with the OECD average ($85) in 2019/20 (NZPC, 2021f)... Finding 3: New Zealand's output growth over the last two decades to 2020 has kept up, or eclipsed, the growth rates of other OECD countries. However, New Zealand's labour productivity has remained well below the OECD average, and the gap has widened." (NZPC, 2022, Part 2, p.23-24, Figure 2.13)

**Qualifiers:**
- Figures are GDP per hour worked in 2015 US$ (per Figure 2.13 source, OECD 2022); the OECD average is a weighted average across all OECD countries.
- This productivity gap exists despite New Zealand's GDP growth keeping pace with or exceeding other OECD countries over the same period (see claim-0141) — the gap is in output per hour, not aggregate output.
- This finding underlies the Commission's overall conclusion that immigration is neither the cause of, nor solution to, New Zealand's productivity problem.
