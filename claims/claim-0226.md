# claim-0226

Auckland generated $157 billion of GDP in the year to March 2024 — 38% of the national total — yet its GDP per hour worked was only 9% above the national average, and its GDP per capita premium has ranged from just 11–13% in recent years (2016–2024).

**Source:** [Blick & Stewart (2025)](../sources/source-0015.md) — [Auckland's productivity performance](../sources/source-0015.md#productivity-performance)

> "In the year to March 2024, Auckland's GDP was $157 billion, or 38% of the national total. That's $88,400 per person compared with the national average of $78,200 … Auckland's GDP per capita premium has ranged from 11–13% in recent years (2016–2024)." (Blick & Stewart, 2025)

> "GDP per hour worked, which measures labour productivity, shows Auckland had a 9% premium over the national average." (Blick & Stewart, 2025)

**Qualifiers:**
- The GDP per capita premium (11–13%) partly reflects Auckland's younger population and higher labour force participation; the GDP per hour worked premium (9%) is the cleaner labour productivity measure.
- The premium dipped to 5–9% between 2008 and 2015 before recovering to early-2000s levels.
- The gap between Auckland's 38% GDP share and its single-digit hourly productivity premium is the paper's core "scale without premium" observation — see claim-0065 for the international comparison (25–35% typical for comparable primate cities).
- This claim replaces the former claim-0098, which recorded the same figures via an unpublished secondary source; the figures are now attributed to their published source.
