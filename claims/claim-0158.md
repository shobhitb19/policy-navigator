# claim-0158

An expansionary immigration shock raises the labour force participation rate by around 0.10% on impact, with the response remaining statistically significant for more than a year.

**Source:** [Javed, Power & Richardson (2026)](../sources/source-0028.md) — [Section 4.1: Baseline IRFs — unemployment, real GDP, participation rate, real wages](../sources/source-0028.md#section-4-1-unemployment-gdp-participation-wages)

> "We observe a positive impact response of the participation rate of around 0.10%... For the participation rate, we observe a significantly positive response for more than a year after an expansionary immigration shock." (Javed, Power & Richardson, 2026, Section 4.1, p. 13)

**Qualifiers:**
- By construction of the model's sign-restriction identification scheme, an expansionary immigration shock is defined to produce a positive co-movement between real GDP and the participation rate, so this result is partly built into how the shock is identified rather than purely emergent.
- Significance is assessed at the 68% credible interval.
