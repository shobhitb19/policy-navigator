# claim-0223

Recent house price increases in New Zealand are due in large part to rising house price distortions — wedges between house prices and underlying costs of supply arising from binding supply constraints — and these distortions are largest in Auckland, Queenstown, Tauranga, Hamilton, and Wellington.

**Source:** [Nunns (2019)](../sources/source-0041.md) — [Abstract](../sources/source-0041.md#abstract)

> "Recent house price increases in New Zealand are due in large part to rising house price distortions, which reflect 'wedges' between house prices and underlying costs of supply. These distortions are largest in Auckland, Queenstown, Tauranga, Hamilton, and Wellington. They arise due to the collision of rising demand for housing, due to factors such as population growth, availability of mortgage credit, and tax policies that incentivise property investment, with housing supply constraints, such as zoning rules that limit new subdivision, limit redevelopment of existing sites, or require large lot sizes and costly features such as on-site carparking." (Nunns, 2019, p. iii)

**Qualifiers:**
- The distortion component is identified by decomposing regional house prices into building value, hedonic land value, and the residual wedge; the method follows Glaeser & Gyourko-style price-cost comparisons adapted to New Zealand data for 2000–16.
- Demand-side drivers (population growth, credit, tax settings) and supply constraints jointly produce the wedge; the paper attributes the distortion to their collision, not to either alone.
- Published in revised form as Nunns (2021), *New Zealand Economic Papers* 55(1).
