# claim-0244

Auckland's gateway flows — air, corporate, student, visitor, trade and innovation — aggregate to within 5% of the average share captured by comparable international gateway cities; the binding weakness is not the size of the flows but the failure to convert them into retained investment and tradeable industry.

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland & TBoC (2026)](../sources/source-0048.md) — [Focus area 1: gaps and implications](../sources/source-0048.md#gateway-gaps)

> "Overall, Auckland's gateway flows aggregate to within 5% of the average share of other comparable international cities, performing especially well as a corporate, aviation and student gateway. Its challenge appears to lie in converting these flows into more investment, visitors and tradeable industries." (The Business of Cities & Committee for Auckland, 2026, p. 25)

**Qualifiers:**
- "Share" here is measured relative to national totals — the proportion of the national flow that lands in the primate city — not relative to population. On a population-adjusted basis Auckland runs above 1.0× on most gateway measures (see claim-0247).
- This is the report's own synthesis judgement across eight gateway indicators, not a single measured statistic. The comparator set varies by indicator.
- The finding reframes the primate-city diagnosis: Auckland's concentration of national functions is broadly normal for a gateway city of its type, so the productivity shortfall documented elsewhere (claims 0085–0086) cannot be attributed to insufficient concentration alone.
