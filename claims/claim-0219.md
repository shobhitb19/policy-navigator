# claim-0219

Successful small advanced economies invest in building "innovation ecosystems" around their frontier firms in selected focus areas — networks in which larger "anchor" firms provide "canopy cover" for smaller firms and entrepreneurs, alongside skilled workers, research bodies, specialist investors, international links, and enabling infrastructure and regulation.

**Source:** [NZPC (2021)](../sources/source-0039.md) — [Chapter 6: Innovation ecosystems](../sources/source-0039.md#chapter-6-innovation-ecosystems)

> "These ecosystems are made up of entities, their capabilities, and the networks between them. Firms are at the centre of the ecosystem, including larger 'anchor' firms providing 'canopy cover' for small and medium enterprise (SMEs) and entrepreneurs. The ecosystem also includes workers with the right skills, international links, research bodies, education and training providers, mentors and investors with deep knowledge and understanding of the industry, and enabling infrastructure and regulations." (NZPC, 2021, p. 4; Chapter 6)

**Qualifiers:**
- The Commission finds New Zealand's innovation ecosystems "are not currently working well for actual and potential frontier firms": links between firms and public research institutions are mostly poor, and research is too focused on science excellence rather than impact and industry needs.
- The key difference from successful SAEs is not the existence of firm-level support (NZTE, Callaghan Innovation) but the absence of intentional, concentrated investment in a few ecosystems.
- Innovation ecosystems are conceptually close to, but broader than, geographic clusters: the report does not require co-location, though anchor-firm spillovers, labour pooling and specialist input sharing are the same mechanisms that underpin agglomeration economies.
