# claim-0147

The Productivity Commission recommended that the Government encourage wages to reflect labour-market scarcity rather than exempting specific industries, firms, and occupations from general labour-market rules, and reduce reliance on shortage lists for temporary work visas in favour of data-driven indicators (vacancy data, wage movements, demand for migrant labour) reviewed at least every two years.

**Source:** [NZ Productivity Commission (2022)](../sources/source-0027.md) — [Chapter 4, Finding 15 and Recommendations 4-5: Skill Shortage Lists are backward-looking and prone to lobbying](../sources/source-0027.md#chapter-4-skill-shortage-lists)

> "Recommendation 4: The Government should encourage wages to reflect scarcity, rather than exempt specific industries, firms, and occupations from general labour-market rules. Recommendation 5: The Government should reduce the use of shortage lists for approving temporary work visas and design them to inform education and training priorities, active labour market programmes, careers advice, industry strategy, and future resident-selection priorities. These lists should be data-driven and based on a set of indicators including vacancy data, wage movements, and demand for migrant labour. They should be monitored and updated regularly and reviewed every two years." (NZPC, 2022, Part 4, Recommendations 4-5, p.54-55)

**Qualifiers:**
- This is a normative policy recommendation rather than an empirical finding; it follows from Finding 15 (claim-0146) about the limitations of existing Skill Shortage Lists.
- The Commission does not propose eliminating shortage lists entirely, but repurposing them to inform education, training, and active labour market programmes rather than primarily gatekeeping visa access.
- Implementation depends on the data-infrastructure investments recommended elsewhere in the report (e.g., labour-market modelling, vacancy indicators in the Business Operations Survey).
