# claim-0039

South Africa's post-1994 housing programme built low-cost developments on city peripheries without transport links to employment centres; these developments failed to reduce intra-urban spatial division, and the program was redesigned only when connectivity was incorporated.

**Source:** [World Bank (2009)](../sources/source-0008.md) — [South Africa: peripheral housing without transport links](../sources/source-0008.md#south-africa-housing)

> "The first wave of low-cost housing developments, on the peripheries of major cities, lacked basic amenities and transport links to city (and job) centers. These developments failed to ameliorate intraurban divisions." (World Bank, 2009, Chapter 7, p. 214)

**Qualifiers:**
- The WDR 2009 cites this as a cautionary case; it does not provide counterfactual evidence on what would have happened with better-located housing.
- South Africa faced a uniquely severe starting condition — a 1.5 million unit deficit, 7.4 million people in slums, and a 35-piece legislative framework being simultaneously reformed.
- "Failed to ameliorate intra-urban divisions" is a qualitative assessment; specific welfare outcome data are not cited in the chapter.
- The programme was subsequently redesigned ("Breaking New Ground") to integrate transport access, which the WDR describes as more encouraging.

**Related:** Illustrates: [claim-0038](claim-0038.md) (targeted interventions fail without connective infrastructure in place). See also: [claim-0040](claim-0040.md) (Bogotá BRT — positive case of connectivity enabling inclusion).
