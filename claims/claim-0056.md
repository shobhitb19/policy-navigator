# claim-0056

Doubling the number of municipalities within a metropolitan area is associated with 3.2% lower productivity; where no metropolitan governance body exists the fragmentation penalty reaches 6%; a metropolitan governance body roughly halves the penalty to ~3%.

**Source:** [Ahrend et al. (2015)](../sources/source-0012.md) — [Section 4: Fragmentation and governance](../sources/source-0012.md#section-4-fragmentation)

> "Administrative fragmentation carries a significant penalty: doubling the number of municipalities within a metropolitan area is associated with 3.2% lower productivity... For metropolitan areas without a governance body the fragmentation penalty is 6%, but this penalty is halved (to 2.5–3%) when a metropolitan governance body exists." (Ahrend et al., 2015, SERC DP178, p. 17–19)

**Qualifiers:**
- Results are robust across the five-country sample but vary in magnitude; Mexico shows the largest fragmentation penalty.
- The governance body effect is estimated from variation in whether a metro area has a formal cross-municipal authority; it does not identify the specific powers of that body.
- Consistent with claim-0023 and claim-0024 from OECD (2015).

- [claim-0024](claim-0024.md) — OECD: metro governance body reduces fragmentation penalty by ~50%
