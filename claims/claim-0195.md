# claim-0195

Doubling urban population density is associated with approximately 4% higher wages in high-income countries, rising to approximately 8% in non-high-income countries, and the authors judge a causal interpretation justifiable given the identification strategies used in the underlying studies.

**Source:** [Ahlfeldt and Pietrostefani (2019)](../sources/source-0032.md) — [Table 6: Recommended elasticity estimates by category](../sources/source-0032.md#table-6-recommended-elasticities)

> "We are confident in recommending the [wage] elasticity of 0.04... a causal interpretation seems justifiable." (Ahlfeldt and Pietrostefani, 2019, Table 6 and accompanying discussion)

**Evidence base:** [evidence-0003](../evidence/evidence-0003.md)

**Qualifiers:**
- The 0.04 figure is a citation-weighted mean across the collected literature, predominantly North American and European studies; the non-high-income elasticity (~0.08) is based on a thinner evidence base.
- This is one of only five categories (wage, rent, vehicle miles travelled, pollution, average speed) for which the authors regard a causal reading as justified, rather than a pure association.
- Supersedes the wage figure previously bundled into claim-0043 (retired), which cited this result second-hand via Lall et al. (2021).

**Related:** Reinforces: [claim-0001](claim-0001.md). Replaces (in part): [claim-0043](claim-0043.md) (retired).
