# claim-0018

Auckland's public transport fares are among the highest in the world (third highest globally as of 2018), and transport poverty — spending more on transport than one can afford — falls disproportionately on Māori and Pasifika residents who have lower average incomes and live in areas with poor PT coverage.

**Source:** [Gluckman et al. (2022)](../sources/source-0005.md) — [chapter 2 (scenario 6)](../sources/source-0005.md#chapter-2-transport)

> "Auckland's public transport fares remain the third highest in the world (Greater Auckland, 2018). These problems contribute to transport poverty, a situation which arises when people pay more than they can afford for transport (Ministry of Transport, 2020). Māori and Pasifika disproportionately experience transport poverty since they tend to have lower average incomes than other Aucklanders, and many live in places that are not well served by public transport." (Gluckman et al., 2022, Chapter 2, Scenario 6)

**Qualifiers:**
- The "third highest globally" ranking is from Greater Auckland (2018), a policy advocacy group; comparison methodology and city selection are not detailed in Koi Tū.
- Transport poverty data from Ministry of Transport (2020); distributional figures from Spoonley et al. and Ministry of Transport sources cited in Koi Tū (2022).
- A recent study found Auckland's transport network was associated with psychological distress, particularly for lower-income residents (Wild et al., 2021, cited in Koi Tū 2022).
- The distributional inequity of transport costs is compounded by the geography of South and West Auckland, where Māori and Pasifika populations are concentrated and PT coverage is poorest.
