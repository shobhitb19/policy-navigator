# claim-0091

New Zealand's total fertility rate reached 1.55 in 2025 — well below the replacement rate of 2.1 — having declined rapidly since approximately 2014, driven by increased female workforce participation, housing costs, and inadequate parental leave settings.

**Source:** [Lala, Spoonley & Gluckman (2026)](../sources/source-0021.md) — [Section 2: Declining fertility](../sources/source-0021.md#section-2-fertility)

> "New Zealand's total fertility rate (TFR) declined rapidly since roughly 2014, reaching 1.55 in 2025 — below the replacement rate of 2.1." (Lala, Spoonley & Gluckman, 2026, p. Section 2)

**Qualifiers:**
- TFR of 1.55 is the 2025 figure; the trend has been consistently downward since 2014.
- Identified drivers: increased female education and workforce participation (60% in 1990 → 80% in 2024); one-third of low-income households spending >40% of income on housing; maximum paid parental leave of $780/week.
- Below-replacement fertility is common across high-income OECD countries; NZ is not an outlier but the rate of decline is notable.
