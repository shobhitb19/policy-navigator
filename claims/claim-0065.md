# claim-0065

Auckland's GDP per capita is only 13% above the national average, compared with the 25–35% typical for comparable primate cities in small-to-medium economies — suggesting significant unrealised agglomeration potential.

**Source:** [Auckland Council Chief Economist (2025)](../sources/source-0015.md) — [Productivity performance](../sources/source-0015.md#productivity-performance)

> "In small-to-medium economies, primate cities like Auckland often have GDP per capita 25-35% above the national average... Auckland's relatively low premium of 13% raises questions about how well its advantages of scale and density are translating into productivity gains." (Blick & Stewart, 2025, Auckland Economic Quarterly Q1-Q2, p. 1–2)

**Qualifiers:**
- Auckland's GDP per capita premium has ranged 11–13% since 2016 (and 5–9% between 2008 and 2015), so the current 13% is actually at the higher end of recent experience.
- The 25–35% comparator figure is based on GDP per capita premiums for primate cities as of 2021–22.
- Auckland also shows a 9% labour productivity premium (GDP per hour worked), suggesting part of the gap to the 25–35% benchmark reflects population/participation rather than pure productivity.
