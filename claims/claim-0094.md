# claim-0094

New Zealand fell from 11th to 18th in the Global Talent Competitiveness Index since 2015; in 2024, net emigration of New Zealand citizens reached approximately 44,000 — the largest net loss in 25 years — compounding the fertility-driven workforce contraction.

**Source:** [Lala, Spoonley & Gluckman (2026)](../sources/source-0021.md) — [Section 3: Growing reliance on immigration](../sources/source-0021.md#section-3-immigration)

> "New Zealand fell from 11th to 18th in the Global Talent Competitiveness Index (GTCI) since 2015... 2024 saw the largest net migration loss of New Zealand citizens in 25 years, totalling approximately 44,000 people." (Lala, Spoonley & Gluckman, 2026, Section 3)

**Qualifiers:**
- GTCI ranking decline is across all six pillars (enable, attract, grow, retain, vocational/technical, generalist adaptive); NZ fell below the high-income group average.
- The New Zealand Productivity Commission (2022) concluded that immigration is neither the cause nor the solution to NZ's productivity challenge — workforce growth must be supported by capital and infrastructure investment (see claim-0154, primary citation).
- International medical graduates made up 43% of the NZ specialist workforce and migrants 40% of aged-care nurses — indicating structural dependency, not just marginal reliance.
