# claim-0040

Bogotá's TransMilenio bus rapid transit system (launched 2000) reduced average travel times by 15 minutes, with larger reductions for households in poorer parts of the city; combined with neighbourhood upgrading, the program reached 930,000 people and is estimated to have raised house values by up to 11%.

**Source:** [World Bank (2009)](../sources/source-0008.md) — [Bogotá: TransMilenio BRT travel time outcomes](../sources/source-0008.md#bogota-transmilenio)

> "Since 2000 a public-private bus rapid transit system, the TransMilenio, has improved citywide accessibility. Travel times have fallen by an average of 15 minutes, with larger reductions for households in poorer parts of the city... The program is believed to have contributed increases of up to 11 percent in house values." (World Bank, 2009, Chapter 7, p. 223)

**Qualifiers:**
- The travel time reduction is an average across the system; the distribution of gains (larger for poor households) is directionally described but not quantified with precision.
- The "up to 11%" house value increase is an estimated contribution of the combined upgrading program, not of TransMilenio alone; attribution is partial.
- Bogotá represents a relatively high-income developing city with strong political leadership over this period (Mayors Mockus and Peñalosa); results may not transfer to cities with weaker institutions.
- BRT is noted as costing approximately $10 million per km to install, compared to $30 million for light rail and $100+ million for metro — the cost-effectiveness framing is relevant for developing-country contexts.

**Related:** Positive case for: [claim-0038](claim-0038.md) (connective infrastructure enables targeted inclusion). See also: [claim-0026](claim-0026.md) (transport authority and PT satisfaction).
