# claim-0058

Doubling the distance-weighted population of other cities within 300km is associated with 1–2% higher city productivity; port cities show an additional 2–4% productivity premium.

**Source:** [Ahrend et al. (2015)](../sources/source-0012.md) — [Section 4: Inter-city proximity](../sources/source-0012.md#section-4-proximity)

> "Proximity effects matter: doubling the distance-weighted population of other cities within 300km is associated with 1–2% higher city productivity... Port cities exhibit 2–4% higher productivity than comparable cities without ports." (Ahrend et al., 2015, SERC DP178, p. 20–21)

**Qualifiers:**
- The 300km radius captures inter-city knowledge and supply-chain spillovers but the threshold is empirically determined and may vary by country and transport network quality.
- Port city premium may partly reflect trade access and specialisation rather than pure agglomeration.
- Capital city status showed no significant independent productivity premium after controlling for size and industry mix.
