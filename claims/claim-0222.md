# claim-0222

Functional urban areas are better suited than administrative boundaries to capture agglomeration economies and the full extent of a city's labour market, because national administrative definitions rarely reflect the functional and economic extent of cities and bias international comparisons through the modifiable areal unit problem.

**Source:** [Dijkstra et al. (2019)](../sources/source-0040.md) — [Section 1: Introduction](../sources/source-0040.md#section-1-introduction)

> "National definitions of cities are rarely consistent across countries and they rely on administrative or legal boundaries that do not necessarily reflect the functional and economic extent of cities. … FUAs are better suited than administrative areas to capture agglomeration economies and they encompass the full extent of the city's labour market." (Dijkstra et al., 2019, p. 4)

**Qualifiers:**
- This is a methodological design argument rather than an empirical estimate; the size mismatch between administrative and functional boundaries varies by country.
- The practical implication for cross-country city comparisons (including Auckland benchmarking) is that statistics computed on administrative boundaries are not comparable to those computed on FUAs — boundary definitions must be checked before comparing productivity premiums across cities.
