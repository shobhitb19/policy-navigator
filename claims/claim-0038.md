# claim-0038

Urbanisation policy must be sequenced to the stage of urbanisation: incipient areas need spatially blind institutions (land rights, basic services); intermediate areas also need connective transport infrastructure; advanced areas also need spatially targeted interventions. Targeted slum interventions consistently fail when applied before functioning land markets and connective infrastructure are in place.

**Source:** [World Bank (2009)](../sources/source-0008.md) — [Table 7.1: Three-dimensional urbanisation policy framework](../sources/source-0008.md#table-7-1-framework)

> "For areas of incipient urbanization, the policy challenge is one-dimensional: build density with spatially blind institutions. For areas of intermediate urbanization, it is two-dimensional: build density and reduce economic distance with spatially connective infrastructure. And for areas of advanced urbanization, it is three-dimensional: build density, overcome distance, and address the economic and social divisions... targeted policies to integrate slums cannot come before the application of geographically blind and connective policies." (World Bank, 2009, Chapter 7, pp. 200, 213)

**Qualifiers:**
- This is a normative design framework synthesised from historical case studies (UK, Korea, Singapore, South Africa, Bogotá); the WDR does not present a systematic causal test of sequencing effects.
- The three stages (incipient <25%, intermediate ~50%, advanced >75% urban share) are illustrative thresholds, not precise tipping points.
- Singapore is acknowledged as an exception that implemented all three simultaneously, due to exceptional state capacity and being a city-state; most countries cannot replicate this.
- The framework applies to developing countries primarily; OECD countries mostly face 3D challenges.

**Related:** Supported by negative case: [claim-0039](claim-0039.md) (South Africa housing on periphery without transport failed). Positive case: Bogotá — [claim-0040](claim-0040.md) (BRT enabled neighbourhood upgrading). See also: [claim-0024](claim-0024.md) (governance bodies reduce fragmentation penalty).
