# claim-0237

Auckland's GDP per capita is around $8,000 above the New Zealand average with among the highest regional growth over the last decade, yet remains below the OECD average and below most internationally comparable cities — although that international gap has been shrinking.

**Source:** [MartinJenkins (2022)](../sources/source-0046.md) — [Executive summary](../sources/source-0046.md#executive-summary)

> "Auckland's GDP has grown more strongly than New Zealand's over the last decade. GDP per capita is relatively high and is around $8,000 higher than the New Zealand average. GDP per capita growth has been one of the highest of all regions over the last decade. Auckland's GDP per capita is below the OECD average and most comparable cities internationally but the gap has been shrinking." (Chen, Knuckey & Jones, 2022, p. 6)

**Qualifiers:**
- The international comparator set is twelve city-regions: Adelaide, Brisbane, Copenhagen, Dublin, Helsinki, Melbourne, Portland, Stockholm, Santiago, Sydney, Toronto, Vancouver.
- Note the nuance against other sources: the domestic premium is real and growing strongly, and the *international* gap was assessed as shrinking (as of 2022) — whereas Treasury (2017) assessed the *domestic* premium as shrinking. Different comparators and periods; the sources are complementary rather than contradictory.
- Commissioned report, publicly released; insights are the authors', not government policy.
