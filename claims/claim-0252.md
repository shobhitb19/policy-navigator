# claim-0252

Auckland's city centre generates about 8% of New Zealand's GDP and hosts 5.3% of all New Zealand jobs in fewer than five square kilometres — a national role matching Helsinki's in Finland and Dublin's in Ireland — including roughly one in four of all New Zealand finance jobs and nearly one in six professional and technical jobs.

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland & TBoC (2026)](../sources/source-0048.md) — [Focus area 3: city centre assets](../sources/source-0048.md#city-centre-assets)

> "It generates about 8% of New Zealand's GDP, an outsized national contribution for its size." (p. 32)
>
> "It hosts over 5% of all New Zealand jobs in fewer than five square kilometres, its role matching Helsinki's in Finland and Dublin's in Ireland. Such concentration means its performance has a significant effect on national outcomes." (p. 32)
>
> "New Zealand's finance sector and related professional economy especially gravitate to Auckland's centre. It hosts roughly one in four of all New Zealand finance jobs, a higher national share than most, as well as nearly one in six of all professional and technical jobs." (p. 32)

**Qualifiers:**
- Measured values from the spectrum chart (p. 33): national jobs role 5.3%; national financial jobs hub 25%. Sources are Infometrics, Stats NZ and the Auckland Economic Monitor, with TBoC analysis for peer comparisons.
- On this measure Auckland's centre is behind Dublin, Copenhagen and Edinburgh but the report presents the row as one of Auckland's stronger positions.
- The 8% national GDP share supersedes the 2025 edition's NZ$33bn figure (claim-0087, ~21% of Auckland output) as the framing statistic; the two are consistent — the 2026 report separately states the centre generates "more than 20% of regional GDP."
- Roughly five square kilometres for over 5% of national employment is the single sharpest statement in the New Zealand evidence base of how much national output rests on a very small area of land, and therefore of how much national performance is exposed to that area's planning, transport and amenity settings.
