# claim-0247

Measured against its population share, Auckland punches above its weight on most gateway functions — 2.12× for corporate headquarters, 2.1× for air passengers, 1.61× for international students and 1.58× for venture-funded firms — but only 1.00× for international visitor spend and just +8 percentage points on high-skill concentration.

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland & TBoC (2026)](../sources/source-0048.md) — [Focus area 1: gateway assets](../sources/source-0048.md#gateway-assets)

> "The 1.0× 'proportional' line means Auckland's share matches its population share exactly. Above 1.0× means Auckland punches above its weight: for example 1.61× for students means it attracts about 61% more international students than its size would predict." (The Business of Cities & Committee for Auckland, 2026, p. 21, "Auckland's Global Gateway attributes: spectrum of performance")

**Qualifiers:**
- Comparator cities differ by row and include Amsterdam (3.88× students, 3.32× funded firms), Dublin, Dubai, Stockholm, Helsinki, Copenhagen, Oslo, Lisbon and Santiago. Auckland is never the strongest performer on any row.
- The pattern is diagnostic: Auckland concentrates the *inputs* of a gateway economy — firms, students, arrivals, capital-raising companies — at roughly twice its population weight, but converts them into skilled labour and visitor spend at close to population weight. Concentration is not the constraint; conversion is.
- The high-skill premium is expressed in percentage points of the highly-skilled population share, not as a multiple, so it is not directly comparable with the other rows.
