# claim-0026

Metropolitan areas with a dedicated transport authority have public transport satisfaction rates approximately 14 percentage points higher than those without.

**Source:** [OECD (2015)](../sources/source-0006.md) — [Chapter 2: Transport authority and public transport satisfaction](../sources/source-0006.md#chapter-2-transport)

> "In metropolitan areas with a dedicated transport authority, satisfaction with public transport is around 14 percentage points higher than in those without." (OECD, 2015, Chapter 2, p. 62; citing OECD Metro Governance Survey)

**Qualifiers:**
- Based on the OECD Metro Governance Survey; self-reported satisfaction is a subjective outcome and may reflect expectations as well as service quality.
- "Dedicated transport authority" varies in scope — some have planning powers only, others control operations and fares.
- Satisfaction is not the same as usage or mode share; the relationship to actual transport outcomes (ridership, emissions, accessibility) is not quantified here.
- Survey coverage and representativeness across OECD metropolitan areas is not detailed in the cited passage.

**Related:** See also: [claim-0017](claim-0017.md) (Auckland congestion costs), [claim-0018](claim-0018.md) (Auckland PT fares globally third highest). Supports the case for: [levers/transport-investment](../levers/transport-investment.md).
