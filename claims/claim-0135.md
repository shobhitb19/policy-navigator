# claim-0135

The Green Party Response argues that the Government Policy Statement (GPS) on Land Transport 2024-2027 — which the Government's own response relies on as a guiding mechanism for new capital investment — allocates significantly more funding to new state highway capital expenditure than to any other activity type, including renewals and maintenance, which the Green Party states is at odds with Recommendations 1, 2, 7, and 13 of the National Infrastructure Plan.

**Source:** [NZ Government (2026)](../sources/source-0026.md) — [Green Party Response](../sources/source-0026.md#green-party-response)

> "We remain concerned the Government in its response is referring to the Government Policy Statement (GPS) on Land Transport as guiding new capital investment. The GPS on Land Transport 2024-2027 sets out levels of funding for different types of projects and sets aside significantly more funding for new capital expenditure for state highways than any other type of activity, including renewals and maintenance. This is at odds with recommendations 1, 2, 7 and 13." (Green Party Response, p.4)

**Qualifiers:**
- This is a dissenting/critical position from an opposition party included within the Government Response document, not a Government claim or commitment — it should not be read as the Government's own assessment.
- The Green Party separately notes, as a partial counterpoint, that "the Government response notes that they are spending a larger portion of rail spending on asset maintenance and renewals," while calling for the same prioritisation to be extended to road funding.
- The Green Party states it otherwise supports all 16 recommendations of the Plan "in full," so this critique is specifically about the adequacy of one implementation mechanism (the GPS), not disagreement with the recommendations themselves.
