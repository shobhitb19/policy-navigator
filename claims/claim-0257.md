# claim-0257

The population living along Auckland's better-connected public transport corridors has grown much more slowly than in comparable cities, and Auckland remains the most congested city in Oceania and in the bottom 5% worldwide on traffic congestion outcomes.

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland & TBoC (2026)](../sources/source-0048.md) — [Focus area 3: gaps and headwinds](../sources/source-0048.md#city-centre-gaps)

> "Growth has not yet spread across the network. Compared to other cities, the population living along better connected public transport corridors has grown much slower. A key litmus test of the City Rail Link is how well it leads to growth around new and existing rail catchments." (p. 34)
>
> "More is required to relieve a highly congested city. Auckland remains regularly rated the most congested in Oceania, in the bottom 5% worldwide on traffic congestion outcomes, with the burden spread more evenly across the whole city than most. This suggests that there is no quick fix, and that policy settings around demand management alongside wider transport investment will be required." (p. 34)

**Qualifiers:**
- Corridor population growth from TBoC research based on Infometrics, Kummu et al. and the JRC Global Human Settlement Layer; congestion from the TomTom Traffic Index 2026.
- This is the transport-and-land-use coordination failure stated directly: Auckland has built rapid transit capacity without concentrating population growth around it. Housing intensification and transit investment have been sequenced independently rather than jointly (see claim-0259 on where new housing has actually gone).
- The observation that congestion is "spread more evenly across the whole city than most" is diagnostic of dispersed origins and destinations rather than a radial commute problem, which limits what centre-focused transit investment alone can achieve.
- Congestion indices measure travel-time delay relative to free flow; they penalise dense cities with high transit use less than the raw ranking suggests, so a bottom-5% placement for a city of Auckland's size is a strong signal.
- The 2030 "signal to watch" is Auckland exiting the world's 100 most congested cities.
