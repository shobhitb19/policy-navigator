# claim-0077

Sydney and Melbourne are approximately three times the size of Auckland; applying New Zealand's agglomeration elasticity, this size gap alone could explain approximately one-third of the 20% productivity gap between New Zealand and Australia.

**Source:** [MHuD (2024)](../sources/source-0018.md) — [Section B: Agglomeration economies](../sources/source-0018.md#section-b-agglomeration)

> "Given Australia's major metros of Sydney and Melbourne are about three times the size of Auckland, then the lack of urban agglomeration might explain about a third of the 20% productivity gap with Australia." (MHuD, 2024, para. 31)

**Qualifiers:**
- This is an illustrative back-of-envelope calculation — it applies the NZ elasticity (0.04–0.05) to a population ratio (≈3×) and does not model the counterfactual path for NZ.
- "About a third" implies agglomeration accounts for ~6–7pp of the 20pp NZ-Australia gap; the remaining ~13–14pp must reflect other structural differences.
- Does not imply that growing Auckland to Sydney's size would deliver this gain — the elasticity is estimated from existing variation.
