# claim-0022

Doubling city population is associated with a 2–5% increase in GDP per worker, based on OECD analysis of functional urban areas.

**Sources:** [OECD (2015)](../sources/source-0006.md) — [Chapter 2 synopsis](../sources/source-0006.md#chapter-2-synopsis); [Ahrend et al. (2015)](../sources/source-0012.md) — [Section 4: Agglomeration elasticity results](../sources/source-0012.md#section-4-agglomeration-results)

> "There is a large body of evidence showing that GDP per worker increases by around 2-5% each time city size doubles." (OECD, 2015, Chapter 2, p. 36; citing Ahrend et al., 2014)

**Qualifiers:**
- The 2–5% range in the OECD report summarises Ahrend et al. (2014); the published vsummarises multiple studies; individual estimates vary by country, time persion of that research (SERC Discussion Paper 178,d, and method.
- OECD cites Ahrend et al. (2015 = source-0012) reports a pooled elasticity of 3.8% across five OECD countries (range: 1.6% UK to 6.3% USA) — see [claim-0055](claim-0055.md).
- OECD cites Ahrend et al. (2014) as the primary underlying study; that study is specific to OECD metropolitan areas.
- This estimate overlaps with but is somewhat below the Donovan et al. (2021) meta-analysis median of 4.6% ([claim-0001](claim-0001.md)), consistent with OECD-specific estimates being at the lower end of the global distribution.
- Fragmented governance reduces realisation of this potential — see [claim-0023](claim-0023.md).

**Related:** Consistent with: [claim-0001](claim-0001.md) (global meta-analysis, 2.7–6.4%, median 4.6%). Mechanism reduced by: [claim-0023](claim-0023.md) (fragmentation). See also: [claim-0011](claim-0011.md) (metropolitan-scope estimates are lower than national-scope).
