# claim-0173

Differences in cities' initial industry mix explain only about 10% of the differences in cross-city employment growth rates between 1976 and 2013; most of the variation is attributable to differential local performance within industries rather than which industries a city happened to specialise in.

**Source:** [Coleman, Maré & Zheng (2019)](../sources/source-0029.md) — [Section 3.3: Esteban-Marquillas decomposition — initial industry mix vs growth](../sources/source-0029.md#section-3-3-esteban-marquillas)

> Applying the Esteban-Marquillas (1972) shift-share decomposition, the authors find that "differences in the initial mix of industries do not explain much of the differences in cross-city growth rates, only about 10%." (Coleman, Maré & Zheng, 2019, Section 3.3)

**Qualifiers:**
- This decomposes growth into national-growth, industry-mix, and local-competitiveness/allocative components; the finding pertains specifically to the industry-mix component.
- This does not contradict claims 0168-0169 (sectoral shifts favouring Auckland) — Auckland's gains came disproportionately from out-performing its expected share within sectors, not merely from having "the right" industries to begin with.
