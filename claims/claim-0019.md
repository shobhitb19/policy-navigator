# claim-0019

Auckland ranked lowest of all New Zealand cities on quality of life in the 2018 Nielsen Quality of Life Survey, and did not penetrate the global top 10 most liveable cities in the two years before the Covid-19 pandemic; the primary drivers cited were housing unaffordability, inadequate infrastructure, and traffic congestion.

**Source:** [Gluckman et al. (2022)](../sources/source-0005.md) — [chapter 2 (scenario 6)](../sources/source-0005.md#chapter-2-transport)

> "In pre-Covid years, Auckland failed to rank highly in global indices in terms of liveability. In the two years preceding the pandemic, Auckland did not penetrate the top 10 most liveable cities list in the EIU's Global Liveability Index. The Nielsen 2018 Quality of Life Survey ranked Auckland lowest of all New Zealand cities on quality of life among residents. Not surprisingly, the low ranking was attributed to issues such as housing unaffordability, lack of satisfactory infrastructure and amenities, and traffic congestion." (Gluckman et al., 2022, Chapter 2, Scenario 6)

**Qualifiers:**
- Data are from pre-pandemic period; liveability rankings may have changed since, partly because some competing cities fell further.
- The Nielsen (2018) survey is a resident perception survey, not an objective measure of outcomes.
- Liveability deficits are uneven within Auckland: residents of North and Central Auckland rate their areas more positively than South and West Auckland residents (Allpress & Reid, 2020, cited in Koi Tū 2022).
- Māori and Pacific Peoples were less positive about their quality of life than Auckland as a whole.
