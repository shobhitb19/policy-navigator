# claim-0245

Auckland concentrates 73% of New Zealand's international passenger movements — a clear gateway role, but a more dispersed one than in Ireland, Finland, Denmark or Chile, where 80%+ of international traffic funnels through the main city.

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland & TBoC (2026)](../sources/source-0048.md) — [Focus area 1: gateway assets](../sources/source-0048.md#gateway-assets)

> "Auckland concentrates 73% of New Zealand's international passenger movements. This is a clear gateway role, albeit distributed more than in the capital cities of Ireland, Finland, Denmark or Chile, where geography and airport systems funnel 80%+ of international traffic through the main city." (The Business of Cities & Committee for Auckland, 2026, p. 18)

**Qualifiers:**
- Auckland Airport is also New Zealand's third largest cargo port by value, handling $26.6bn of exports and imports combined in 2024.
- New Zealand's dispersed international air access reflects tourism geography — Christchurch and Queenstown carry substantial direct international traffic — rather than a deficiency in Auckland's airport as such.
- On a population-adjusted basis Auckland's air gateway role is 2.1× proportional, ahead of Helsinki and Santiago but behind Dubai and Dublin.
- The report notes Auckland Airport's passenger experience slipped outside the global top 50 in 2026, with a NZ$5bn+ investment programme running to 2032.
