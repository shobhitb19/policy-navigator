# claim-0141

New Zealand's GDP growth over 2000-2020 kept pace with or exceeded other OECD comparator countries, but this was achieved by adding more workers (both local and migrant) to the labour force and by working longer hours, rather than by raising output per hour worked.

**Source:** [NZ Productivity Commission (2022)](../sources/source-0027.md) — [Overview: Immigration is neither the cause of, nor solution to, New Zealand's productivity woes](../sources/source-0027.md#overview-immigration-productivity)

> "New Zealand's GDP growth has kept pace with other countries in recent years, but it has done so by working harder, rather than working smarter. GDP growth has relied on adding more people to the labour force, and by those workers (both locals and migrants) working longer hours compared with other OECD countries." (NZPC, 2022, Overview, p.4)

**Qualifiers:**
- The report attributes weak productivity growth broadly to most developed economies' sluggish post-GFC productivity performance, not specifically to New Zealand's immigration settings.
- The Commission explicitly distinguishes GDP growth (which depends on labour force size and hours worked) from productivity growth (output per hour), arguing the latter is what matters most for living standards and wellbeing.
- This finding is consistent with overseas studies the Commission cites, which mostly find small positive effects of immigration on average labour productivity levels.
