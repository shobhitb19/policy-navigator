# claim-0150

Although immigration was a significant motivation behind the signing of Te Tiriti o Waitangi and continues to have disproportionate impacts on Māori, New Zealand's immigration policies and institutions have largely ignored Te Tiriti, and the Commission recommends good-faith Crown-Māori engagement on immigration policy beginning with the proposed Government Policy Statement process.

**Source:** [NZ Productivity Commission (2022)](../sources/source-0027.md) — [Chapter 6, Findings 27-28 and Recommendation 21: Te Tiriti o Waitangi and immigration policy](../sources/source-0027.md#chapter-6-te-tiriti)

> "Immigration was a significant motivation behind the signing of Te Tiriti and continues to be relevant to the relationship between the Crown and Māori... Yet immigration policies and institutions have largely ignored Te Tiriti, and they are increasingly out of step with the evolution in the Crown–Māori relationship... Finding 27: Te Tiriti o Waitangi (Te Tiriti) is fundamentally connected with immigration. Immigration policy and institutions should reflect Te Tiriti... Recommendation 21: The Government should work with Māori on how to reflect Te Tiriti o Waitangi in immigration policy and institutions. This engagement should be in good faith and allow Māori to define their own interests." (NZPC, 2022, Part 6, p.79-80)

**Qualifiers:**
- The Commission explicitly states it "has neither the mana nor the expertise to make definitive recommendations on how best to reflect Te Tiriti in immigration policy and institutions," framing its recommendations as a starting point rather than a prescribed outcome.
- The Commission emphasises Te Tiriti is a partnership: engagement is "just as much about Māori inviting the Crown to their table as it is the Crown inviting Māori to its table," and Māori may have legitimate hesitations about engaging given past experience.
- Open questions remain on how to coordinate the local and regional character of Māori engagement with the national structure of the immigration system and the international dimensions of immigration policy; the Commission notes a dedicated engagement body (analogous to Ngā Toki Whakarururanga in trade policy) as one option, without recommending it definitively.
