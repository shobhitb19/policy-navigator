# claim-0046

Mexico City metro expansion reduced informality by 4 percentage points near new stations. For every dollar invested in metro construction, average real incomes rose by 20% relative to a counterfactual of a perfectly efficient labour market — capturing large welfare gains from connecting workers to better job matches in a city with spatial misallocation.

**Source:** Zarate (2020), cited in [Lall et al. (2021)](../sources/source-0009.md) — [Box 3.6: Mexico City metro — informality and real income effects](../sources/source-0009.md#box-3-6-mexico-city)

> "Informality drops 4pp near new stations... $1 construction → 20% real income increase relative to a perfectly efficient economy." (Zarate, 2020, cited in Lall et al., 2021, Box 3.6)

**Qualifiers:**
- The 20% real income figure is a welfare gain relative to a counterfactual where labour markets function perfectly (zero spatial misallocation), not relative to no intervention.
- Mexico City is an upper-middle-income, very large (>20m population) developing city; returns to transit investment may differ in smaller or lower-income cities.
- The estimate is from a structural model, not a reduced-form identification; assumptions about labour market structure and commuting costs matter for the magnitude.

**Related:** Consistent with: [claim-0040](claim-0040.md). Supports: [claim-0034](claim-0034.md). See also: [claim-0033](claim-0033.md).
