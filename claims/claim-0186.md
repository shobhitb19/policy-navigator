# claim-0186

In the Henderson (1974)/Duranton (2008) urban migration equilibrium model, people migrate to cities as long as the private agglomeration benefits of city size exceed its congestion costs; city size settles where the marginal agglomeration benefit of additional population equals the marginal congestion cost.

**Source:** [Donovan (2019)](../sources/source-0031.md) — [Henderson (1974) / Duranton (2008) agglomeration-vs-congestion migration equilibrium](../sources/source-0031.md#slide-henderson-duranton-model)

> Drawing on Henderson (1974) and Duranton (2008): "People migrate to cities as long as (private) agglomeration benefits > congestion costs." Migration equilibrium is reached where the marginal agglomeration benefit of additional population equals the marginal congestion cost. (Donovan, 2019, slide deck)

**Qualifiers:**
- This is a conceptual equilibrium model, not a New Zealand-specific empirical estimate; it provides the theoretical scaffolding for the QL/QB empirical work in source-0030.
- The equilibrium is defined in terms of private benefits and costs; it does not account for externalities not captured in individual migration decisions.
