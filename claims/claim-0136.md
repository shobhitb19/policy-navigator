# claim-0136

The New Zealand Productivity Commission defines "absorptive capacity" as a broad concept covering physical infrastructure, land supply and housing infrastructure, core public health and education services, and broader community infrastructure, which is not a fixed constraint in the long run but limits how quickly a country can successfully accommodate population growth in the short run.

**Source:** [NZ Productivity Commission (2022)](../sources/source-0027.md) — [Overview: Managing absorptive capacity alongside migrant inflows](../sources/source-0027.md#overview-absorptive-capacity-gps)

> "The Commission sees absorptive capacity as a broad concept, covering physical infrastructure (like transport, communications), land supply and housing infrastructure, core public health and education services, and broader community infrastructure. Absorptive capacity in the longer run is not a fixed constraint." (NZPC, 2022, Overview, p.6-9)

**Qualifiers:**
- The report distinguishes short-run pressure (where capacity is effectively fixed and population growth can outpace it) from long-run capacity (which can be expanded with sufficient time and investment).
- Restricting immigration can provide temporary relief from absorptive-capacity pressure but does not address the root causes, which include underinvestment in infrastructure.
- Pressures on absorptive capacity were present in New Zealand long before the pre-Covid migration surge, indicating the constraint is structural rather than solely immigration-driven.
