# claim-0216

The small size of New Zealand's domestic market and its distance from large international markets create high upfront costs and risks of entering export markets, which partly explains why New Zealand has relatively few large, established exporting firms and a low overall level of exports to GDP.

**Source:** [NZPC (2021)](../sources/source-0039.md) — [Overview: headline findings](../sources/source-0039.md#overview-key-findings)

> "Part of the explanation for New Zealand's weak productivity performance and underperforming frontier firms is the small size of its domestic market and its distant location from large international markets. These factors create high upfront costs and risks of expanding into overseas markets. … Together with New Zealand's remote location, the high fixed costs of exporting partly explain why New Zealand has relatively few large, established and successful exporting firms, and a low overall level of exports to GDP." (NZPC, 2021, pp. 2, 25, citing Boulhol et al., 2008; Conway, 2018; McCann, 2009)

**Qualifiers:**
- Because the domestic market is small, New Zealand firms must begin exporting while still small by international standards, making expansion more difficult, expensive and risky — particularly for firms transporting physical commodities over long distances.
- The Commission notes these risks appear lower for firms in "weightless" industries such as software, health technology and creative industries.
- The Commission explicitly rejects geographic determinism ("geography is not destiny"): other remote or small SAEs have overcome similar constraints, and New Zealand has examples of firms at or near the global frontier (Fisher & Paykel Healthcare, Xero).
