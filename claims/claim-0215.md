# claim-0215

New Zealand's exports stand at 28% of GDP — much lower than the average of 59% across comparator small advanced economies (excluding hub economies such as Hong Kong, Singapore and Ireland) — and this share has remained at roughly the same level as in the early 1980s.

**Source:** [NZPC (2021)](../sources/source-0039.md) — [Chapter 2 / Box 2.1: New Zealand's international performance](../sources/source-0039.md#chapter-2-box-2-1-international-performance)

> "At 28% of GDP, New Zealand's export share of GDP is much lower than the average of 59% across the SAE group (even after stripping out the outliers of hub economies like Hong Kong, Singapore and Ireland). … New Zealand's export share of GDP has not changed meaningfully over the past few decades, and it remains at the same level as in the early 1980s." (NZPC, 2021, Box 2.1, p. 26)

**Qualifiers:**
- The flat export share contrasts with most other advanced economies, where export shares rose, particularly in the 15 years before the global financial crisis.
- The Commission attributes the low export rate partly to the risks facing firms launching into exporting to distant markets from a small domestic base (see claim-0216 for the mechanism).
- Box 2.1 also records low participation in global value chains on both forward and backward measures, and outward direct investment of 8% of GDP against an SAE average of 84% (Skilling, 2020).
