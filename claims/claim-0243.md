# claim-0243

Domestic tradability — the extent to which firms trade their output over geographic distance — is positively linked to productivity convergence toward the national frontier: New Zealand firms that operate across larger markets have greater potential to learn from firms at the national productivity frontier.

**Source:** [Zheng (2016)](../sources/source-0047.md) — [Abstract](../sources/source-0047.md#abstract)

> "Results show a positive link between domestic tradability and productivity convergence to the national frontier, suggesting that firms that operate across larger markets have greater potential to learn from firms at the national productivity frontier within the New Zealand economy." (Zheng, 2016, p. iii)

**Qualifiers:**
- The author describes this analysis as tentative and exploratory.
- The mechanism parallels the international trade-diffusion channel at domestic scale: market reach substitutes partially for physical proximity as a conduit for learning from the frontier.
- Policy relevance: transport and connectivity investments that enlarge firms' effective domestic markets may accelerate diffusion from the national frontier — an additional channel beyond labour-market deepening.
