# claim-0025

Metropolitan areas with a governance body experienced 0.28 percentage points higher annual population growth over 2000–2010 and declining urban sprawl over 2000–2006; those without a governance body experienced increasing sprawl over the same period.

**Source:** [OECD (2015)](../sources/source-0006.md) — [Chapter 2: Governance bodies, sprawl, and population growth](../sources/source-0006.md#chapter-2-governance-bodies)

> "Metropolitan areas with a governance body had around 0.28 percentage points higher annual population growth rates over 2000-10... [and] metropolitan areas without a governance body have become more sprawled over 2000-06, while those with a governance body have become less sprawled." (OECD, 2015, Chapter 2, p. 59)

**Qualifiers:**
- Cross-sectional and longitudinal comparison; causality is uncertain — governance bodies may form in faster-growing areas rather than causing growth.
- Sprawl is measured by land use intensity (population-weighted density change); different metrics may yield different results.
- The population growth differential (0.28pp/year) is modest but compounds over time.
- The finding conflates governance body presence with the specific policies those bodies implement.

**Related:** Supports: [claim-0024](claim-0024.md) (governance bodies reduce fragmentation penalty). See also: [claim-0028](claim-0028.md) (compact cities lower transport emissions), [claim-0199](claim-0199.md) (doubling density: −17% unit cost of local public services).
