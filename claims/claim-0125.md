# claim-0125

Modelling for Auckland suggests time-of-use road charging could cut excess congestion delay by around 35% and deliver equivalent road network performance with roughly 20% less new capital investment than otherwise required.

**Source:** [Te Waihanga (2026)](../sources/source-0025.md) — [Fixing land transport funding: time-of-use charging](../sources/source-0025.md#land-transport-funding)

> "Previous modelling for Auckland suggests time-of-use charging could cut excess delay by around 35% and deliver equivalent network performance with roughly 20% less new capital investment." (Te Waihanga, 2026, Section 3.3, "Implement time-of-use charging and fleetwide road user charges")

**Qualifiers:**
- Described as "previous modelling," i.e. this is a secondary citation of earlier modelling work rather than new analysis presented in the 2026 Plan.
- Framed as a hedged projection ("suggests"), not a guaranteed outcome.
- The Plan separately notes Auckland commuters lose 66 hours a year stuck in traffic, with social and economic congestion costs estimated to reach $2.6 billion by 2026 — context for why time-of-use charging is prioritised, but a distinct figure from the 35%/20% modelling result.
- Current road pricing tools (fuel excise duty, road user charges) are set nationally and do not reflect the localised nature of congestion, which the Plan presents as the rationale for moving to time-of-use and fleetwide road user charges.
