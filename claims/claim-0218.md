# claim-0218

Because a small economy has only a limited number of areas that can reach critical mass and sustain world-class competitive performance, finite government innovation resources should be deliberately focused on a small number of high-potential areas rather than spread thinly in "sub-therapeutic doses" — the practice of most successful small advanced economies, but not of New Zealand.

**Source:** [NZPC (2021)](../sources/source-0039.md) — [Chapter 7: Focused innovation policy](../sources/source-0039.md#chapter-7-focused-innovation-policy)

> "A small economy has only a limited number of areas that can get to critical mass and support sustained world-class competitive performance. As a complement to broad-based innovation policies (which benefit all firms), finite government resources also need to be deliberately focused on a small number of high-potential areas rather than being thinly spread in what David Skilling terms 'sub-therapeutic doses'." (NZPC, 2021, p. 5; Chapter 7)

**Qualifiers:**
- This is the Commission's policy judgement, informed by case studies of small-advanced-economy practice (section 7.4 finds most SAEs employ focused innovation policies), not an econometric finding.
- Focused policy is presented as a complement to, not a replacement for, broad-based innovation policy.
- Focus areas should reflect existing and emerging strengths, may not align with standard industry classifications, and should be chosen collaboratively with industry and stakeholders rather than top-down; they need materiality of investment, long-horizon funding certainty, and rigorous evaluation with willingness to exit failures.
- New Zealand's contrasting history is "small-scale, sector-focused initiatives that often fade away without any clear idea of what they have achieved" (p. 5).
