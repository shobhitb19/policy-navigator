# claim-0043 (RETIRED)

**This claim has been retired.** It bundled five distinct outcomes into one claim (violating one-claim-one-idea practice) and was sourced second-hand via Box 1.4 of Lall et al. (2021), which compressed — and in the case of pollution, inverted the sign of — the primary source's findings. Replaced by atomic claims sourced directly from the primary paper: [claim-0195](claim-0195.md) (wages), [claim-0196](claim-0196.md) (patents), [claim-0197](claim-0197.md) (energy), [claim-0198](claim-0198.md) (pollution — **corrected: density increases, not decreases, local pollution concentration**), and [claim-0199](claim-0199.md) (public service costs, corrected to −17% from −14.4%). See [source-0032](../sources/source-0032.md) for the primary source.

Original (retired) statement: doubling urban density is associated with: +4% wages, +19% patent rates, −7% energy consumption, −8% air pollution, and −14.4% unit cost of local public services — a meta-synthesis spanning productivity, innovation, sustainability, and fiscal efficiency.

**Source:** Ahlfeldt and Pietrostefani (2019), cited in [Lall et al. (2021)](../sources/source-0009.md) — [Box 1.4: Density effects meta-synthesis](../sources/source-0009.md#density-meta-effects)

> "Doubling density is associated with 4 percent higher wages, 19 percent more patents, 7 percent lower energy consumption, 8 percent less pollution and 14.4 percent lower unit costs of local public services." (Ahlfeldt and Pietrostefani, 2019, as cited in Lall et al., 2021, Box 1.4)

**Evidence base:** [evidence-0002](../evidence/evidence-0002.md)

**Qualifiers:**
- These are meta-analytic averages across the empirical literature, primarily drawing on high-income OECD cities; estimates for low-income cities will differ (see claim-0044 for GHG divergence).
- "Density" here refers to population per unit area; the estimates do not distinguish between horizontal compactness and vertical height.
- The 19% patent figure reflects both learning spillover and sorting of innovative workers into dense cities.
- Primary source: Ahlfeldt, G. M., and E. Pietrostefani. 2019. "The Economic Effects of Density: A Synthesis." CEPR Discussion Paper 13440.

**Related:** Reinforces: [claim-0001](claim-0001.md), [claim-0022](claim-0022.md). Provides mechanism behind: [claim-0028](claim-0028.md), [claim-0036](claim-0036.md).
