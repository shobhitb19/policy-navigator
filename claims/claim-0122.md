# claim-0122

New Zealand has more than $330 billion worth of existing infrastructure assets, most of which was built after 1950, and the first big wave of post-war infrastructure is now reaching the end of its usable life.

**Source:** [Te Waihanga (2026)](../sources/source-0025.md) — [Maintenance and renewals as the priority for the decade ahead](../sources/source-0025.md#maintenance-and-renewals-funding-gap)

> "Previous generations have left us with more than $330 billion worth of infrastructure, most of which was built after 1950. With the first big wave of post-war buildings and networks now reaching the end of their usable lives, we need to invest wisely to ensure we're handing over rebuilt and well-maintained assets, not burdens, to future generations of New Zealanders." (Te Waihanga, 2026, Section 4.2, "Prioritise adequate maintenance and renewals")

**Qualifiers:**
- The $330 billion figure is the estimated financial value of New Zealand's infrastructure stock; the Plan's Figure 30 shows this asset value series back to 1875 in inflation-adjusted 2025 NZD.
- The Plan frames poor asset condition data as compounding the problem: "many agencies have inaccurate or incomplete data," making it difficult to verify how much of current spending is reaching maintenance and renewals versus new infrastructure.
