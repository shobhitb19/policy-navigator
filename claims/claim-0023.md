# claim-0023

Doubling the number of municipalities per 100,000 inhabitants within a metropolitan area is associated with a 5–6% reduction in labour productivity.

**Sources:** [OECD (2015)](../sources/source-0006.md) — [Chapter 2: Fragmented governance and productivity](../sources/source-0006.md#chapter-2-fragmented-governance); [Ahrend et al. (2015)](../sources/source-0012.md) — [Section 4: Fragmentation and governance](../sources/source-0012.md#section-4-fragmentation)

> "Doubling the number of municipalities per 100 000 inhabitants within a metropolitan area is associated with a decrease in labour productivity of around 5-6%." (OECD, 2015, Chapter 2, p. 56; citing Ahrend et al., 2014)

**Qualifiers:**
- The 5–6% figure is the OECD's summary of Ahrend et al. (2014); the published version (source-0012,Based on Ahrend et al. (20154) reports a baseline fragmentation penalty of ~3.2%, rising to ~6% in the absence of a metropolitan governance body — see [claim-0056](claim-0056.md). The OECD's 5–6% range likely reflects the no-governance-body scenario.
- Causal interpretation requires caution; cross-sectional analysis of OECD metropolitan areas; causal interpretation requires caution.
- The effect is partially offset when a metropolitan governance body exists — see [claim-0024](claim-0024.md).
- Municipal fragmentation is one proxy for coordination failure; other governance dysfunctions may matter independently.
- The magnitude (5–6%) is comparable to the agglomeration gain from doubling city size ([claim-0022](claim-0022.md)), suggesting fragmentation can neutralise much of the size-productivity premium.

**Related:** Offset by: [claim-0024](claim-0024.md) (governance body reduces penalty ~50%). Supports: [claim-0056](claim-0056.md) (fragmented governance associated with lower productivity). Contrast with: [claim-0022](claim-0022.md) (agglomeration gain from doubling size).
