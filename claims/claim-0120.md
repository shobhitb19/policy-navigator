# claim-0120

Consenting costs New Zealand infrastructure providers around $1.3 billion per year, with a typical project spending 5.5% of its total budget seeking a resource consent — rising to around 16% for smaller projects worth less than $200,000.

**Source:** [Te Waihanga (2026)](../sources/source-0025.md) — [Consenting costs and regulatory complexity](../sources/source-0025.md#consenting-costs)

> "Infrastructure providers spend around $1.3 billion each year on consenting. International comparisons indicate New Zealand may be near the upper end for regulatory approval costs. A typical infrastructure project requires a firm to spend, on average, 5.5% of their total budget seeking a resource consent. For smaller projects worth less than $200,000, the figure is more like 16%." (Te Waihanga, 2026, Section 6.1)

**Qualifiers:**
- Sourced from "The cost of consenting infrastructure projects in New Zealand," Sapere, commissioned by the New Zealand Infrastructure Commission (2022) — this is a secondary citation within the Plan, not Te Waihanga's own primary analysis.
- The Plan also reports that the time required to gain a resource consent increased by 150% over the 2010–2014 to 2015–2019 period, and that the cost of consenting as a share of project budget rose by 70% between 2014 and 2022 — these are separate trend statistics, not part of the $1.3 billion annual figure itself.
- "International comparisons indicate New Zealand may be near the upper end for regulatory approval costs" is hedged, not stated as a definitive ranking.
