# claim-0249

Auckland's highly-skilled population premium over the rest of New Zealand is only 8 percentage points, behind Dublin, Lisbon, Stockholm and Oslo — and the report attributes to this thin premium slower productivity gains from job-switching, fewer pathways for New Zealanders into higher-value roles, and large knowledge-intensive firms forming less readily.

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland & TBoC (2026)](../sources/source-0048.md) — [Focus area 1: gaps and headwinds](../sources/source-0048.md#gateway-gaps)

> "Auckland is not as much of a high-skill gateway as most. Its high-skill concentration sits behind Dublin, Lisbon, Stockholm and Oslo, with less of a 'gateway premium' than most peer regions. The likely costs are slower productivity gains from job-switching and specialist depth, fewer pathways for New Zealanders into higher-value roles, and large knowledge-intensive firms forming less readily." (The Business of Cities & Committee for Auckland, 2026, p. 22; premium figure +8.0% from p. 21)

**Qualifiers:**
- Auckland figures from the Auckland Economic Monitor (Infometrics); peers from Nordregio/Eurostat LFS, SOLAS/CSO LFS and OECD Job Creation 2024. Definitions of "highly skilled" vary, so the comparison is indicative.
- The stated costs are the report's interpretation, not an estimated causal effect. They are, however, exactly the mechanisms the agglomeration literature identifies — matching and learning in thick skilled labour markets (claims 0053–0056, 0227).
- The 2030 "signal to watch" set for this focus area targets a 10% premium by 2030, up from 8% today.
- A thin skill premium is the labour-market counterpart of the thin productivity premium recorded in the 2025 edition (claim-0086: ~15% vs 30–40% for peer capitals).
