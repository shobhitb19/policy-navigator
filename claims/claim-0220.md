# claim-0220

In comparator European small advanced economies more labour is employed in the top labour-productivity decile of firms than in any other decile, whereas in New Zealand employment peaks in the 5th decile, and a smaller proportion of capital is allocated to the top two multi-factor-productivity deciles than in the European economies.

**Source:** [NZPC (2021)](../sources/source-0039.md) — [Chapter 3: Allocation of resources across productivity deciles](../sources/source-0039.md#chapter-3-resource-allocation)

> "While the allocation of labour is very similar in New Zealand and the European SAEs [by MFP decile], a higher proportion of capital is allocated to the top two deciles in the European SAEs, and a smaller proportion to the lower deciles, than in New Zealand. … In the European SAEs, more labour is employed in the top labour-productivity decile than in any other. In New Zealand, this happens in the 5th decile. This contrast in the allocation of labour is a concern, as is the much lower average capital intensity compared with the European firms." (NZPC, 2021, pp. 46, 55, Figures 3.8–3.9)

**Qualifiers:**
- Because much of the cross-country difference in aggregate productivity can arise from the ability to reallocate resources to more productive uses (Bartelsman et al., 2013), this pattern is evidence of resource misallocation, not only of weak firm-level performance.
- The allocation of labour looks similar between New Zealand and the SAEs when firms are ranked by MFP but different when ranked by labour productivity; capital-intensive firms sit in upper labour-productivity deciles by construction, so the two rankings differ.
- The Commission suggests the pattern "may reflect sluggish reallocation of resources away from some low-productivity firms" in New Zealand.
