# claim-0139

Since 2012, an increasing share of people approved within the Essential Skills and Work to Residence temporary visa categories have been at lower ANZSCO skill levels (levels 4 and 5), while approvals at higher skill levels (1 and 2) have remained stable in number, making New Zealand's skilled temporary visa categories relatively less skilled over this period.

**Source:** [NZ Productivity Commission (2022)](../sources/source-0027.md) — [Chapter 2, Finding 1: Brain drain offset by skill-selective immigration](../sources/source-0027.md#chapter-2-brain-drain-skills)

> "Since 2012, an increasing number of people with lower-skill levels have been approved within both the Essential Skills and the Work to Residence visa categories. This means the skilled temporary visa categories have become relatively less skilled over this period." (NZPC, 2022, Part 2, Finding 1, p.19, citing Figure 2.7, MBIE, 2022b)

**Qualifiers:**
- Skill levels are measured using the Australian and New Zealand Standard Classification of Occupations (ANZSCO), a five-level scale; levels 4-5 are "low skilled" and 1-2 are "high skilled."
- The shift is documented for the largest skilled temporary visa category, the Essential Skills visa, via approvals data 2012-2021 (Figure 2.7); higher-skill-level approvals "remained very stable in number" rather than declining outright — it is the lower-skill increase that shifted the composition.
- This compositional shift occurred before the Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV) reforms, which replaced ANZSCO skill assessment with a wage-threshold proxy for skill.
