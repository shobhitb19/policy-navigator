# claim-0163

In forecast error variance decomposition, the immigration shock explains between 10% and 30% of the variation in real GDP and the unemployment rate over a three-year horizon, reaching around 30% for both by that horizon, while its contribution to participation rate variation rises sharply to 25% after three years.

**Source:** [Javed, Power & Richardson (2026)](../sources/source-0028.md) — [Section 4.2: Forecast error variance decomposition](../sources/source-0028.md#section-4-2-fevd)

> "The immigration shock explains between 10% and 30% of the variation in real GDP levels and the unemployment rate over a three year horizon. For the participation rate, the contribution of the immigration shock increases sharply, reaching 25% after three years." (Javed, Power & Richardson, 2026, Section 4.2, pp. 16-18)

**Qualifiers:**
- The abstract characterises the GDP and unemployment contribution at the three-year horizon specifically as "a major contributor" at around 30%, the upper end of the 10-30% range reported in the body text.
- The immigration shock explains over 40% of the variation in the immigration rate itself, which the authors treat as a validation check that the shock is well identified rather than a substantive economic finding.
