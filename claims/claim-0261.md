# claim-0261

In a weakening development cycle Auckland has recorded one of the ten most positive housing-delivery trendlines of any major city, with measured affordability improving ahead of many close peers and the medium-density share of new consents shifting faster than in Vancouver or Brisbane.

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland & TBoC (2026)](../sources/source-0048.md) — [Focus area 5: assets and advantages](../sources/source-0048.md#homes-assets)

> "Housing supply trend has been more favourable than almost anywhere. In a weakening development cycle, Auckland has seen one of the top 10 most positive trendlines of any major city in housing delivery, a tangible dividend from 10 years of planning. Measured affordability is improving and is now ahead of many close peers, while the perceived priority placed on housing affordability has also slightly eased." (p. 44)
>
> "The supply mix is reorienting. The medium density share of new consents has shifted faster than in Vancouver or Brisbane, and the build to rent pipeline now sits alongside the more established end of international markets." (p. 44)

**Qualifiers:**
- Delivery trendline from TBoC research on local house-completion figures; affordability from Demographia International Housing Affordability 2026. "Trendline" measures direction and persistence of delivery, not level — a city can improve fastest from a low base.
- This continues the 2025 finding (claim-0088) that Auckland's delivery moved into the top third globally after the Unitary Plan, and extends it: the gain has held through a downturn, which is the harder test.
- The report immediately qualifies the achievement on location grounds (claims 0258–0259): the units are being built, but not where jobs and transit are. Volume success and location failure are simultaneous.
- "Measured affordability improving" refers to the house-price-to-income multiple. Auckland remains severely unaffordable by international standards; the improvement is in the rate, not the level.
- The 2030 "signal to watch" is that the multiple keeps improving relative to peer cities.
