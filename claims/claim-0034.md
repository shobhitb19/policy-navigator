# claim-0034

City connectivity — measured by road density and market potential within 10km (the De la Roca–Puga index) — is the key driver of formal sector employment growth in developing cities; formal employment more than doubles with each doubling of market potential within 10km of the average resident.

**Source:** [Collier, Jones & Spijkerman (2018)](../sources/source-0007.md) — [Section 3: Connectivity](../sources/source-0007.md#section-3-connectivity)

> "Formal sector employment more than doubles with each doubling of market potential within 10km of the average person in the city. By contrast, the coefficient on log population density is not significant." (Collier et al., 2018, Section 3, p. 22–23)

**Qualifiers:**
- The De la Roca–Puga (DRP) market potential measure captures population accessible within 10km (with a distance discount); it reflects economic density rather than administrative density.
- Population density per se is not significant in the employment regressions — only the DRP connectivity measure. This implies it is the quality and distribution of density that matters, not aggregate density.
- The finding applies to formal sector employment; impacts on informal enterprises are not measured.
- The panel is limited to cities where two Enterprise Survey waves were conducted 2006–2012; sample size is smaller than for the cross-sectional regressions.
- Road density is also significant in some specifications; the result is robust across both connectivity proxies.
