# claim-0255

Auckland's city centre generates more than 20% of regional GDP, but a smaller share of Aucklanders live within a convenient commute of it than in most comparable cities; the City Rail Link is expected to bring the commutable catchment share much closer to peer benchmarks.

**Source:** [Committee for Auckland & TBoC (2026)](../sources/source-0048.md) — [Focus area 3: gaps and headwinds](../sources/source-0048.md#city-centre-gaps)

> "Bridging the gap. The centre generates more than 20% of regional GDP despite a smaller share of Aucklanders than most cities living within a convenient commute of it. City Rail Link is set to bring the commutable catchment share much closer to peer benchmarks, and in doing so, will widen access to higher value jobs, deepen the labour pool and spread the centre's economic benefits more evenly." (p. 34)

**Qualifiers:**
- "Convenient commute" is operationalised elsewhere in the report as the number of residents able to reach a central reference point within 30 minutes door-to-door by public transport (TravelTime and TBoC, 2025); Auckland's peer field on that row includes Tel Aviv and Dublin.
- The 2030 "signal to watch" is more than 1 million residents within 45 minutes door-to-door of the centre by public transport.
- This is the report's clearest statement of the accessibility mechanism: the productivity value of a job concentration depends on how many workers can reach it, not on how many jobs it contains. A high-output centre with a thin catchment realises less agglomeration benefit than its job count suggests.
- The CRL expectation is a forecast, not a measured outcome, and the report elsewhere cautions that "connecting bus performance and spatial development choices will also be critical" (p. 34).
