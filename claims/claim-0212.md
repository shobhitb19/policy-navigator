# claim-0212

Over 2003–16, New Zealand's non-frontier firms benefited from technology diffusion only from the domestic frontier — unlike non-frontier firms in comparator European small advanced economies, they did not benefit from diffusion from firms at the international small-advanced-economy frontier, a result likely reflecting New Zealand's distant location and the difficulty of diffusing tacit, non-codified technologies over distance.

**Source:** [NZPC (2021)](../sources/source-0039.md) — [Chapter 3: Diffusion comes only from the domestic frontier](../sources/source-0039.md#chapter-3-diffusion-domestic-frontier)

> "Non-frontier firms in the European countries in the Commission's small-advanced-economy study benefited from technology diffusion from firms at the small-advanced-economies frontier (albeit to a lesser extent than from their national frontier firms). This did not happen in New Zealand over 2003–16. This result likely reflects New Zealand's distant location, and that diffusion of tacit and non-codified technologies is difficult over distance." (NZPC, 2021, F3.4, p. 45, citing Zheng et al., 2021)

**Qualifiers:**
- The finding is from econometric modelling on CompNet microdata comparing New Zealand with Belgium, Denmark, Finland, the Netherlands and Sweden; the "distant location" explanation is the Commission's interpretation ("likely reflects"), not a directly identified causal channel.
- In all six countries, non-frontier firms benefited significantly from diffusion from their own domestic frontier; the missing channel in New Zealand is specifically the international one.
- The Commission draws two implications: New Zealand's frontier firms performing well matters more than elsewhere (they are the only diffusion source), and improving international connections in trade, investment, people and knowledge is critical.
