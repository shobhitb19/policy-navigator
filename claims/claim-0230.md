# claim-0230

Rapid transit corridors can invert Auckland's car/public-transport accessibility gap: North Shore suburbs served by the Northern Busway (Takapuna, Albany) are forecast to have *more* jobs accessible by public transport than by car, while suburbs without rapid transit at comparable distances have public transport access several times lower than car access.

**Source:** [Auckland Transport (2021)](../sources/source-0043.md) — [Access to Employment (pp. 34–35)](../sources/source-0043.md#access-to-employment)

> "Jobs accessible from Takapuna: by car: 200,000 to 300,000; by PT: 300,000 to 400,000 … Jobs accessible from Albany: by car: 100,000 to 200,000; by PT: 200,000 to 300,000 … the relative advantage of PT access over the car on the North Shore, in those areas served by the Northern Busway … areas of relatively high public transport access extending outwards along the rapid transit network to the south, north and west." (Auckland Transport, 2021, p. 35)

**Qualifiers:**
- 2031 forecasts within a reasonable travel time (30 min car / 45 min PT); the PT advantage on the busway corridor partly reflects congestion suppressing car access rather than PT speed alone.
- The inverse pattern — car access several times higher than PT — holds in the Inner South and in suburbs like Māngere and Botany Downs that lack rapid transit connections.
- Policy implication: the accessibility payoff of rapid transit is demonstrated within Auckland's own network, not only in international comparisons.
