# claim-0166

A robustness check splitting immigrants by age finds that an "older" (30-44) immigrant shock produces largely insignificant responses across the model except for labour productivity, which rises significantly around one year after the shock.

**Source:** [Javed, Power & Richardson (2026)](../sources/source-0028.md) — [Section 5: Robustness check — younger (15-29) vs older (30-44) immigrant age groups](../sources/source-0028.md#section-5-robustness-age-groups)

> "In response to an 'older' immigration shock, the IRFs of all variables are largely insignificant except for labour productivity. The median response of labour productivity remains positive throughout the horizon and shows a statistically significant increase (at the 68% credible interval) at around the 1 year horizon." (Javed, Power & Richardson, 2026, Section 5.1, pp. 19-21)

**Qualifiers:**
- This is the only robustness-check result the paper highlights as a significant productivity effect, and it contrasts with the muted, insignificant labour productivity response found in the (all-ages) baseline model.
- The Section 6 conclusion summarises this as one of two key robustness findings: "labour productivity is likely to increase with an increase in the inflow of relatively older immigrants."
