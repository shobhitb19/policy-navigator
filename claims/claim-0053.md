# claim-0053

In New Zealand, service sectors benefit most from agglomeration: property and business services have an elasticity of 0.16 and finance and insurance 0.087 (within local industry estimates at mean density), while manufacturing and mining elasticities are lower or statistically insignificant.

**Source:** [Maré & Graham (2009)](../sources/source-0011.md) — [Section 5: Industry-level elasticities](../sources/source-0011.md#section-5-industry-elasticities)

> "Property and business services, finance and insurance, and communication services show the highest elasticities (0.16, 0.13, and 0.12 respectively at mean density)... Mining and quarrying is insignificant." (Maré & Graham, 2009, p. 18–19)

**Qualifiers:**
- Elasticities evaluated at mean density; the relationship is non-linear (decreasing returns), so high-density firms face lower marginal agglomeration benefits.
- Finance & insurance figure of 0.087 is the within-local-industry estimate; the 0.13 figure is at-mean-density from a different specification.
- Consistent with global evidence that knowledge-intensive and financial services benefit most from density.
