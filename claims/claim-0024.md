# claim-0024

A metropolitan governance body reduces the fragmentation-productivity penalty by approximately 50%.

**Sources:** [OECD (2015)](../sources/source-0006.md) — [Chapter 2: Fragmented governance and productivity](../sources/source-0006.md#chapter-2-fragmented-governance); [Ahrend et al. (2015)](../sources/source-0012.md) — [Section 4: Fragmentation and governance](../sources/source-0012.md#section-4-fragmentation)

> "The existence of a metropolitan governance body reduces the negative impact of fragmentation on labour productivity by around half." (OECD, 2015, Chapter 2, p. 56; citing Ahrend et al., 2014)

**Qualifiers:**
- The primary source is Ahrend et al. (2014/2015); source-0012 is the published version (SERC DP 178). The same finding appears in source-0012 as "a metro governance body roughly halves [the fragmentation penalty]" — see [claim-0056](claim-0056.md).
- CBased on Ahrend et al. (2014) cross-sectional analysis of OECD metropolitan areas; causal interpretation requires caution.
- "Metropolitan governance body" is defined broadly; the analysis does not distinguish between governance body types or their fiscal and planning powers.
- The 50% reduction applies to the fragmentation penalty ([claim-0023](claim-0023.md)), not to absolute productivity; a fragmented area with a governance body still underperforms an equally-sized unfragmented area.
- What specific governance functions drive the gain (land use coordination, transport, fiscal pooling) is not identified in this finding.

**Related:** Quantifies the partial offset to: [claim-0023](claim-0023.md). See also: [claim-0025](claim-0025.md) (governance bodies also associated with lower sprawl).
