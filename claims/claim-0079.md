# claim-0079

New Zealand's capital stock per worker is approximately 50% of OECD comparator levels — a structural deficit that directly suppresses labour productivity relative to peers.

**Source:** [Winter et al. (2025)](../sources/source-0019.md) — [Section 3: Capital intensity and innovation in NZ](../sources/source-0019.md#section-3-capital-innovation-nz)

> "New Zealand's capital stock per worker sits at approximately 50% of OECD comparator levels." (Winter et al., 2025, AN 25/12, Figure 3, citing Penn World Tables)

**Qualifiers:**
- Derived from Penn World Tables; the exact peer comparator set is not specified in the summary but is implied to be small advanced OECD economies.
- The 50% figure is approximate (read from Figure 3); it refers to capital stock per worker, not capital services per worker.
- This is a structural, not cyclical, deficit: it persists through normal business cycle variation.
- Capital intensity gap compounds with the technology diffusion gap (see claim-0080).
