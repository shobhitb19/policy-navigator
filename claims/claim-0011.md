# claim-0011

Agglomeration elasticity estimates are lower when measured at metropolitan spatial scope (populations 0.2m–1m) than at national scope: the median metropolitan estimate is 3.3% (90% CI: 1.5–5.0%) compared to 6.4% (90% CI: 4.6–8.2%) at national scope.

**Source:** [Donovan et al. (2021)](../sources/source-0004.md) — [section 5](../sources/source-0004.md#section-5)

> "For the metro and national levels of spatial scope, for example, we find median elasticities of 3.3% and 6.4% with 90% probability intervals of 1.5–5.0% and 4.6–8.2%, respectively." (Donovan et al., 2021, section 5, p. 30)

**Evidence base:** [evidence-0001](../evidence/evidence-0001.md)

**Qualifiers:**
- Spatial scope is defined by the average population of the spatial units used to measure agglomeration, not the city's own population.
- The lower metropolitan estimate likely reflects that metropolitan-scale measures capture a smaller spatial footprint of spillovers than national potential measures.
- These are distributions of meta-estimates for the preferred specification (wages, density, published, panel, micro-data, IV, worker/firm effects, own skills, sectoral/occupational composition, social capital).
- International-scope estimates have a median of 11.6% (90% CI: 9.6–13.7%), driven partly by trade and cross-border spillover effects; these are not directly relevant for city-level policy analysis.
