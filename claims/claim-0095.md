# claim-0095

The cost of New Zealand Superannuation is forecast to rise from 5.1% to 8% of GDP by 2065; the worker-to-retiree ratio is falling from 7 (1960s) to 4 (2025) to an anticipated 2 by 2065.

**Source:** [Lala, Spoonley & Gluckman (2026)](../sources/source-0021.md) — [Section 4: An ageing population](../sources/source-0021.md#section-4-ageing)

> "The New Zealand government spends approximately five times as much on the average person aged 65+ as on the average person under 65... Cost of NZ Superannuation forecast to rise from 5.1% to 8% of GDP by 2065. In the 1960s, there were seven New Zealand workers aged 15–64 for every person over 65; by 2025, four; by 2065, the number is anticipated to reach two." (Lala, Spoonley & Gluckman, 2026, Section 4, citing Treasury 2025)

**Qualifiers:**
- Fiscal projections are from Treasury (2025) Long-Term Fiscal Statement; assumptions include current policy settings.
- By 2048, 22% of NZ's population will be over 65 (vs 17% in 2025); the "old-old" (over 85) cohort will grow particularly fast.
- Several regions (Tasman, Marlborough, Nelson, West Coast) will exceed 30% aged 65+ by 2048 — a hyper-ageing threshold.
- Labour force participation among 65–69-year-olds has already risen substantially (10% in 1993 → 49% in 2025), partially offsetting the dependency ratio increase.
