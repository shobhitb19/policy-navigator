# Lever: Land Use Planning

## What this is

Land use planning is the regulatory and statutory framework that determines where different uses (residential, commercial, industrial, open space) are permitted and at what scale. It encompasses strategic plans, district/local plans, spatial strategies, and development assessment processes.

## What problem it addresses

- Uncoordinated development that creates mismatches between housing location, employment, and infrastructure.
- Lack of a long-run framework for infrastructure investment sequencing.
- Failure to capture value from publicly funded infrastructure improvements.

## How it works (mechanisms)

- A strategic spatial plan aligns infrastructure investment with growth areas, enabling sequenced and cost-effective provision. (See [claim-0035](../claims/claim-0035.md), [claim-0025](../claims/claim-0025.md))
- Coordinating land use and transport planning allows transit investment to be complemented by density, generating both productivity and emissions benefits. (See [claim-0029](../claims/claim-0029.md), [claim-0028](../claims/claim-0028.md), [claim-0257](../claims/claim-0257.md))
- Plan-led systems reduce uncertainty for developers and communities, potentially lowering the cost of development. (See [claim-0070](../claims/claim-0070.md))

## Preconditions

- Governance authority that spans the functional metropolitan area.
- Technical capacity in planning agencies.
- Integration with transport, infrastructure, and fiscal planning (not just a land use exercise).
- Meaningful community engagement to build legitimacy.

## Risks and trade-offs

- Plan-led systems can be slow and inflexible if plan reviews are infrequent.
- Planning frameworks that reflect existing patterns (rather than aspirational futures) can entrench low density and car dependence.
- Without coordination mechanisms, plans across adjacent councils can be inconsistent.

## Key claims

- [claim-0070](../claims/claim-0070.md) Housing and land-use constraints reduce productivity through spatial misallocation of workers and firms
- [claim-0066](../claims/claim-0066.md) Restrictive land use in Auckland estimated to have reduced NZ GDP by 0.9–1.8%
- [claim-0029](../claims/claim-0029.md) Halving travel time between a large city and its hinterland raises regional productivity growth 0.2–0.4pp/yr
- [claim-0028](../claims/claim-0028.md) Per capita ground-transport CO₂ falls consistently as urban density rises
- [claim-0035](../claims/claim-0035.md) Leapfrog development drove 40%+ of built-up area growth in several major African cities
- [claim-0259](../claims/claim-0259.md) Auckland's new housing went to the centre and the urban edge, not to transit-served suburbs
