# Lever: Metropolitan Governance

## What this is

Metropolitan governance refers to formal institutional arrangements for managing the functional urban area as a coherent unit — including coordinating land use, transport, infrastructure, economic development, and (sometimes) social services across the boundaries of individual local governments.

## What problem it addresses

- Coordination failures between fragmented municipalities in a single functional economic area. (See [claim-0023](../claims/claim-0023.md), [claim-0056](../claims/claim-0056.md))
- Misaligned incentives where individual councils internalise costs but not benefits of development or transit.
- Inability to make and implement strategic spatial plans at the scale that matters economically.

## How it works (mechanisms)

- A metropolitan authority with strategic land use powers can align development patterns with infrastructure investment cycles.
- Own-source revenue (land value capture, development levies, dedicated taxes) gives the authority credibility and reduces dependence on central transfers. (See [claim-0020](../claims/claim-0020.md), [claim-0264](../claims/claim-0264.md))
- Unified transport planning eliminates modal and spatial gaps that arise when multiple agencies plan independently.

## Preconditions

- Political will from both central government and constituent local councils.
- Adequate technical capacity — institutional structure alone is insufficient.
- Accountability mechanisms to prevent capture by incumbent landowners or existing residents.

## Risks and trade-offs

- Amalgamation or centralisation can reduce local responsiveness and democratic accountability.
- Large authorities can become bureaucratically slow; subsidiarity requires careful assignment of functions.
- Creating metropolitan authorities in political environments without strong fiscal decentralisation may produce underpowered bodies.

## Key claims

- [claim-0023](../claims/claim-0023.md) Doubling municipalities per 100,000 inhabitants associated with 5–6% lower labour productivity
- [claim-0056](../claims/claim-0056.md) A metropolitan governance body roughly halves the fragmentation productivity penalty
- [claim-0025](../claims/claim-0025.md) Metros with a governance body saw declining sprawl 2000–2006; those without saw increasing sprawl
- [claim-0026](../claims/claim-0026.md) Metros with a dedicated transport authority have PT satisfaction ~14pp higher
- [claim-0264](../claims/claim-0264.md) Auckland wins where it holds the levers and loses where central government does
