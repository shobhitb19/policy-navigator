Density effects meta-synthesis: the productivity, innovation, environmental, and fiscal effects associated with a doubling of urban population density, synthesised across the empirical literature on the economic effects of density.

Ahlfeldt and Pietrostefani (2019) synthesise the empirical literature on the economic effects of density across four domains — productivity (wages), innovation (patents), environmental sustainability (energy and pollution), and fiscal efficiency (unit cost of local public services). Cited here via Lall et al. (2021), "Pancakes to Pyramids" (World Bank), Box 1.4. The synthesis finds that doubling density is associated with simultaneous gains across all four domains, indicating that well-managed density delivers compounding benefits rather than a single-dimension trade-off.

**Key results:**

- Doubling density associated with +4% higher wages (Ahlfeldt and Pietrostefani, 2019, as cited in Lall et al., 2021, Box 1.4)
- Doubling density associated with +19% more patents (innovation/learning spillovers)
- Doubling density associated with −7% lower energy consumption
- Doubling density associated with −8% less air pollution
- Doubling density associated with −14.4% lower unit cost of local public services (fiscal efficiency)

**Derived claims:** [claim-0043](../claims/claim-0043.md)

**Limitations:**

- Known to this wiki only as a secondary citation (via Lall et al., 2021); the primary paper (CEPR Discussion Paper 13440) has not itself been ingested as a source
- Estimates are meta-analytic averages drawn primarily from high-income OECD cities; effects in low-income cities are expected to differ
- Does not distinguish horizontal compactness from vertical building height as the source of density
- The patent effect likely reflects both genuine learning spillovers and the sorting of innovative workers into dense cities, and the synthesis does not decompose the two

**Source:** [source-0009](../sources/source-0009.md#density-meta-effects)
