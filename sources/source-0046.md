A 231-page evidence base on Auckland's wellbeing and prosperity, built on ~90 indicators
organised through an "Auckland Wellbeing and Prosperity Framework" adapted from the
Treasury's Living Standards Framework. Where data allows, Auckland is benchmarked against
NZ regions and twelve international city-regions (Adelaide, Brisbane, Copenhagen, Dublin,
Helsinki, Melbourne, Portland, Stockholm, Santiago, Sydney, Toronto, Vancouver). The report
deliberately trades depth for breadth; its value to this wiki is as a broad official
indicator baseline for Auckland circa 2022.

<a id="executive-summary"></a>
## Executive summary

> "Auckland has many strengths, including in knowledge and skills; income, consumption and wealth; health and economy and industrial structure; but has weaknesses in a range of areas." (p. 5)

> "Auckland's GDP has grown more strongly than New Zealand's over the last decade. GDP per capita is relatively high and is around $8,000 higher than the New Zealand average. GDP per capita growth has been one of the highest of all regions over the last decade. Auckland's median hourly earnings is currently the second highest of all regions but earnings growth has been below most other New Zealand regions. Auckland's GDP per capita is below the OECD average and most comparable cities internationally but the gap has been shrinking." (p. 6)

<a id="economy-footprint"></a>
## Economy and industrial structure: the national footprint

> "Auckland plays an important economic role for New Zealand, accounting for close to 40% of New Zealand's GDP and over a third of employment. It holds 35% of all businesses and 61% of the top 200 companies. Its economic performance has been mixed – achieving strong outcomes in terms of value add and incomes but more limited employment outcomes, particularly for some populations." (p. 5)

> "[Auckland] has 61% of the top 200 companies and is the departure point for 18% of the value of exports." (Economy and industrial structure chapter)

<a id="knowledge-innovation"></a>
## Knowledge and skills: patent intensity

> "Comparing Auckland to internationally comparable city-regions, again using the older data source, shows that PCT patent applications are low, relative to high patenting regions like Helsinki, Oregon, and Copenhagen (Figure 89). However, Auckland inventors are seeking patents at about the midpoint of the set of internationally comparable city-regions." (p. 95, OECD regional data, fractional count by inventor and priority year, 2001–2015)

Supporting detail: New Zealand's strongest patent ties are with Australia, the US and the UK;
the most prolific institutional applicants are AgResearch and the University of Auckland; and
domestic applications concentrate in agriculture and food, industrial engineering, and civil
engineering (Clarivate 2020 for IPONZ). More recent WIPO data suggest Auckland-based
businesses file a high share of New Zealand PCTs.

<a id="demographics"></a>
## Population

> "Auckland's population has grown about 1.8% a year over the previous 20 years, higher than the national rate of 1.4% a year. In the wake of the COVID-19 pandemic, Auckland's population fell for the first time in 2021. COVID-19 border restrictions are likely the key contributor to this decline." (p. 5)

> "Almost 40% of Aucklanders were born overseas, compared to 20% for the rest of New Zealand (excluding Auckland). … Auckland is a significant part of New Zealand – a third of New Zealand's residents live in Auckland." (p. 5)
