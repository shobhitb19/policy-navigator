Methodology paper describing the joint EU-OECD definition of functional urban areas (FUAs),
developed so that cities can be compared consistently across countries. National city
definitions rely on administrative or legal boundaries that rarely reflect the functional and
economic extent of cities, biasing international comparisons through the modifiable areal unit
problem. The FUA definition is people-based (population density, size, and commuting flows),
not morphology-based. This is the framework underlying OECD metropolitan statistics and the
city-size comparisons used throughout the agglomeration literature. The paper contains no
country-specific analysis of New Zealand; its value to this wiki is methodological.

<a id="section-1-introduction"></a>
## Section 1: Introduction — the boundary problem

> "National definitions of cities are rarely consistent across countries and they rely on administrative or legal boundaries that do not necessarily reflect the functional and economic extent of cities." (p. 4)

> "FUAs are better suited than administrative areas to capture agglomeration economies and they encompass the full extent of the city's labour market. It can guide national and city governments when they plan infrastructure, transportation, housing, schools, and spaces for culture and recreation." (p. 4)

The definition is deliberately people-based — population density, population size, and daily
mobility — rather than based on built-up area or morphology, because built-up area per capita
varies between cities of different sizes and development levels and grows over time, reducing
comparability.

<a id="section-2-short-definition"></a>
## Section 2: The short definition

The four-step FUA definition (p. 5):

> "1) Identify an urban centre: a set of contiguous, high density (1,500 residents per square kilometre) grid cells with a population of 50,000 in the contiguous cells; 2) Identify a city: one or more local units that have at least 50% of their residents inside an urban centre; 3) Identify a commuting zone: a set of contiguous local units that have at least 15% of their employed residents working in the city; 4) A functional urban area is the combination of the city with its commuting zone." (p. 5)

Required data: a 1 km² residential population grid, digital local-unit boundaries, and
commuting flows between local units.
