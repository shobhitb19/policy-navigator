Uses the NZ Prototype Longitudinal Business Database (1999–2006) to estimate firm-level agglomeration elasticities. Key methodological contribution: decomposing the raw density-productivity relationship into true agglomeration, compositional, and sorting components.

## Section 3: Methods

Effective employment density is measured with a distance-decay function (parameter α=1), weighting employment in surrounding areas by inverse distance. Production function approach estimates TFP as a function of effective density.

## Claims extracted

- [claim-0051](../claims/claim-0051.md) 10% higher effective density → 0.69% higher firm productivity (NZ, within local industry)
- [claim-0052](../claims/claim-0052.md) 91% of apparent NZ density-productivity elasticity reflects sorting — pooled 0.171 collapses to 0.015 with firm fixed effects
- [claim-0053](../claims/claim-0053.md) NZ service sectors (finance & insurance 0.087; property & business services 0.16) have highest agglomeration elasticities; manufacturing and mining insignificant
- [claim-0054](../claims/claim-0054.md) Agglomeration in NZ reduces capital and labour use (9–15%) but raises intermediate consumption (12%) — efficiency gains partially offset by higher input costs
