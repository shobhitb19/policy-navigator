Key addition relative to Conway & Meehan (2013): updated data through the COVID-19 period, broader treatment of wellbeing and non-market production, and expanded analysis of NZ's capital intensity deficit.

## Claims extracted

- [claim-0062](../claims/claim-0062.md) NZ GDP per hour worked has been below the OECD average for 50+ years; relative decline began with the 1973 oil shock and continued to the mid-2000s
- [claim-0063](../claims/claim-0063.md) NZ growth since the 1980s has come primarily from working more hours (labour utilisation), not from higher hourly productivity — NZ workers work longer hours than most OECD peers while producing less per hour
- [claim-0064](../claims/claim-0064.md) NZ capital stock per hour worked is roughly half that of Australia, Sweden and the US — and the gap has widened: Denmark's capital per worker was 81% above NZ's in 1970, rising to 164% above by 2019
