<a id="executive-summary"></a>
## Executive Summary

Outlines nine scenarios for Auckland's future in 2070: Socially Cohesive Region; Creativity and Culture; Quality Education; Innovative Region; Sustainable and Resilient Region; Human-focused Infrastructure/Transport/Housing; Integrated Precincts; Connected Region; Indigenously Inspired National Park City. Each scenario has an aspirational 2070 narrative and a current-state "Where is Auckland now?" section with empirical data.

<a id="chapter-1"></a>
## Chapter 1: Where is Auckland Now?

Current-state baseline data for the Auckland region as of c.2020–2021:

- Population 1.72 million (2020); projected 2 million by early 2030s (Statistics NZ, 2021)
- Auckland accounts for **37.9% of NZ GDP** and **35.5% of the national workforce** (Infometrics, 2021) → [claim-0016](../claims/claim-0016.md)
- Auckland workers are **11.1% more productive** than workers outside Auckland (Infometrics, 2021) → [claim-0015](../claims/claim-0015.md)
- Māori economy contributed ~$9.6 billion (9%) to Auckland's GDP in 2018
- NZ frontier firms less than half as productive as those in comparable small advanced economies (NZPC, 2021 — now ingested directly as [source-0039](source-0039.md); see [claim-0211](../claims/claim-0211.md))
- Auckland Supercity established 2010 through amalgamation of eight councils

<a id="chapter-2-scenarios"></a>
## Chapter 2: Nine Scenarios

Each scenario chapter contains a "Where is Auckland now?" current-state section followed by a 2070 aspirational vision. Scenarios are: Socially Cohesive Region (1); Creativity and Culture (2); Quality Education (3); Innovative Region (4); Sustainable and Resilient Region (5); Human-focused Infrastructure/Transport/Housing (6); Integrated Precincts (7); Connected Region (8); Indigenously Inspired National Park City (9).

<a id="chapter-2-transport"></a>
### Scenario 6: Human-focused Infrastructure, Transport and Housing

Current-state transport and liveability data:

- Auckland **not in EIU Global Liveability Index top 10** in 2018–2019 → [claim-0019](../claims/claim-0019.md)
- **Nielsen 2018 Quality of Life Survey**: Auckland ranked lowest of all NZ cities; attributed to housing unaffordability, transport, and amenity deficits → [claim-0019](../claims/claim-0019.md)
- Economic cost of Auckland road congestion: **~NZ$1.3 billion/year** (Leung et al., 2017) → [claim-0017](../claims/claim-0017.md)
- Auckland PT fares: **third highest in the world** (Greater Auckland, 2018); transport poverty disproportionate for Māori and Pasifika → [claim-0018](../claims/claim-0018.md)
- Transport accounts for ~44% of Auckland's carbon footprint; household emissions rose 18% between 2007 and 2018 (Statistics NZ, 2020)

<a id="chapter-3"></a>
## Chapter 3: Enabling Change — Governance, Management and Planning

Analysis of Auckland's central-local governance fragmentation:

- Central government controls most fiscal and strategic levers; Auckland Policy Office is the only dedicated interface — described as "relatively small"
- Only the mayor is elected across the full Auckland region (1 of 200+ elected officials)
- 70% of Aucklanders believe the public has little influence on Council processes (Nielsen, 2018)
- Long-term decisions (port location, second harbour crossing, light rail) unresolved for decades
- CCOs (Auckland Transport, Eke Panuku, Watercare, Auckland Unlimited) are described as "somewhat siloed" with overlapping interests and insufficient alignment
- Proposed reforms: unified planning and strategy unit; Commission for Future Generations; deliberative democracy mechanisms (citizens' juries); stronger Māori co-governance

→ [claim-0020](../claims/claim-0020.md)

<a id="chapter-4"></a>
## Chapter 4: Towards an Integrated Perspective

Synthesis recommendations:

1. System-wide alignment on a long-term vision and strategy for Auckland
2. A singular integrated planning agency sharing common data across central and local decision-makers
3. Citizen and Māori buy-in through participatory and deliberative democratic methods
4. Commission for Future Generations to enforce intergenerational perspective
5. Reconsideration of Auckland's fiscal arrangements (local sales tax, GST share, or equivalent)
