Policy analysis by Auckland Council's Chief Economist unit benchmarking Auckland's productivity performance against comparable primate cities and identifying barriers and policy levers. Combines agglomeration theory with Auckland-specific evidence on housing costs, congestion, and land use patterns.

<a id="productivity-performance"></a>
## Auckland's productivity performance

> "In the year to March 2024, Auckland's GDP was $157 billion, or 38% of the national total. That's $88,400 per person compared with the national average of $78,200 … Auckland's GDP per capita premium has ranged from 11–13% in recent years (2016–2024), similar to levels seen in the early 2000s after dipping to 5–9% between 2008 and 2015." (Blick & Stewart, 2025)

> "Auckland's GDP per capita partly reflects its younger population and higher labour force participation. GDP per hour worked, which measures labour productivity, shows Auckland had a 9% premium over the national average." (Blick & Stewart, 2025)

## Claims extracted

- [claim-0065](../claims/claim-0065.md) Auckland's GDP per capita is 13% above the national average vs. the 25–35% typical for comparable primate cities — indicating unrealised agglomeration potential
- [claim-0066](../claims/claim-0066.md) Restrictive land use in Auckland has contributed to rising house prices and reduced NZ national GDP by an estimated 0.9–1.8% by preventing workers from living where their productivity would be highest
- [claim-0067](../claims/claim-0067.md) Auckland congestion costs ~$0.7bn/year (~0.5% of Auckland's GDP); dispersed growth lengthens commutes, raises infrastructure costs, and forgoes the productivity benefits of compact urban form
- [claim-0226](../claims/claim-0226.md) Auckland generates 38% of national GDP but its GDP per hour worked premium is only 9% (year to March 2024)
