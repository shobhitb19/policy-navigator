<a id="abstract"></a>
## Abstract

Preferred model (Bayesian mixed effects, Model 5): median agglomeration elasticity **4.6%**, 90% credible interval **2.7–6.4%**. Based on 6,684 estimates from 295 studies across 54 countries, 1960–2020. → [claim-0001](../claims/claim-0001.md)

<a id="section-1"></a>
## Section 1: Introduction

Motivates the meta-analysis; notes wide dispersion in the agglomeration literature and the need for a model that accounts for unobserved heterogeneity and publication bias. → [claim-0014](../claims/claim-0014.md)

<a id="section-2"></a>
## Section 2: Methodology

Bayesian mixed effects framework. Preferred specification uses Student's t-distribution and an errors-in-outcomes correction. Discusses instruments (Bartik-type historical variables), spatial scope definitions, and outcome variable coding.

<a id="section-3"></a>
## Section 3: Exploratory Data Analysis

Descriptive statistics on the 6,684 estimates: distribution by country, sector, time period, agglomeration measure, and control set. Median raw OLS estimate is substantially higher than the preferred-model estimate.

<a id="section-4"></a>
## Section 4: Regression Results

<a id="section-4-1"></a>
### Section 4.1: Benchmark Models

Table 3, Model 5 is the preferred specification. Key moderator effects: spatial scope (metropolitan vs. national), sector (manufacturing vs. economy-wide), and control variables. → [claim-0011](../claims/claim-0011.md), [claim-0013](../claims/claim-0013.md), [claim-0014](../claims/claim-0014.md)

<a id="section-4-2-2"></a>
### Section 4.2.2: Publication Bias

Estimates from published studies are approximately 2.1% higher than unpublished studies, consistent with publication bias toward positive and significant results.

<a id="section-4-2-3"></a>
### Section 4.2.3: Time Trends

Economy-wide elasticities peaked c.1980–2000 and declined by ~1.5% by 2020. Manufacturing elasticities declined throughout the full 1960–2020 period. → [claim-0012](../claims/claim-0012.md), [claim-0013](../claims/claim-0013.md)

<a id="section-5"></a>
## Section 5: Discussion

Synthesises results; discusses heterogeneity by income level, sector, and spatial scope. Notes declining manufacturing agglomeration and possible explanations (falling freight costs, environmental regulation). → [claim-0001](../claims/claim-0001.md), [claim-0012](../claims/claim-0012.md), [claim-0013](../claims/claim-0013.md)

<a id="section-6"></a>
## Section 6: Conclusions

Recommends using metropolitan-scope estimates with appropriate controls as the most reliable basis for policy. Cautions against raw OLS estimates and single-country studies without heterogeneity controls.
