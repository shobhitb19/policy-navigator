## Box 7.3: Mumbai FSI restrictions and slum formation

Mumbai's Floor Space Index (FSI) was reduced from 4.5 to **1.33** in 1991, producing average space consumption of only **4 square metres per person** (vs 12 sq m in Shanghai, 20+ in Moscow). Result: **54% of Mumbai's 16 million residents live in slums**. Height regulations hold Mumbai's buildings to one-fifth to one-tenth of floors permitted in comparable cities. Overly restrictive land and building regulations have put upward pressure on land prices, hampering competitiveness (pp. 205–206). → [claim-0037](../claims/claim-0037.md)

<a id="box-7-6-atlanta-barcelona"></a>
## Box 7.6: Atlanta vs Barcelona — density and transport emissions

Atlanta and Barcelona had similar populations (~2.5–2.8 million) in 1990, but Atlanta's density was **6 people per hectare** versus Barcelona's **176 people per hectare**. Per capita CO₂ from transport: **400 metric tons in Atlanta, 38 tons in Barcelona** (~10x difference). Transit accessibility: **60% of Barcelona residents live within 600m of a metro station**; in Atlanta only **4% live within 800m**. Transit mode share: **30% in Barcelona, 4.5% in Atlanta** (pp. 211–212; citing Bertaud, 2004; Kenworthy, 2005). → [claim-0036](../claims/claim-0036.md)

<a id="table-7-1-framework"></a>
## Table 7.1: Three-dimensional urbanisation policy framework

The WDR 2009 proposes calibrating urbanisation policy to the stage of urbanisation (Table 7.1, p. 215):
- **Incipient** (<25% urban): 1D challenge — build density. Instrument: spatially blind institutions (land rights, basic services).
- **Intermediate** (~50% urban): 2D challenge — density + reduce distance. Add: connective transport infrastructure.
- **Advanced** (>75% urban): 3D challenge — density + distance + eliminate division. Add: spatially targeted interventions (slum upgrading, social integration).

The sequencing rule: targeted interventions fail unless preceded by functioning land markets and connective infrastructure. → [claim-0038](../claims/claim-0038.md)

<a id="south-africa-housing"></a>
## South Africa: peripheral housing without transport links

South Africa's post-1994 housing program faced a deficit of **1.5 million units** (7.4 million people in slums; 18% of households). The first wave of low-cost housing developments, placed on city peripheries, **lacked basic amenities and transport links to job centres** and failed to ameliorate intra-urban divisions. The programme succeeded only once it was redesigned to integrate public transport access alongside housing supply (p. 214). → [claim-0039](../claims/claim-0039.md)

<a id="bogota-transmilenio"></a>
## Bogotá: TransMilenio BRT travel time outcomes

Bogotá's TransMilenio bus rapid transit system (launched 2000) reduced average travel times by **15 minutes**, with larger reductions for households in poorer parts of the city. The city used combined infrastructure improvements, neighbourhood upgrading, and community centres to make urbanisation more inclusive. The neighbourhood upgrading program (Programa de Mejoramiento Integral de Barrios) reached 930,000 people and is estimated to have contributed house value increases of up to **11%** (p. 223). → [claim-0040](../claims/claim-0040.md)
