# State of the City 2025 — Benchmarking Tāmaki Makaurau Auckland

Committee for Auckland, Deloitte, & Auckland Council. (2025). *The State of the City 2025: Benchmarking Tāmaki Makaurau Auckland's international performance* (3rd ed.). Committee for Auckland.

**Full document:** [State-of-the-City-2025-final-embargoed.pdf](State-of-the-City-2025-final-embargoed.pdf)

**Superseded in part by:** [source-0048 — State of the City 2026](source-0048.md). Note that the 2026 report is not a full benchmarking edition and does not restate the ten-pillar decile scores, so the 2025 figures below remain the most recent for the pillar series.

<a id="executive-summary"></a>
## Executive summary

Now in its third annual edition, the report benchmarks Auckland against a peer group of ten comparable cities: Auckland, Austin, Brisbane, Copenhagen, Dublin, Fukuoka, Helsinki, Portland, Tel Aviv, and Vancouver. These peers were selected as cities of broadly similar size, economic profile, or strategic function.

The headline finding is Auckland's persistent and deepening **productivity gap** relative to peers. Auckland's Prosperity pillar score dropped from the 6th decile (2023) to the 4th decile (2024 and 2025) — the largest decline of any pillar across all three editions of the report. The report identifies productivity, not housing, as the central strategic challenge for the next decade.

Positive signals include Auckland's housing delivery performance (now top-third globally) and the resilience of the city centre economy.

<a id="opportunity-and-prosperity"></a>
## Opportunity and prosperity

**Productivity ranking:** Auckland ranks 99th globally and last among its ten peer cities on productivity, as measured by the Resonance Consultancy's World's Best Cities survey methodology (GDP per capita and labour productivity proxies).

**GDP premium over rest of NZ:** Auckland contributes approximately 38–40% of national GDP. However, Auckland's productivity premium over the rest of New Zealand is only approximately 15% — substantially lower than the 30–40% premium that comparable peer capital cities (Dublin, Helsinki, Tel Aviv) enjoy over their national averages.

**Labour productivity advantage over secondary cities:** Auckland's labour productivity is only 3–5% above New Zealand's secondary cities (Hamilton, Tauranga). This is unusually small for a primate city; comparable international primate cities typically show 10–20% advantages over their secondary cities.

**Median personal income:** Auckland median personal income is approximately 8% above the New Zealand national average. Austin's is approximately 25% above the US national average — indicating Auckland is not capturing typical agglomeration returns in household incomes.

**Prosperity decile trajectory:**
- 2023: 6th decile
- 2024: 4th decile
- 2025: 4th decile (no recovery)

<a id="in-focus-productivity"></a>
## In focus: Auckland's productivity gap

The report's in-focus chapter explicitly frames Auckland's productivity as "15–20% lower than comparable international cities."

**Identified causes of the productivity gap:**

1. **Inefficient land use:** Less dense urban form reduces agglomeration economies. Auckland's dispersed growth pattern means fewer workers access deep labour markets and knowledge spillovers within effective commuting distance.

2. **Small domestic market:** New Zealand's population of 5 million limits market scale and competitive intensity. Peer cities (Dublin, Helsinki, Tel Aviv) either access large common markets (EU) or serve large export markets (technology products).

3. **Low capital investment rates:** New Zealand's business investment rate is approximately 2.8% of GDP, versus approximately 3.9% for Australia. This reduces the rate at which new technology vintages are embedded in the capital stock.

4. **Lower export intensity:** Auckland's export intensity is approximately 20% of GDP; Nordic peer city economies export approximately 40% of GDP. Lower export intensity limits competitive exposure and market scale.

5. **Management capability deficits:** The report flags sub-benchmark management practice scores as a contributor, consistent with broader New Zealand productivity literature.

**City centre performance:** Despite aggregate underperformance, Auckland's city centre economy is a relative bright spot. The central city generates NZ$33 billion in GDP = approximately 21% of Auckland's total output (up from approximately 16% in 2000), growing approximately 2 percentage points per year faster than the rest of New Zealand.

<a id="innovation-and-knowledge"></a>
## Innovation and knowledge

**Foreign direct investment:** Auckland's annual share of new business attributable to FDI is more than 80% below the OECD peer average. This is a severe underperformance and limits the technology transfer and management practice diffusion that FDI typically brings.

**Technology and digital sector:** Auckland's technology/digital sector contributes approximately NZ$16.5 billion ≈ 11% of Auckland GDP.

**Global connectivity:** Auckland ranks 63rd most globally connected corporate city and is one of only six cities under 2 million people appearing in both the top 100 most corporate-connected and top 100 most important global cities lists.

<a id="place-and-connectivity"></a>
## Place and connectivity

**Housing delivery:** Auckland has substantially improved housing supply performance. The city now ranks in the top third globally for new homes completed annually per capita, exceeding the peer-city average by approximately 60%. Among the ten peer cities, only Austin delivers more new homes per head of population. This improvement follows the 2016 Unitary Plan and subsequent intensification reforms.

**Housing affordability:** Auckland improved its affordability ranking from 11th to 16th least affordable among English-speaking world cities (Demographia International Housing Affordability Survey). This represents progress, though Auckland remains severely unaffordable by international standards.

<a id="people-and-inclusion"></a>
## People and inclusion

**Māori and Pacific unemployment:** Māori and Pacific unemployment rates are rising faster than the Auckland average. NEET (not in employment, education, or training) rates are approximately 10 percentage points and approximately 7 percentage points higher respectively than for the general Auckland population.

**Income distribution:** Despite the nominal 8% income premium over national average, Auckland's income distribution reflects significant spatial and demographic concentration of the productivity gains that do exist.
