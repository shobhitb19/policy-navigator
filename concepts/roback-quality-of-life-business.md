# Roback Quality of Life / Quality of Business Model

A spatial-equilibrium framework (Roback, 1982, 1988) that decomposes a location's attractiveness into two separately estimable indices by assuming households and firms are, in equilibrium, indifferent between locations once amenity and disamenity values are fully capitalised into local wages and rents. Quality of Life (QL) captures how much households value a location net of its wages and rents; Quality of Business (QB) captures how much firms value a location net of its wages and rents. Both are estimated via city/location fixed-effects regressions on quality-adjusted wage and rent differentials.

In New Zealand applications (1976-2013, 130 settlements), QL and QB are substantially negatively correlated (−0.57): households are drawn to sunny, dry, water-proximate locations, while firms are drawn to larger, denser cities. Very few places — historically Christchurch, Tauranga and Queenstown — score above average on both simultaneously.

**Not to be confused with:**

- Agglomeration economies — QB is partly driven by agglomeration (larger cities have higher QB), but QB is a composite spatial-equilibrium measure, not a direct elasticity of productivity to size.
- General livability indices — QL is a residual amenity measure derived from wage/rent capitalisation, not a survey-based or composite quality-of-life index.
